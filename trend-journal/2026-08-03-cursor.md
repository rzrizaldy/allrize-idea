# RRP JobFile — EPA §745.86 recordkeeping vault for painting & remodeling firms — 2026-08-03

**Contributor:** Cursor (Grok)
**File:** `trend-journal/2026-08-03-cursor.md`
**Market:** United States first; Indonesia relevance: not yet established (US TSCA/RRP only)
**Motion:** B2B. Certified renovation firm owner / ops manager pays; certified renovator and crew use the field checklist and photo capture; property owner/occupant receives the required post-job packet but does not pay.
**Verdict:** Watch

## Summary

**RRP JobFile** would be a Stripe-billed, mobile-web job file that maps one pre-1978 renovation to the documents and attestations required by **40 CFR §745.86**: firm/renovator certificates, Renovate Right pamphlet receipt, containment and cleaning-verification checklist, worker on-the-job training topics, optional test-kit/paint-chip records, timestamped photos, and a one-click owner/occupant PDF delivered within 30 days—retained for three years for EPA request. The obligation is durable federal law, enforcement is active in 2025–2026 (including an explicit June 2026 settlement citing missing records), and EPA’s own sample checklist plus OMB burden estimate (4.5–7 hours per response) show real paperwork friction.

This is **Watch, not Build**. The free official Sample Renovation Recordkeeping Checklist already occupies the bare compliance job; Mecherath sells RRP documentation inside a $399/month contractor suite; QuoteIQ markets EPA RRP documentation inside a field CRM from $29.99/month; and photo-first tools (e.g., CompanyCam-class products) plus binders already serve as workarounds. Without interviews proving solo painters will pay for an RRP-only vault instead of those options, promotion to idea-grill is premature.

- **Primary rule text — 3-year retention and required records, collected 2026-08-03:** [40 CFR §745.86](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/40/745.86) requires firms to retain and make available to EPA all records necessary to demonstrate RRP compliance for **three years** after renovation completion, including (where applicable) lead-determination records, signed pamphlet acknowledgments / delivery certifications, common-area/child-occupied notification records, and documentation that a certified renovator was assigned, trained workers, directed §745.85 work practices, and performed post-renovation cleaning verification (including topics, containment elements, waste handling, and wet/dry cloth counts). Owner/occupant compliance information must be provided when the final invoice is delivered or within **30 days** of completion, whichever is earlier; EPA’s sample checklist (or a similar form) may be used.
- **Official free substitute, collected 2026-08-03:** EPA publishes a [Sample Renovation Recordkeeping Checklist](https://www.epa.gov/sites/default/files/documents/samplechecklist.pdf) and lists it among [RRP program resources](https://www.epa.gov/lead/renovation-repair-and-painting-program-resources) (page last updated March 6, 2026). The sample form’s Paperwork Reduction Act notice estimates public reporting/recordkeeping burden at **4.5 to 7 hours per response** and cites OMB Control No. 2070-0195.
- **Firm certification economics, collected 2026-08-03:** EPA’s [Firm Certification](https://www.epa.gov/lead/renovation-repair-and-painting-program-firm-certification) page (last updated May 27, 2026) states renovation firm certification/recertification is **$300 for five years**, that firms (including sole proprietorships) must be certified to work in pre-1978 housing/child-occupied facilities where EPA administers the program, and that firm responsibilities explicitly include meeting **recordkeeping requirements**. Fifteen listed states (plus one Tribe) run authorized programs instead of or in addition to EPA certification.

## Inference

- RRP recordkeeping is a **durable regulatory workflow**, not a search fad: the rule has been in force since 2010, retention is three years, and 2025–2026 EPA materials still prioritize lead-safe renovation enforcement—including settlements that explicitly cite missing records.
- The product job is **assemble → attest → deliver → retain**, not “AI explains lead paint.” Value sits in mapping field steps to §745.86 elements, forcing missing attachments before closeout, emailing the owner packet inside 30 days, and surviving an information-request letter years later.
- A plausible pricing wedge exists between (a) free PDF/binder workflows and (b) Mecherath’s $399/month suite: a focused **$39–$79/month RRP-only job file** for 1–10 person painting/remodeling firms that already have Jobber/Housecall Pro for scheduling. That wedge is **hypothesis**, not proven demand.
- QuoteIQ and photo CRMs blur the wedge: if contractors already attach photos and custom checklists to jobs at ~$30/month, a standalone RRP vault must win on **schema completeness, owner delivery, retention search, and EPA-request export**—not on another checklist UI.
- AI should only OCR certificates, suggest which uploaded photos map to checklist rows, and draft the short renovator narrative from checked items. Autogenerating false attestations would create legal and product risk; human-signed certification remains mandatory under the sample form’s penalty-of-law language.

## Assumptions

- A meaningful segment of US solo/small painting and remodeling firms still fails §745.86 retention in practice (supported by enforcement examples) **and** will pay a subscription rather than fix process with the free EPA form.
- Buyers will adopt a second tool beside their existing scheduling CRM rather than demand RRP features only as a Jobber/CompanyCam add-on.
- $39–$79/month (or ~$9–$15 per closed pre-1978 job) is credible versus expected fine/insurance/reputation risk for that segment.
- Operating an RRP documentation aid does not require being an EPA-accredited trainer or giving legal advice if the product stays in workflow/checklist territory (confirm with counsel before any “compliance guarantee” marketing).
- Indonesia is out of scope for v1; no credible Indo secondary market was established for US TSCA renovation records.

## Consulting assessment

### Trend durability

High durability, low novelty. Demand is anchored in federal TSCA/RRP obligations and ongoing OECA/regional enforcement, not a 2026 consumer trend spike (Trends values unavailable here). Durability favors a workflow product; novelty risk is low, competitive awareness among trades is high after 16 years of the rule.

### Customer and JTBD

**Motion:** B2B. **Buyer:** owner of a US EPA- or state-certified renovation firm (often a painting, window, remodeling, or GC specialty shop doing compensated work on pre-1978 housing). **User:** certified renovator completing the job file on-site; office staff assembling owner packets and responding to EPA information requests. **JTBD:** before invoicing / within 30 days, produce a complete, searchable record that proves §745.85 practices and §745.84 education for this address, deliver the required summary to owner/occupant, and retrieve it within days if EPA asks during the three-year window.

### Pain evidence

- Regulatory: mandatory three-year retention and multi-element documentation (§745.86); EPA estimates 4.5–7 hours burden per response on the sample checklist.
- Enforcement: ReArch (June 2026) and franchise/general-contractor cases in EPA’s Enforcement Alert show missing records are observed and penalized alongside work-practice failures; Fortune Painting CAFO summary alleges §745.86 retention failure with a five-figure penalty.
- Operational: firms must coordinate certificates, pamphlet receipts, training topics, containment steps, cleaning verification counts, and photo/evidence across truck/phone/office—work that free PDFs and ad-hoc photo apps do not structure end-to-end.
- Gap: pain proves **process risk**; it does not yet prove willingness to pay for a fourth SaaS after Jobber + CompanyCam + Dropbox.

### Alternatives

| Alternative | Gap vs proposed wedge |
| --- | --- |
| EPA Sample Renovation Recordkeeping Checklist (free PDF) | No mobile guided flow, photo binding, deadline nudges, multi-job search, or Stripe billing; easy to lose across three years |
| Binders / Google Drive / email | Weak schema enforcement; hard to prove completeness under information request |
| Mecherath ($399/mo suite with RRP module) | Exact documentation claim, but priced as full contractor OS—overkill for RRP-only buyers |
| QuoteIQ / Jobber / Housecall Pro + custom forms | Scheduling CRM first; §745.86 completeness and owner-packet semantics not independently verified as first-class |
| CompanyCam-class photo documentation | Strong photos; weak statutory checklist + certificate + pamphlet linkage |
| Doing nothing / incomplete files | Exposed in settlements; not a rational long-term strategy |

### Wedge

v1: **RRP-only job file for painting and interior remodel firms** doing ≤40 pre-1978 jobs/month, starting in EPA-administered states (exclude multi-state authorized-program complexity at first). Explicitly not abatement clearance labs, not HUD grant reporting OS, not full field-service CRM, not “AI certifies you compliant.”

### MVP

- Firm profile: firm certificate upload + expiry reminder; renovator certificate roster.
- Per-job wizard mapped to §745.86 / sample checklist rows; required photo slots (signs, containment, waste, cleaning verification).
- Pamphlet acknowledgment capture (upload signed form or e-sign stub) with date stamp.
- Worker OJT topic checklist per worker.
- Generate owner/occupant PDF packet + archive; calendar nudge at invoice/30-day deadline.
- Searchable three-year vault with one-click “EPA request export” ZIP.
- Stripe Billing: monthly subscription or per-closed-job credits.

### AI role

OCR/classify uploaded certificates and pamphlet receipts; suggest mapping of photos to checklist rows; draft the short renovator certification narrative **only from checked structured fields**. Must not invent containment steps, cloth counts, or test results. Durable value is schema + retention + delivery, not a chatbot wrapper.

### Pricing

**Hypothesis, not verified:** $49/month including 25 closed RRP job files, then $8/file; or $99/month unlimited with fair-use cap. Concierge validation: $79 to rebuild one firm’s last three job files into audit-ready packets in 48 hours and measure willingness to convert to subscription.

### Distribution

1. EPA-accredited RRP training providers and refresher courses (handout / affiliate after class).  
2. Paint-store and supplier counter programs (Sherwin/PPG independent dealers) in older-housing metros.  
3. SEO/content around “RRP recordkeeping checklist,” “EPA information request renovation records,” and state-authorized program differences.  
4. Partnerships with CPL insurance brokers who already sell lead-related coverage to painters (education, not coverage underwriting).

Avoid enterprise GC procurement and Home Depot-scale vendor security as v1 channels.

### Risks

- **Market:** Free official form + CRM photo stacks absorb the job; WTP may be near zero until after an inspection scare.  
- **Product:** State-authorized RRP programs (15+ states) add form and certification variance.  
- **Legal:** Any “guarantees compliance” claim is dangerous; fabricated/AI-filled attestations create liability.  
- **GTM:** Trade buyers are relationship-driven; cold SaaS CAC may exceed LTV at $49/month.  
- **Technical:** Offline/poor-signal job sites; photo storage costs; identity of who signed the renovator certification.

### Kill criteria

Stop or park the idea if any of the following hold after a short validation window:

1. **10 structured interviews** with certified firm owners yield fewer than **3** who currently lose ≥2 hours/month to RRP paperwork **and** reject both the free EPA checklist and their existing CRM as sufficient.  
2. **5 paid concierge packet rebuilds** ($79) produce fewer than **2** subscription conversions at ≥$39/month.  
3. A named incumbent ships a **transparent RRP-only SKU ≤$49/month** that covers §745.86 packet + owner delivery + three-year vault with clear adoption.  
4. Legal review concludes the product cannot be marketed without becoming a regulated training/compliance-advice service the solo team cannot support.
