## Summary

HeatLedger would be a web app for recording Wet Bulb Globe Temperature (WBGT) readings, translating the reading into the applicable University Interscholastic League (UIL) practice restrictions, timestamping staff acknowledgement, and sending practice modifications or cancellations to coaches and families. The timing is real: Texas converted its heat-stress guidelines into required standards effective August 1, 2026. The product should nevertheless be rejected in this form. Zelus already offers hardware-free WBGT, saved locations, alerts, and compliance documentation for athletic departments; Perry Weather combines on-site stations, automated logs, threshold alerts, historical exports, and public communication widgets and says it serves more than 700 Texas schools. A thin rule-specific logger is buildable, but it does not have a defensible wedge or a proven path through school procurement.

### Regulatory trigger and workflow

- On **June 10, 2026**, UIL announced approval of a proposal making its Heat Stress and Athletic Participation Guidelines required standards for all member schools effective **August 1, 2026**: [UIL Legislative Council results](https://www.uiltexas.org/press-releases/detail/uil-legislative-council-meeting-results35).
- UIL's 2026–27 rule page confirms that the prior recommendations became required standards for athletic and marching-band practices during elevated heat, effective **August 1, 2026**: [UIL 2026–27 rule changes](https://www.uiltexas.org/policy/2026-27-policy-info/rule-changes).
- UIL's operational guidance says WBGT should be checked within 15 minutes before practice and every 30 minutes during practice, with activity modified whenever the reading crosses a level. It recommends keeping the readings on file: [UIL Heat Stress and Athletic Participation Plan](https://wwwprod.uiltexas.org/health/info/heat-stress-and-athletic-participation).
- Reporting on **August 4, 2026** confirms schools are implementing the rule as fall practices begin; Muleshoe ISD described using the required pre-practice and 30-minute readings: [Plainview Herald](https://www.myplainview.com/muleshoe/sports/article/uil-imposes-new-heat-safety-measures-athletes-22372650.php).

### Alternatives and competitive overlap

- [Zelus WBGT](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/zelus-wbgt/id1605250081) markets real-time, hyperlocal WBGT without expensive hardware, saved locations, alerts, forecasts, and timestamped compliance documentation. It explicitly targets athletic departments and youth-sports organizations.
- [Perry Weather's UIL offering](https://perryweather.com/resources/2026-uil-wbgt-mandate/) provides on-site measurement, automated threshold alerts, and timestamped compliance logs. Its related FAQ describes historical exports and public-facing widgets and says the company already supports more than 700 Texas schools: [Perry Weather UIL FAQ](https://perryweather.com/resources/2026-uil-wbgt-requirement-webinar/).
- A school can also use a portable WBGT meter plus a paper form or spreadsheet. The UIL guidance permits both on-site instruments and internet-based applications, so a new SaaS would compete with hardware, apps, and manual logging rather than replace a prohibited workflow.

### Relevant GitHub evidence and founder build fit

- [Open-Meteo](https://github.com/open-meteo/open-meteo) is an open-source weather platform with hourly North American model updates, historical data, air-quality APIs, and maintained TypeScript and Python clients. Its repository had approximately 5.7k GitHub stars when collected. It demonstrates that a forecast and notification prototype is technically feasible, although commercial use requires appropriate terms and forecast data is not identical to an on-site measurement.
- The founder's [RizeBot Hermes](https://github.com/rzrizaldy/rizebot-hermes) demonstrates deterministic scheduled checks, state records, policy-driven actions, audit history, alerts on state changes, and human-review gates. Those are strong technical primitives for HeatLedger.
- Founder build fit is therefore high for logging and notifications; the weakness is market access and differentiation, not implementation.

## Inference

- The regulation creates durable recurring behavior during every hot-weather practice, not merely a one-day news spike.
- The record-and-communicate workflow is real, but established vendors already perform it and add either hardware accuracy or broader weather coverage.
- A web-only product could be cheaper and easier to deploy than an installed station, but Zelus already occupies the hardware-free position.
- School districts may prefer an incumbent with on-site measurement, support, and procurement references because mistakes concern student safety and liability.
- Google Trends' geographic concentration aligns with a Texas beachhead, but it does not show willingness to pay or sustained growth.
- Indonesia expansion is not justified yet because no comparable nationwide school-athletics WBGT mandate or buyer channel was verified.

## Assumptions

- A meaningful number of UIL schools still lack a digital logging system after three years of recommended WBGT use.
- Coaches or athletic trainers would trust an internet-derived reading where UIL permits it.
- A district can purchase a $19–$49 monthly web subscription through Stripe without a lengthy vendor-security and procurement process.
- Existing tools have material usability or pricing gaps that users will articulate.
- Parent communication is painful enough to justify a separate product rather than existing school, band, or team-messaging software.

## Consulting assessment

### 1. Trend durability

The durable driver is the new required standard, effective August 1, 2026. Search interest is geographically present, but the Trends time series failed and therefore provides no verified growth claim.

### 2. Customer and JTBD

- **Motion:** B2B.
- **Who pays:** school district athletics office or individual UIL member school.
- **Who uses:** athletic trainer, coach, band director, and designated WBGT reader.
- **Who receives output:** administrators, athletes, and parents.
- **Possible acquisition intermediaries:** regional education service centers, Texas athletic-trainer associations, coaching associations, and marching-band director networks.
- **JTBD:** prove that every outdoor practice was monitored on schedule, immediately apply the correct restriction, and preserve an audit trail if a decision is questioned.

### 3. Pain evidence

UIL requires repeated readings and practice changes. Schools are implementing the workflow now, and the need to keep records creates administrative work. However, this run found no direct complaint showing that current meters, spreadsheets, Zelus, or Perry Weather are failing badly enough to support a new purchase.

### 4. Alternatives

- Portable WBGT instrument plus paper or spreadsheet log.
- Zelus for hardware-free WBGT, alerts, and compliance documentation.
- Perry Weather for installed sensors, automated logging, forecasts, alerts, and public widgets.
- Existing school/team communication platforms for parent messages.

The proposed functionality is already covered in whole or in composable parts.

### 5. Wedge

Proposed wedge: a Texas-UIL-specific, browser-based practice session that prompts readings every 30 minutes, applies the correct Class 2 or Class 3 rules, captures acknowledgement, and sends a decision notice.

The wedge is understandable but not defensible: Zelus owns hardware-free compliance documentation, and Perry Weather owns automated school-grade monitoring and communication.

### 6. MVP

If validation were attempted without product development, use a clickable prototype plus a manual service for one school:

1. select county/classification and activity;
2. enter the device reading;
3. display the deterministic UIL action;
4. timestamp the reading and staff acknowledgement;
5. export a PDF/CSV practice log; and
6. send one pre-approved modification notice.

Do not calculate a safety-critical “measured” WBGT from a generic forecast and present it as equivalent to an on-site instrument.

### 7. AI role

No AI is needed. Threshold mapping, timers, required actions, and audit logs should be deterministic. AI-generated safety advice would add risk and turn the concept into an unnecessary wrapper.

### 8. Pricing

Unverified hypotheses:

- $99 one-time season setup and rule-template pack.
- $29/month per campus for logging, exports, and five staff seats.
- $79/month district tier for multiple campuses and centralized audit export.

Stripe supports the purchase path technically, but school purchasing-card policy and vendor onboarding remain unverified.

### 9. Distribution

The first credible path would be one Texas athletic-trainer or band-director association and direct outreach to small UIL schools still using paper logs. The validation offer should be a paid season setup, not a free waitlist.

### 10. Risks

- **Market:** many schools may already have a meter and adequate logging process.
- **Competition:** Zelus and Perry Weather directly cover the wedge.
- **Accuracy:** forecast-derived WBGT may diverge from conditions at the field.
- **Safety/legal:** a wrong threshold, missed update, or misleading alert can affect student health and school liability.
- **Procurement:** districts can require security review, insurance, accessibility, purchase orders, and approved-vendor onboarding.
- **Product:** parent communication duplicates existing school/team systems.
- **Seasonality:** usage and urgency concentrate around hot-weather practices.
- **GTM:** Texas schools are a finite, reference-driven market with incumbent relationships.

### 11. Kill criteria

Reject permanently if any of the following is true after 20 interviews with qualified Texas athletic trainers or band directors:

- fewer than 5 still use a painful manual log;
- fewer than 3 will prepay at least $99 for a season;
- no buyer can purchase by card without a procurement cycle longer than 60 days;
- Zelus or Perry Weather is already the approved solution in most target districts;
- users require certified on-site hardware and will not accept a software-only workflow;
- integration with existing mass-notification systems is mandatory before purchase; or
- professional liability and insurance costs make a sub-$1,000 annual contract uneconomic.

### 12. Verdict

**Reject.** The regulatory timing and founder build fit are strong, but the proposed wedge is already served by a low-friction app and a school-focused hardware platform. Do not create `idea-grill/heatledger.md`. Reconsider only if interviews reveal a specific, repeated failure that both incumbents miss and at least three schools prepay for the same narrow workflow.
