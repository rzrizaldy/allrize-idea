# PoolProof — 2026-08-13

**Contributor:** ChatGPT/Codex  
**File:** `trend-journal/2026-08-13-chatgpt.md`  
**Market:** United States first; Indonesia expansion is not relevant because lottery law, authorized ticket channels, age limits, taxation, and payment-processor rules are jurisdiction-specific  
**Motion:** B2C group utility — the lottery-pool captain pays and operates the workspace; adult friends, relatives, or coworkers join as participants; state-authorized lottery retailers remain the ticket-sale intermediary  
**Verdict:** Reject

## Summary

PoolProof is a proposed web app for documenting a private Powerball or Mega Millions pool before each drawing. The captain defines rules, records contributions, uploads front-and-back ticket photos, locks the participant roster, and gives every member a timestamped read-only record. It would never sell tickets, collect pool dues, hold prize money, choose numbers, or claim to make agreements legally enforceable.

The workflow is real. On August 13, 2026, Google Trends showed “powerball winner” at 1M+ US searches after a $1.04 billion jackpot was won. Official state lotteries explicitly tell groups to put rules in writing, record who contributed, photograph tickets before the drawing, and secure the originals. The five-year Trends series also shows that “lottery pool” has recurring but jackpot-driven demand.

The venture is nevertheless a Reject. Lottery Pool Manager, OfficeLotteryPools, WonYet, SmartCaptain, and several mobile apps already cover member tracking, ticket scanning, rules, shares, and results—often free or for $4–$8 per month. Stripe lists lotteries and games of chance among prohibited gambling businesses, so Stripe monetization cannot be assumed even if the app charges only for recordkeeping. Demand is episodic, captains are price-sensitive, free group chat and spreadsheets are good enough, and consumer reacquisition would recur with every jackpot cycle.

### Official group-play workflow

- The [New Jersey Lottery's pool guidance](https://www.njlottery.com/en-us/playingresponsibly/office-pools.html), collected **2026-08-13**, tells groups to record who contributed, establish written rules, choose a leader, keep originals secure, and distribute front-and-back ticket copies before the drawing.
- The same guidance identifies concrete edge cases: absent regular members, contributions made for another player, members entering or leaving the pool, personal tickets bought by the captain, retailer location, and purchase time.
- The [Iowa Lottery's group-play reminders](https://www.ialottery.com/Pages/PlayerSecurity/GroupPlayReminders.aspx) likewise recommend written rules, a per-drawing contribution list, participant age checks, and distribution of ticket images including identifying numbers before the draw.
- [WyoLotto](https://wyolotto.com/players/pool-play/) recommends a signed contract containing player names, buy-ins, number-selection rules, and lump-sum-versus-annuity treatment, plus ticket copies and secure storage.
- These official sources verify the recordkeeping job. They also provide free guidance that a captain can implement with a document, spreadsheet, and group chat.

### Tax and claiming complexity

- The IRS [2026 Instructions for Forms W-2G and 5754](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/iw2g), collected **2026-08-13**, state that Form 5754 is used when the person receiving reportable gambling winnings is not the actual winner or is one member of a group sharing the winnings.
- Form 5754 requires actual winners' names, addresses, taxpayer identification numbers, respective shares, and other details; it is furnished to the payer and retained for records rather than sent directly to the IRS.
- The instructions also state that regular federal withholding for qualifying lottery winnings is 24%.
- PoolProof should not collect tax IDs, prepare tax filings, or provide payout advice. Those responsibilities make a “jackpot mode” materially riskier than pre-draw recordkeeping.

### Existing alternatives and price evidence

- [Lottery Pool Manager](https://lotterypoolmanager.app/) advertises live Powerball and Mega Millions pool management, member and payment tracking, AI ticket scanning, automatic prize calculation, draw history, and payout splits. Its published plans are **free**, **$4/month**, and **$8/month**.
- [OfficeLotteryPools](https://www.officelotterypools.com/) lets captains set rules, invite members, collect member consent, upload tickets, lock records at draw time, and preserve timestamped intent. It offers a free tier with no card required and explicitly says its consent record is not a legal contract.
- [WonYet](https://wonyet.com/) supports 323 lotteries, scans and checks tickets, stores ticket data while discarding the photo, and includes shared office pools and share calculations. It says there is no paid plan and the first pool is free.
- [SmartCaptain](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/smartcaptain-pool-lottery/id1260664876) tracks payments and members, scans tickets, calculates prizes, and shares pool summaries. The US App Store showed 28 ratings and a 3.7 score; one 2024 review complained that ticket scanning was no longer reliable.
- [Winner Circle](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/winner-circle-lottery-pool/id6757233499) is a $1.99 offline-first iPhone app for member payments, proportional shares, and draw history.
- These products cover the proposed wedge and demonstrate a very low price ceiling.

### Stripe constraint

- Stripe's current [prohibited and restricted businesses page](https://stripe.com/legal/restricted-businesses), collected **2026-08-13**, lists games of chance, lotteries, and entry or player fees promising prizes under prohibited gambling businesses.
- Stripe's [restricted-business FAQ](https://support.stripe.com/questions/prohibited-and-restricted-businesses-list-faqs) says account review ultimately determines support and that explicit prior approval may be possible for some listed categories, while some categories are never eligible.
- A recordkeeping-only SaaS that never handles stakes may be distinguishable from operating a lottery, but approval cannot be assumed. This directly conflicts with the repository's preference for a straightforward Stripe-backed purchase path.

### GitHub and founder build-fit

## Inference

- The durable job is “prove who was in, what was bought, and what everyone agreed to before the numbers are drawn,” not “improve the odds of winning.”
- Jackpot growth repeatedly creates short acquisition windows, but the winning event resets the jackpot and removes urgency. The August 13 spike is therefore an acquisition event, not a retention engine.
- A safe product would need to stay entirely outside ticket sales, dues, pooled wallets, prize custody, tax-ID collection, and claim submission.
- Even that narrow boundary does not create differentiation because current products already offer locked tickets, rules, consent, shares, and member portals.
- The price evidence suggests consumers expect the utility to be free or nearly free. A one-time $9 fee might match episodic use better than a subscription, but free incumbents make conversion doubtful.
- Paid search around a billion-dollar jackpot would likely attract expensive lottery, news, and courier advertisers. Organic pages would lose traffic after the jackpot resets.
- Indonesia is not a logical secondary launch: national and regional lottery legality, payments, cultural acceptance, and distribution differ fundamentally, while Stripe support for Indonesia does not solve gambling restrictions.

## Assumptions

- At least some US pool captains run pools manually through Venmo, cash, spreadsheets, and group chats.
- Captains fear disputes enough to adopt a dedicated record before a draw.
- Participants will click an invite and acknowledge rules without creating a full account.
- A timestamped record is useful evidence of intent even though it is not guaranteed to be legally enforceable.
- Captains would pay **$9 per jackpot run** or **$39/year** for a cleaner workflow.
- A payment processor would approve a software-only product that never handles wagers, ticket purchases, dues, or prizes.
- The product can obtain current draw schedules and results without violating data terms.
- Ticket photos can be stored securely without creating ownership confusion or exposing claim-relevant barcodes.
- Responsible-gambling controls and age gates can keep the product from encouraging increased play.
- Support costs will remain low even when users believe a large prize or disputed share is at stake.
- Organic and referral acquisition can keep CAC below **$4**.
- At least 30% of paid captains would use the product during a second jackpot cycle.

## Consulting assessment

### 1. Trend durability

“Lottery pool” has persistent low-level interest and recurring jackpot-linked peaks, but no verified upward trend. The 1M+ “powerball winner” spike came from a $1.04 billion result and will decay after the reset. The category recurs; the urgency does not persist.

### 2. Customer and JTBD

- **Motion:** B2C group utility.
- **Who pays:** the adult pool captain.
- **Who uses:** captain and adult participants.
- **Intermediary:** an authorized state lottery retailer sells the physical tickets; the product must not act as retailer, courier, wagering operator, or prize custodian.
- **JTBD:** “Before the drawing, freeze who paid, which tickets belong to the pool, and which rules everyone accepted so the group can verify the same record later.”

### 3. Pain evidence

Official state lotteries independently recommend exactly the proposed actions, confirming a real dispute-prevention workflow. Captains must resolve member changes, missing contributions, personal versus group tickets, ticket distribution, and prize-split rules. However, official guidance does not quantify frequency, losses, software spend, or willingness to pay.

### 4. Alternatives

1. Group chat plus ticket photos.
2. Google Sheets or Excel contribution ledger.
3. Shared Google Drive folder.
4. Written agreement template from a state lottery.
5. Lottery Pool Manager.
6. OfficeLotteryPools.
7. WonYet.
8. SmartCaptain.
9. Winner Circle.
10. Other mobile pool and ticket-scanner apps.
11. Attorney and tax professional after a material win.

The dedicated software alternatives already span the complete wedge at free-to-$8 monthly prices.

### 5. Wedge

The narrowest plausible wedge would be a privacy-first, pre-draw evidence seal:

1. Captain creates one draw.
2. Rules are versioned and acknowledged.
3. Participants and contributions are frozen at a cutoff.
4. Front-and-back ticket photos are uploaded before the draw.
5. Personal tickets are explicitly excluded.
6. Every participant receives the same timestamped record.
7. A cryptographic digest allows later verification.
8. Data exports as a neutral PDF/ZIP.
9. The app never checks winnings, moves money, or provides legal advice.

This is clear but not sufficiently differentiated from OfficeLotteryPools' locked records and ticket visibility.

### 6. MVP

If tested despite the Reject verdict, a strong solo developer could ship a non-production prototype in one week:

1. Magic-link pool and participant access.
2. One-draw roster and contribution ledger.
3. Rule template with version history.
4. Required participant acknowledgement.
5. Ticket-image upload and manual metadata.
6. Pre-draw cutoff with immutable event log.
7. Read-only participant page.
8. PDF/ZIP evidence export.
9. Automatic deletion after 90 days.
10. Responsible-play disclosure and age confirmation.
11. No Stripe checkout until written processor approval.
12. No dues, wallets, ticket sales, result checking, winnings, tax IDs, or payouts.

### 7. AI role

AI is unnecessary. Ticket-number OCR creates high-consequence errors and is already offered by competitors. Rules, participant status, deadlines, hashes, and exports should be deterministic. At most, OCR could suggest ticket metadata with mandatory human confirmation after the core product is validated. The concept should not be marketed as AI.

### 8. Pricing

The only credible hypothesis is:

- **Free:** one draw, up to ten participants.
- **$9 Jackpot Run:** unlimited draws for 30 days and evidence exports.
- **$39/year Captain:** recurring pool history and up to 50 participants.

This is not a valid launch plan until Stripe or another reputable processor gives written approval for the exact software-only model. Incumbent pricing suggests weak revenue per captain and little room for support or paid acquisition.

### 9. Distribution

Possible channels are jackpot-triggered SEO, responsible-play content, office-pool agreement searches, and referrals from state-specific lottery information publishers. Employer/HR distribution is unattractive because many employers may not want to endorse workplace gambling. The brief spike and crowded search results make repeatable low-CAC acquisition doubtful.

### 10. Risks

- **Competition:** exact feature overlap with several active free and low-cost products.
- **Payments:** Stripe lists lotteries and games of chance as prohibited; approval is uncertain.
- **Legal:** state laws, age limits, ticket ownership, group agreements, and claiming rules differ.
- **Gambling facilitation:** features that collect dues or sell entries can cross the product boundary.
- **Tax:** group wins require sensitive identity and share information; the app should not prepare or store it.
- **Trust:** a recordkeeping failure matters most when enormous money is at stake.
- **Security:** ticket barcodes, participant details, contribution histories, and possible winning tickets are sensitive.
- **Market:** most groups may never face a dispute and will remain on chat plus spreadsheets.
- **Pricing:** incumbents anchor the category at free, $1.99 once, or $4–$8 per month.
- **CAC:** jackpot keywords are crowded and event-driven.
- **Churn:** demand collapses after jackpots reset; annual retention is unverified.
- **Support:** disputed pools can create legal expectations far beyond a low-price SaaS.
- **Data:** results and game rules vary by jurisdiction and can change.
- **Responsible play:** product design could unintentionally increase gambling frequency.
- **Indonesia:** expansion introduces fundamental legal and cultural risk rather than simple localization.

### 11. Kill criteria

This concept is rejected now. Reconsider only if all of the following external facts change:

1. Stripe gives written approval for charging only the captain's software fee.
2. Twenty US captains of 10+ person pools are interviewed.
3. At least twelve use a manual spreadsheet/chat workflow every jackpot cycle.
4. At least eight report a material dispute or repeated administrative burden.
5. At least six pay a **$9 non-gambling software deposit** before product development.
6. OfficeLotteryPools and Lottery Pool Manager users identify one unsolved workflow rather than merely bugs.
7. A gaming attorney defines a viable multi-state boundary with no ticket, stake, prize, or payout handling.
8. Organic acquisition produces deposits below **$4 CAC**.
9. At least four captains return for a second jackpot cycle.
10. Support burden remains below ten minutes per active draw.

Keep the idea dead if processor approval is absent, fewer than six deposits arrive, users want money/ticket handling, the only wedge is nicer design, or free incumbents solve the workflow.

### 12. Verdict

**Reject.** The official workflow pain and recurring jackpot cycles are verified, and the founder could ship the software. But this is the wrong venture: exact incumbents already provide the core workflow for free or at very low prices; Google Trends shows episodic rather than compounding demand; consumer CAC and churn are structurally difficult; a high-stakes dispute creates disproportionate support and legal risk; and Stripe monetization cannot be assumed for a lottery-adjacent product. No `idea-grill` promotion is warranted.
