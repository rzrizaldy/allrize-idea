**Verdict:** Watch

## Summary

CompanionCadence is a Stripe-monetizable web app that turns multi-state US companion-AI disclosure and crisis-referral duties into an auditable event ledger and California annual-report packager. New York’s AI Companion Models Law (GBL Art. 47) has been in effect since November 5, 2025, with session-start and every-three-hour AI-not-human notices plus crisis protocols; California’s SB 243 has been in effect since January 1, 2026, with published safety protocols, known-minor break reminders, a private right of action ($1,000 per violation), and Office of Suicide Prevention reporting beginning July 1, 2027. The durable job is not “another crisis classifier”—NOPE already sells usage-priced evaluate APIs for that—but **disclosure-cadence logging, protocol versioning, referral-count evidence, and OSP report assembly** for small operators who cannot staff a compliance program. Evidence of statute and enforcement posture is strong; willingness to pay for a ledger (versus counsel memos + NOPE + in-house logging) is unverified, so the correct verdict is Watch, not Build.

### New York AI Companion Models Law (effective)

- New York Governor Kathy Hochul’s office announced on **November 10, 2025** that first-in-nation AI companion safeguards were in effect as of **November 5, 2025**: operators must implement crisis-intervention protocols that detect suicidal ideation or self-harm and refer users to crisis providers, and must notify users they are interacting with AI at session start and at least every three hours of continued use. Enforcement is by the Attorney General; fines support suicide-prevention programs. Source: [Governor Hochul press release](https://www.governor.ny.gov/news/governor-hochul-pens-letter-ai-companion-companies-notifying-them-safeguard-requirements-are), collected **2026-08-12**.
- N.Y. General Business Law §1701 requires a protocol with reasonable efforts to detect and address suicidal ideation or self-harm expressions, including referral to crisis providers such as 988. Source: [N.Y. GBL §1701](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/GBS/1701) via [newyork.public.law](https://newyork.public.law/laws/n.y._general_business_law_section_1701), collected **2026-08-12**.
- N.Y. General Business Law §1702 requires a clear and conspicuous notification at the beginning of any AI companion interaction (need not exceed once per day) and at least every three hours for continuing interactions, stating the user is not communicating with a human. Source: [N.Y. GBL §1702](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/GBS/1702) via [newyork.public.law](https://newyork.public.law/laws/n.y._general_business_law_section_1702), collected **2026-08-12**.
- Morrison Foerster’s **November 20, 2025** client alert summarizes NY Art. 47 vs CA SB 243: NY uses a three-part conjunctive definition (memory/personalization + unprompted emotion questions + sustained personal dialogue), applies three-hour notices to **all** users, authorizes AG penalties up to **$15,000 per day**, and has **no** annual reporting mandate. Source: [MoFo alert](https://www.mofo.com/resources/insights/251120-new-york-and-california-enact-landmark-ai), collected **2026-08-12**.

### California SB 243 (chaptered; core duties live)

- California SB 243 (Padilla), chaptered **October 13, 2025**, adds Business and Professions Code Chapter 22.6 (§§22601–22606). Primary text defines companion chatbots (with customer-service, video-game, and voice-assistant carve-outs), requires AI-not-human notice when a reasonable person would be misled, requires a published protocol preventing production of suicidal ideation/self-harm content and crisis referrals, adds known-minor break reminders every three hours plus sexually explicit safeguards, requires a minors-unsuitability disclosure, and creates a private right of action for the greater of actual damages or **$1,000 per violation**. Annual reports to the Office of Suicide Prevention begin **July 1, 2027**, including crisis-referral counts and protocol descriptions, without user identifiers. Source: [California Legislative Information — SB 243 chaptered text](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/billTextClient.xhtml?bill_id=202520260SB243), collected **2026-08-12**.
- Gunderson Dettmer’s client insight notes that SB 243 duties fall on the **operator** who makes the platform available to California users even when the model is third-party (e.g., OpenAI/Anthropic/Google), so compliance cannot be delegated to the model vendor alone. Source: [Gunderson insight](https://www.gunder.com/en/news-insights/insights/client-insight-california-sb-243-new-compliance-requirements-for-operators-of-ai-companion-chatbots), collected **2026-08-12**.

## Inference

- The demand shift is **regulatory process**, not a Trends spike: NY notices every three hours for all users create a continuous logging job; CA’s July 2027 OSP report creates a dated, countable evidence job (referral counts + protocol text) that operators must start collecting now.
- The open wedge versus NOPE is **operations evidence**, not classification: keep detection vendors or in-house models for “reasonable efforts,” and sell a ledger that proves disclosures fired, protocols were published/versioned, referrals were counted, and the OSP CSV/PDF can be exported without rebuilding logs from chat dumps.
- One strong developer can ship an MVP as a multi-tenant web app with Stripe Billing, webhook/SDK event intake, protocol page hosting, and report export—without claiming clinical judgment or guaranteeing compliance.
- Customer-service and video-game carve-outs shrink the TAM; many “helpful assistants” will argue they are out of scope, which raises sales friction and kill risk.

## Assumptions

- A meaningful set of US companion-AI startups (character/roleplay, AI boyfriend/girlfriend, social companions) serve CA and/or NY users and lack in-house compliance tooling.
- Operators will pay ~$79–$199/month for an evidence ledger rather than only buying NOPE evaluate calls and storing events in their own database.
- Counsel will accept software-generated cadence/referral logs as useful work-product support (not as a Safe Harbor).
- The product can stay on the safe side of medical-device and mental-health claims by refusing diagnosis language and by integrating (not replacing) crisis-detection vendors.
- Indonesia is not a v1 market unless operators there already monetize CA/NY users.

## Consulting assessment

### Trend durability

Durable as long as NY Art. 47 and CA SB 243 remain in force and additional states copy crisis-protocol + disclosure patterns. This is not a one-week search fad; Trends numeric series were unavailable here, and Trending Now on 2026-08-12 showed no related spike. Durability rests on statutes and enforcement letters, with CA reporting creating a recurring annual deadline after July 2027.

### Customer and JTBD

**Motion:** B2B. **Buyer:** founder, Head of Product, or Trust & Safety lead at a US companion-chatbot operator. **User:** the engineer who instruments chat sessions and the compliance owner who must answer counsel or OSP. **JTBD:** before an AG inquiry, private suit, or July 2027 OSP filing, prove that AI-not-human disclosures fired on the required cadence, that the published crisis protocol is current, and that crisis-referral notifications were counted without retaining forbidden user identifiers in the report.

### Pain evidence

Primary sources establish mandatory duties and material penalties (NY up to $15k/day AG; CA $1k/violation PRA). Gunderson clarifies operator—not model-vendor—liability. That is strong process pain. It does **not** yet prove prepaid demand for a third-party ledger versus a weekend of internal instrumentation plus NOPE.

### Alternatives

- In-house disclosure timers + Postgres event tables.
- NOPE Evaluate / Signpost for detection and crisis resource matching ($0.003/call).
- Outside counsel playbooks and one-off protocol PDFs on a marketing site.
- Model-provider safety layers (insufficient alone per operator-liability analysis).
- Doing nothing / arguing customer-service exemption.

Gap to test: **multi-state cadence + protocol CMS + OSP packager** sold as Stripe SaaS—not a second crisis-ML API and not a generic “AI safety GPT.”

### Wedge

v1: US operators with ≤50k MAU companion products. Ingest session events (start, 3-hour tick, known-minor flag, referral issued). Host versioned protocol pages. Dashboard NY vs CA rule differences. Export CA OSP annual packet (counts + protocol text only). Explicitly **not** a clinical detector, not age verification, not a full Trust & Safety suite.

### MVP

- Workspace + Stripe subscription.
- Lightweight SDK/webhook: `disclosure_shown`, `break_reminder_shown`, `referral_issued` (timestamp, jurisdiction flag, conversation opaque ID hash).
- Protocol editor with public URL + version history.
- Cadence health view (sessions missing NY 3-hour ticks).
- CA OSP export (CSV/PDF) of annual referral counts + current protocol snapshot.
- Optional NOPE (or other) evaluate `request_id` attach field—buy detection, sell evidence.

### AI role

AI may draft plain-language protocol summaries from operator-filled checklists and flag missing statutory elements for human edit. It must **not** invent crisis detections, diagnose users, or auto-file with OSP. Durable value is event integrity, jurisdiction rules, and report assembly.

### Risks

- **Market:** carve-outs and definition fights shrink buyers; big platforms build in-house.
- **Product:** without trusted detection, the ledger feels incomplete; with detection claims, liability spikes.
- **Technical:** high-volume chat event ingestion and privacy minimization (no forbidden identifiers in OSP exports).
- **Legal:** private right of action adjacency; product must avoid guaranteeing compliance or clinical efficacy; counsel review required.
- **GTM:** crowded AI-safety narrative; hard to message “boring ledger” against flashy detectors.

### Kill criteria

- Stop if 10 operator interviews fail to produce at least 3 prepaid pilots at ≥$99/month within 60 days of outreach.
- Stop if NOPE or a major model provider ships a bundled disclosure-cadence + OSP export at or below SMB prices that closes the wedge.
- Stop if CA OSP publishes a free official filing portal that accepts unstructured uploads and operators treat that as sufficient without continuous logging.
- Stop if counsel consensus is that operators remain out of scope for typical “assistant” products so narrowly that TAM cannot support a solo SaaS.
