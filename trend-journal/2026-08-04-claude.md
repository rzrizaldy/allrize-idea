# EPA AIM Act Refrigerant Leak-Rate Compliance Tracking for Small US HVAC/R Contractors — 2026-08-04

**Contributor:** Claude
**File:** `trend-journal/2026-08-04-claude.md`
**Market:** United States first; Indonesia relevance: not established (the demand driver is a US federal rule)
**Motion:** B2B (the HVAC/R contractor or the equipment owner-operator pays; field technicians use it)
**Verdict:** Reject

## Summary

On January 1, 2026 the EPA's HFC leak repair and recordkeeping requirements under the AIM Act (40 CFR Part 84, Subpart C) dropped the regulated charge threshold from 50 pounds to 15 pounds. That change pulls a large population of previously unregulated equipment (mid-size rooftop units, walk-in coolers, small commercial refrigeration racks) into mandatory leak-rate calculation, timed repair, and three-year recordkeeping. On paper this is the ideal solo-SaaS setup: a dated federal mandate, a numeric calculation the buyer currently does in a spreadsheet, and a paper trail that must survive an inspection. In practice the window has already been taken. At least four purpose-built micro-SaaS products (RefriTrak, RefriComply, FMHero, RefrigerantTrack) launched into this exact wedge within the first weeks of 2026, three of them offer a free tier, and entry pricing has already collapsed to the $10 to $29 per month band. A solo builder entering in August 2026 arrives seven months late into a price war against products indistinguishable from what he would build. Reject.

## Verified evidence

- **The regulatory driver is real, federal, and dated.** The EPA HFC Leak Repair and Management Rule took effect January 1, 2026 and applies to owners or operators of HFC-containing appliances with a charge of 15 pounds or greater, replacing the prior 50-pound trigger. A leak rate must be calculated every time refrigerant is added, repair timelines are triggered above threshold, and records must be retained for at least three years. [Fexa, "Refrigerant Regulations in 2026: A Complete Guide"](https://fexa.io/guide/refrigerant-regulations-in-2026-your-complete-guide/); [Nuvolo, "Decoding Refrigerant Regulations 2026"](https://www.nuvolo.com/blog/decoding-refrigerant-regulations-2026-what-facilities-leaders-need-to-know-now/)
- **The rule is codified and citable, not vendor marketing.** The applicable sections are 40 CFR 84.106 (leak repair) and 40 CFR 84.108 (automatic leak detection for systems at or above 1,500 lbs, by January 1, 2027). [eCFR 40 CFR 84.106](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-40/chapter-I/subchapter-C/part-84/subpart-C/section-84.106); [40 CFR 84.108](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/40/84.108)
- **EPA is still actively amending the rule in 2026, which confirms it is live rather than dormant.** A Federal Register action dated May 26, 2026 excludes road and intermodal container transport refrigeration units from the HFC leak repair requirements. [Federal Register 2026-10388](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/05/26/2026-10388/phasedown-of-hydrofluorocarbons-excluding-road-and-intermodal-container-transport-refrigeration)
- **The scope expansion is large.** Industry write-ups put the increase in covered equipment at roughly 70 percent, and explicitly name small and mid-sized businesses that were previously exempt as now in scope. [HVAC School, "The EPA's 15-Pound Refrigerant Threshold in 2026"](http://www.hvacrschool.com/the-epas-15-pound-refrigerant-threshold-in-2026/); [RefriTrak, "2026 EPA 15-lb Refrigerant Rule: Who's Affected?"](https://www.refritrak.com/en/blog/2026-epa-regulations-15-lb-threshold)
- **The exact solo-SaaS wedge is already occupied by multiple solo-scale products.** RefriComply markets itself as "built specifically for HVAC contractors — without the enterprise price tag," runs QR-code-to-browser field entry, automatic leak rate calculation, and one-click audit PDFs, at $23/mo (annual Solo), $47/mo (Pro), $79/mo (Business), with a 14-day no-card trial. [refricomply.com](https://www.refricomply.com/)
- **Entry pricing has already been driven to free.** RefriTrak offers a free plan up to 2 users; FMHero offers a free single-user tier and $15/mo annual paid; RefrigerantTrack advertises a free tier for up to 5 systems. RefriComply's own comparison table lists RefriTrak starting at $9.99/mo. [RefriTrak, "Best Refrigerant & Freon Compliance Software 2026" (published 2026-01-06)](https://www.refritrak.com/en/blog/best-refrigerant-compliance-software-2026); [refricomply.com pricing/comparison](https://www.refricomply.com/); [refrigeranttrack.com](https://refrigeranttrack.com/)
- **The enterprise tier is also occupied and clearly segmented away.** Fexa Trakref (formerly Trakref) is now folded into the Fexa CMMS with custom enterprise pricing; ServiceChannel starts around $1,000+; Sphera targets Fortune 500. General-purpose SafetyCulture covers the job crudely with templates at $24/user/mo. [RefriTrak comparison guide](https://www.refritrak.com/en/blog/best-refrigerant-compliance-software-2026); [Fexa Trakref](https://fexa.io/fexa-trakref/)
- **Competitors are competing on the same launch narrative.** Both RefriTrak and RefriComply publish near-identical "Best Refrigerant Tracking Software 2026" comparison posts, which is a signal that the SEO channel for this keyword is already contested by incumbents in-market. [RefriComply blog](https://www.refricomply.com/blog/best-refrigerant-tracking-software-2026/); [RefriTrak blog](https://www.refritrak.com/en/blog/best-refrigerant-compliance-software-2026)
- **No meaningful open source exists for this workflow.** A GitHub-restricted search for refrigerant tracking and compliance returned only unrelated engineering and home-automation projects (python-hvac, ESPHome integrations, a superheat spreadsheet, BHoM's refrigerant impact tool). No maintained project implements Part 84 leak-rate logic or recordkeeping. ## Inference

- **Demand direction is genuinely up, and the shape is a step function rather than a spike.** Reasoned from the effective date (2026-01-01), the follow-on ALD deadline (2027-01-01), and the fact that four purpose-built vendors published launch and comparison content in January 2026. Vendors do not simultaneously build in a market they believe is flat.
- **The buyer's pain is real but is a compliance chore, not a revenue problem.** Reasoned from the nature of the job: leak-rate math, a repair clock, and a three-year archive. None of that makes the contractor money, which caps willingness to pay.
- **The competitive structure is already a commodity race.** Reasoned from four near-identical feature lists (QR code, auto leak rate, threshold alerts, PDF audit report) with three free tiers and a $9.99 floor. When four independent teams converge on the same feature list within one quarter, the product is not defensible.
- **The absence of open source is not a moat here.** Reasoned from the fact that the hard part is not the code, it is the regulatory interpretation and the field-adoption problem, and the commercial vendors have already solved both to a good-enough standard.
- **A solo builder's realistic wedge is not the contractor.** Reasoned from the fact that Part 84 liability sits on the equipment owner-operator, not only the servicing contractor, and none of the observed vendors is positioned primarily as an owner-side product with contractor-document-collection workflow. That adjacency is where a differentiated entry would have to live.

## Assumptions

- That the free tiers offered by RefriTrak, FMHero, and RefrigerantTrack are durable rather than temporary land-grabs. Not verified.
- That EPA enforcement against small operators will be actual rather than nominal. No enforcement action against a small HVAC contractor under Part 84 Subpart C was located in this run. If enforcement stays nominal, willingness to pay drops further, which strengthens the Reject.
- That the incumbent micro-SaaS products have real paying customers. RefriComply's site says it is "onboarding our first contractors now," which reads as pre-traction. It is possible all four are unmonetized. This does not change the verdict, because a solo builder would still face four free competitors.
- That the penalty figures quoted on vendor sites ($124,426/day, per 40 CFR 19.4) are correctly applied. RefriComply itself labels these "pending legal review" and flags its $871,982 weekly figure as its own extrapolation, not a regulatory number. Treated here as vendor marketing, not verified fact.

## Consulting assessment

### 1. Trend durability

Durable, not a spike. The driver is a codified federal rule with a staged schedule: the 15-pound leak repair and recordkeeping obligation from 2026-01-01, and automatic leak detection for systems at or above 1,500 lbs from 2027-01-01. EPA was still issuing amendments to the rule in May 2026. This is a permanent change in the compliance baseline, not a news cycle. Durability is not the problem with this idea.

### 2. Customer and JTBD

**Motion:** B2B. **Who pays:** the HVAC/R contracting company owner (1 to 20 technicians), or the equipment owner-operator (grocery, restaurant group, small industrial, property manager). **Who uses:** the field technician, under duress, at the end of a job. **JTBD:** "When I add refrigerant to a covered system, help me record it correctly and keep a three-year file I can hand an inspector, without adding paperwork to my tech's day." The secondary job for the owner-operator is: "prove my contractors documented what they did."

### 3. Pain evidence

Weaker than I would like, and I am flagging that. The pain descriptions I found ("most HVAC operations still manage refrigerant tracking with spreadsheets, paper logs, or nothing at all"; "techs won't do paperwork") come from vendor marketing pages, not from buyers speaking for themselves. Sources: [refricomply.com](https://www.refricomply.com/), [oxmaint EPA 608 page](https://oxmaint.com/industries/hvac/epa-608-refrigerant-tracking-compliance-software). I could not surface a first-person contractor complaint thread on Reddit, HN, or a review site in this run. G2 and Capterra entries for these products were not located. The one non-vendor source, [HVAC School](http://www.hvacrschool.com/the-epas-15-pound-refrigerant-threshold-in-2026/), is trade education confirming the rule change rather than a complaint. **Pain is inferred from the regulation, not evidenced from the buyer.** That alone would justify Watch rather than Build; combined with the competitive picture it justifies Reject.

The gap between the $0-to-$79 SMB band and the custom enterprise band is already closed on the SMB side. There is no unserved price point.

### 5. Wedge

There is no defensible wedge left at the contractor tier. The only remaining differentiated angle I can name is **owner-side contractor document collection**: a product sold to the equipment owner (multi-site restaurant group, small grocery chain, property manager) whose job is chasing service records out of three different HVAC vendors and assembling a per-asset file that survives an inspection. That is a document-chasing and vendor-portal problem, not a leak-rate-math problem, and none of the four SMB vendors is positioned there. But this is a narrower buyer, a longer sales cycle, and a job that overlaps heavily with the CAM-reconciliation and COI-collection patterns this journal already rated Watch on 2026-07-30. It is not strong enough to overturn the verdict.

### 6. MVP

For completeness, not as a recommendation. Asset register with refrigerant type and full charge; service log entry (refrigerant added, recovered, date, technician cert number) via QR-to-browser form; annualized leak rate per the Part 84 formula with threshold flags at 10/20/30 percent; repair clock with 14 and 30 day deadlines; three-year immutable record store; one-click PDF audit pack. Realistic build effort for one strong developer: four to six weeks to a shippable v1, most of it in field UX and PDF output rather than logic. The regulatory interpretation and the threshold matrix are the actual risk, not the engineering.

### 7. AI role

AI adds little here, and I will say that plainly rather than manufacture a use case. The core is deterministic arithmetic on a defined formula plus a date clock. The one honest AI application is ingestion: OCR and extraction from photographed paper service tickets and cylinder tags so an owner can backfill history from a shoebox of invoices. That is a real onboarding accelerant but it is a migration feature, not a product. Anyone positioning this as an "AI refrigerant compliance" product would be dressing up a CRUD app.

### 8. Pricing

Anchored to observed alternatives, a new entrant would have to land at roughly $29 to $59 per month per contracting company for the contractor motion, which is exactly where RefriComply already sits, against free from three others. That is a losing anchor. The only pricing that would work is the owner-side motion at $99 to $299 per month per portfolio, priced against the cost of a facilities coordinator's time rather than against refrigerant tools. That reframing is the only version worth a second look.

### 9. Distribution

The obvious channels are already contested. The "best refrigerant tracking software 2026" keyword is being fought over by at least two in-market vendors publishing comparison content, so SEO is a late-entrant's worst channel here. Remaining plausible channels for the owner-side variant: refrigeration service trade associations, restaurant and grocery franchise operator groups, and RSES / HVAC School's audience. First 100 customers would have to come from direct outbound to multi-site food service operators, not from search. That is a much slower path than this journal's bar for a solo builder.

### 10. Risks

- **Commoditization risk, already realized.** Four near-identical products, three free tiers, a $9.99 floor, seven months before entry.
- **Enforcement risk.** If EPA does not enforce Part 84 against small operators, the whole category deflates. No enforcement action was located in this run.
- **Regulatory amendment risk.** EPA amended the rule as recently as May 2026. Further carve-outs shrink the market. [Federal Register 2026-10388](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/05/26/2026-10388/phasedown-of-hydrofluorocarbons-excluding-road-and-intermodal-container-transport-refrigeration)
- **Bundling risk.** ServiceTitan, Housecall Pro, and the FSM incumbents can add a leak-rate module as a checkbox feature to an existing paid base. RefriComply's own comparison table already lists ServiceTitan at $145/tech/mo, which means the FSM vendors are already in the contractor's stack.
- **Field adoption risk.** The binding constraint is whether a technician logs the entry at all. That is a behavior problem no vendor has solved, and it caps every product in this category equally.

### 11. Kill criteria

Falsifiable thresholds, stated for the record even though the verdict is already Reject:

- **Competitor floor.** If two or more purpose-built competitors offer a free tier covering the core job. **Already true.** Kill.
- **Late entry.** If more than 90 days have elapsed since the compliance effective date without an unserved segment identified. **Already true (216 days).** Kill.
- **Pain sourcing.** If no first-person buyer complaint can be sourced from a non-vendor channel within one week of searching. **Currently true.** Kill unless reversed.
- **Enforcement.** If no EPA enforcement action against an operator under 100 employees is published by 2027-01-01, treat the willingness-to-pay assumption as broken.
- **Reversal condition.** This would return to Watch only if a specific owner-side buyer (a multi-site operator with 3+ HVAC vendors) states in their own words that assembling contractor refrigerant records is a named person's recurring job, and two such buyers commit to paying $150+/mo. That is a two-week outbound test against a list of regional grocery and restaurant groups.

### 12. Verdict: Reject

The reasoning that drove it, in order of weight.

First, timing. This journal's repeated pattern is that a dated compliance obligation is the best demand driver available to a solo builder, but only if you arrive before or at the deadline. The 2026-07-31 Claude entry rejected building the day after a deadline; this is the same error at seven months of magnitude. The wave broke in January 2026.

Second, the competitive floor. Four purpose-built products, three of them free at the low end, all shipping the identical feature list of QR entry, automatic leak rate, threshold alerts, and PDF audit pack. There is no price point a fifth entrant can occupy and no feature the fifth entrant would ship first. The absence of open source, which normally reads as an opening, is irrelevant when four commercial teams have already filled it.

Third, and the reason this is Reject rather than Watch: I could not source the pain from the buyer. Every "contractors are drowning in spreadsheets" line I found traced back to a vendor selling the cure. A regulation existing is not the same as a buyer feeling pain, and this journal's standard is the buyer's own words. Without that, and with the market already crowded, there is nothing here worth a solo builder's next quarter.

What I am keeping from this run: the owner-side document-collection pattern keeps recurring across unrelated verticals in this journal (CAM reconciliation 2026-07-30, subcontractor COI 2026-07-30, and now refrigerant records). Three independent hits on "a small operator has to chase compliance documents out of multiple vendors and assemble a defensible file" is worth researching as a horizontal pattern rather than as a fourth vertical. That is the follow-up I would recommend, and it belongs in its own entry.
