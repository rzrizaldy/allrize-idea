## Executive summary

**ParkChange Ops** is an event-to-booking impact workspace for small national-park and adventure-tour operators. It watches official National Park Service alerts, maps a new closure or restriction to scheduled departures and itinerary stops, then gives an operator a human-approved workflow for alternate plans, guide tasks, traveler notices, and refund or credit decisions.

This is technically small enough for one strong developer, but the evidence does not yet justify Build. The current Google Trends spike is an anniversary and fee-free-day event, not proof that operators will buy software. Existing travel platforms already own itineraries, customer communications, and trip operations. The only plausible wedge is a lightweight NPS-alert overlay that works without forcing a booking-system migration.

## Verified evidence

1. **The spike has an event explanation.** National Park Week runs August 22–30, 2026, and August 25 is both the National Park Service's 110th birthday and a fee-free day for U.S. citizens and residents. NPS manages more than 400 parks. [National Park Service](https://www.nps.gov/subjects/npscelebrates/national-park-week.htm)

2. **Operators face a real change-management job.** A commercial tour-software vendor describes last-minute changes and cancellations as manual coordination across schedules, staff, and customers, with resulting errors and delays. This is vendor evidence, not independent measurement of market size or purchase intent. [Resmark Systems](https://www.resmarksystems.com/what-tour-operators-struggle-with-before-switching-to-booking-software-and-what-changes-after)

3. **Official alert data is available.** The National Park Service API exposes authoritative park data including alerts, news, events, campgrounds, and visitor centers. [NPS Developer Resources](https://www.nps.gov/subjects/developer/index.htm?%253E=)

4. **The operational environment is constrained.** AP reported that roughly 1,500 lower-priority maintenance and other projects across more than 200 sites, with an estimated combined value of $400 million, were expected to remain undone; it also reported a repair backlog above $24 billion and material workforce reductions. This establishes constrained operations, but does **not** establish that closures are increasing. [Associated Press](https://apnews.com/article/national-parks-white-house-priorities-051fd27a454094a7cbec0718517e35e3)

5. **Consumers already have a free official alert surface.** The NPS app provides information for more than 400 parks, including maps, tours, amenities, and alerts. ParkChange Ops therefore cannot win as another consumer park-alert app. [National Park Service app](https://www.nps.gov/subjects/digital/nps-apps.htm)

6. **Incumbents own adjacent workflow.** Travefy's Core plan is listed at $39 per month when billed annually and includes itineraries, proposals, CRM, automations, invoicing, email, and mobile apps; it says more than 30,000 agents and agencies use the product. [Travefy pricing](https://travefy.com/plans/pricing) WeTravel says it serves more than 10,000 travel businesses and combines itinerary building, payments, booking/trip management, manifests, and traveler details. [WeTravel](https://www.wetravel.com/)

7. **An alert connector is feasible, not defensible.** The open-source [national-parks-mcp-server](https://github.com/cyanheads/national-parks-mcp-server) already wraps NPS API parks, alerts/closures, campgrounds, activities, and events in TypeScript. Its low adoption means it is evidence of implementation feasibility only.

8. **Founder fit is strong.** [Deck of Prosperity](https://github.com/rzrizaldy/deck_of_prosperity) demonstrates React, TypeScript, Vite, Vitest, Playwright, and web deployment. [RizeBot Hermes](https://github.com/rzrizaldy/rizebot-hermes) demonstrates deterministic monitoring, change detection, state recording, bounded reports, and human review gates.

## Inference

- The durable job is not “help me discover a national park.” It is **“tell me which booked departures are affected, what the approved fallback is, and who must be notified.”**
- Maintenance and staffing constraints may increase operational uncertainty, but the sources do not prove a rising closure rate. That relationship must remain a hypothesis.
- The wedge is credible only as a vendor-neutral overlay: ingest departures by CSV or calendar, match park codes and dates, and export notices or tasks back to the operator's existing system.
- Travefy and WeTravel make a full itinerary or booking product unattractive for a solo founder. ParkChange Ops must remain a narrow incident workflow.
- The U.S. is the right first market because NPS offers a consistent official data layer. Indonesian expansion could be relevant later for tour operators around conservation areas, volcanoes, and weather disruptions, but fragmented agencies and feeds would make it a separate data-integration problem.

## Assumptions

- NPS alerts are timely and structured enough for operational use.
- Operators can map their repeated itinerary stops to NPS park codes without custom engineering.
- A meaningful segment still checks park pages or apps manually before departures.
- Operators will pay **$149 for a seasonal setup** or **$49 per active month** for a standalone overlay.
- Human approval before any traveler notice is sufficient to manage false positives and liability.
- CSV/calendar import plus email/task export is enough for an MVP; deep booking-platform integrations are not initially required.

## 1. Trend durability

The exact Trends spike is **episodic** and explained by the 110th birthday and fee-free day. It should not be interpreted as category growth. The underlying operational job recurs whenever closures, fires, weather, road restrictions, or facility outages affect a scheduled trip, but its frequency and economic cost per operator are unverified.

## 2. Customer and job to be done

- **Who pays:** a small U.S. national-park or adventure-tour operator, destination-management company, or multi-guide outfitter.
- **Who uses:** the owner, operations coordinator, dispatcher, and guides.
- **Who receives value:** travelers receive clear, consistent change notices and alternatives.
- **Intermediaries:** NPS supplies the official alert feed; an existing booking/itinerary system supplies departure data and remains the system of record.

Core job: **When an official park condition changes, identify every affected departure and execute an approved operational response before staff or customers act on stale information.**

## 3. Pain evidence

The workflow is time-sensitive, crosses schedules, staff, and customers, and can create avoidable support load or refunds. However, today's research found no independent survey, buyer interview, public request for this exact product, or paid commitment. Pain is plausible; purchase urgency is not yet verified.

## 4. Current alternatives

- Manual checks of NPS pages and the free NPS app
- Email, SMS, spreadsheets, and guide group chats
- Travefy or WeTravel for itinerary and traveler communication
- General booking systems that already automate customer updates
- A custom NPS API script built by a technically capable operator

The strongest substitute is not another alert product; it is the operator's existing booking platform plus a manual check.

## 5. Differentiated wedge

**Official alert → affected departure → approved fallback → evidence log**, without replacing booking, payments, or itinerary software.

The product should show:

- which departures and itinerary stops intersect a new or changed alert;
- a suggested response drawn from the operator's own playbook;
- guide and operations tasks;
- a preview of traveler-facing notices;
- the approved decision, timestamp, and refund/credit disposition.

## 6. Solo MVP

A three-to-five-week web MVP could include:

1. Stripe Checkout and one operator workspace.
2. CSV or calendar import for departures, park codes, dates, and minimal contact references.
3. Scheduled NPS alert polling with stored diffs.
4. Deterministic date-and-park impact matching.
5. An impact queue with human approve/dismiss controls.
6. Reusable fallback playbooks and staff/customer message templates.
7. Exportable notices, task lists, and an incident decision log.

No automatic refunds, direct mass messaging, route optimization, or deep booking integrations in version one.

## 7. AI role

AI is optional and subordinate. It may classify unstructured alert text, propose a matching playbook, and draft a notice. Park/date matching, approval state, audit history, and outbound actions must remain deterministic. This avoids becoming a generic travel-planning wrapper.

## 8. Pricing and Stripe motion

Pricing is an assumption to test:

- **$149 one-time seasonal setup:** map repeated routes, park codes, and fallback playbooks.
- **$49 per active month:** monitoring, impact queue, templates, and decision log.
- Possible later tier: **$299 per season** for very small seasonal operators.

Stripe Checkout can support setup purchases and subscriptions. The price must beat the perceived cost of manual coordination while remaining distinct from a full booking-platform fee.

## 9. Distribution

Start with direct founder-led outreach to 25 operators running repeated departures around Yellowstone, Yosemite, Grand Canyon, Zion, and Great Smoky Mountains. Offer a manual “alert-to-departure audit” using the operator's next 30 days of trips.

Potential referral channels are booking-software consultants, outfitter associations, travel-insurance brokers, and safety/compliance consultants. None is verified as an acquisition channel yet.

## 10. Risks

- NPS alerts may be late, incomplete, duplicated, or too broad.
- Many changes may not affect a commercial itinerary.
- A wrong recommendation creates customer-service and liability risk.
- Operators may be seasonal, price-sensitive, and content with manual checks.
- Booking platforms can add alert integrations and bundle the wedge.
- Contact data and outbound messaging add privacy and deliverability obligations.
- Each park or route may require bespoke mapping, destroying solo-SaaS economics.

## 11. 48-hour validation experiment

Do not code the SaaS. Build a one-page offer and a manual sample impact packet using public NPS alerts and a hypothetical departure list. Contact 15 small operators and ask for their current pre-departure monitoring workflow, most recent disruptive change, time spent, customer consequences, and software stack.

Offer three operators a paid **$149 seasonal setup pilot** that includes route mapping, daily alert checks, an affected-trip report, and response templates.

## 12. Kill criteria and verdict

Advance to Build only if all are met:

- At least 8 of 15 operators manually check NPS pages/apps for upcoming departures.
- At least 3 operators pay a $149 pilot deposit.
- At least 80% of alerts map to departures without operator-specific code.
- The manual workflow saves at least one staff-hour per material change.
- No incumbent already provides equivalent alert-to-booking mapping at an acceptable price.

**Verdict: Watch.** Buildability is high, but trend quality, differentiation, distribution, and willingness to pay are not yet strong enough for promotion. Do not create an `idea-grill` entry.
