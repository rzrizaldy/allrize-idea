# Subcontractor COI Pre-Mobilization Gate — 2026-07-30

**Contributor:** ChatGPT/Codex
**Market:** United States first; Indonesia relevance: not yet established
**Verdict:** Watch

## Summary

Explore a narrow, project-level certificate-of-insurance (COI) gate for small US general contractors: before a subcontractor is scheduled to mobilize, the project administrator requests a COI through a no-login link, compares only a defined set of coverage requirements, records a human approval, and produces a time-stamped audit packet. This is a real recurring workflow, but it is **not** promoted to Build today: the category already has capable vendors, the research found no independently verified willingness-to-pay signal for the 25–150-subcontractor segment, and legal/compliance accuracy makes an automated approval promise unsafe.

## Inference

- A pre-mobilization gate may be a better wedge than “COI tracking software”: it ties the workflow to a specific operational decision, can start with a simple requirements checklist, and creates an auditable record instead of trying to replace full insurance verification.
- The proposed product is feasible for one strong developer as a conventional multi-tenant web app with uploads, structured fields, reminders, signed links, role-based access, Stripe, and a PDF/CSV audit export. OCR can prefill fields, but a human must approve coverage.
- Existing general COI products make a broad tracker a poor entry point. A Build verdict would require evidence that small GCs will pay specifically for a project-level gate that incumbent tools do not serve well enough.

## Assumptions

- US GCs with roughly 25–150 active subcontractors lack an adequately priced tool or are dissatisfied with incumbent setup and service.
- A project administrator can use a checklist configured from contract language without legal advice from the product.
- $49–$149 per month is a credible self-serve price, or a $299 per-project annualized-equivalent plan has enough urgency to convert.
- No insurance licensing, legal-practice, data-retention, or security obligation makes the initial product impractical. This must be reviewed with qualified US counsel before launch claims are made.

## Consulting assessment

### Trend durability

Insurance documentation, subcontractor onboarding, and renewal dates are recurring operational requirements rather than a novelty-driven AI trend. The Google Trends screen is inconclusive for volume or growth, so durability rests on the persistent workflow, not a search spike.

### Customer and JTBD

**Buyer:** owner, operations lead, or contract administrator at a small US general contractor. **User:** project administrator or project manager. **JTBD:** before letting a subcontractor mobilize, collect the requested insurance documentation, record whether it meets the project’s stated checklist, and prove who approved it and when.

### Pain evidence

The corroborating supplier material consistently describes email, spreadsheets, missing documentation, policy expiry, and review steps. That establishes a plausible pain, but it does not establish its severity for a small GC or prove they will switch/pay. Direct interviews are required.

### Alternatives

- Dedicated COI platforms: BCS, Certificial’s reviewed vendors, Remindax, TrustLayer, myCOI.
- Vertical construction systems and brokers with COI services.
- Spreadsheet plus calendar/email reminders and shared-drive folders.

The gap to test is not “tracking exists.” It is whether small teams need a fast, defensible **project mobilization decision** without enterprise procurement, integrations, or a managed-service contract.

### Wedge

One active project, one subcontractor invite link, one configured coverage checklist, and a clear **approved / needs correction / blocked** status. The first release must explicitly say it assists document collection and checklist review, not insurance verification or legal advice.

### MVP

- CSV/manual subcontractor list and project requirements form.
- Branded no-login upload/request link with reminder sequence.
- Manual field entry plus optional OCR prefill for carrier, policy dates, limits, and named-insured text.
- Human checklist approval, immutable event log, and downloadable audit packet.
- Stripe checkout for a self-serve paid workspace.

### AI role

AI/OCR only extracts candidate fields and highlights which checklist items appear absent. It must never decide coverage adequacy, interpret endorsements, or market itself as an insurer/legal reviewer. The durable value is the workflow record and escalation loop, not a generic document-chat interface.

### Pricing

**Hypothesis, not verified:** $49/month for one active project and $149/month for up to five projects, with a 14-day trial. A simpler test is a $99 paid concierge setup for one live project; this tests urgency before building integrations.

### Distribution

Start with owner-led outreach to small commercial GCs and construction contract administrators in one metro, using a free “pre-mobilization COI checklist” as lead capture. Follow with a live-project concierge setup. Do not rely on broad SEO until interview language identifies a differentiated, high-intent phrase.

### Risks

- **Market:** incumbents may already be affordable enough for the target segment.
- **Product:** a single audit packet may not be valuable enough to displace spreadsheets.
- **Technical/security:** sensitive insurance documents require secure storage, authorization controls, retention policy, and auditability.
- **Legal:** incorrect coverage or endorsement interpretation could create harmful reliance; retain human approval and obtain counsel.
- **Go-to-market:** owner outreach can be slow and channel trust may favor brokers or incumbent platforms.

### Kill criteria

Stop after 15 qualified US GC/contract-admin interviews unless at least 6 describe a live pre-mobilization documentation failure or >2 hours/month of recurring manual chase work, and at least 3 agree to a $99 concierge pilot or give a concrete paid-pilot commitment. Stop immediately if target users require the product to make insurance/legal adequacy decisions, or if they consistently name a current tool that already solves the project-gate workflow at an acceptable price.

# Supplement — Flat-Fee Dispute Evidence Workspace — 2026-07-30

**Contributor:** Cursor
**Market:** United States first; Indonesia relevance: secondary only for exporters/DTC brands selling into US card networks
**Verdict:** Watch

## Summary

Explore a Stripe-first, flat-fee **dispute evidence workspace** for US DTC and SaaS merchants who handle a modest dispute volume but lose winnable cases because shipping, support, and checkout evidence is scattered—and who dislike success-fee automation that auto-submits or takes ~25% of recovered amounts on high-AOV orders. The network rules and Stripe documentation make the workflow real and durable, but this is **not** promoted to Build today: Chargeflow, Justt, Stripe Smart Disputes / CE 3.0 tooling, and several open-source desks already occupy the space, vendor win-rate claims are not independently verified, and no primary interviews yet show willingness to pay a subscription instead of success-based recovery.

## Verified evidence

- **Google Trends screen, collected 2026-07-30:** the required [US, five-year Trends explore URL for `chargeback`](https://trends.google.com/trends/explore?date=today%205-y&geo=US&q=chargeback) was requested. This research environment received HTTP 429 from Google Trends and returned no exportable series, so this entry makes **no numeric interest or growth claim**. Re-check the live chart before treating search interest as evidence.
- **Stripe dispute likelihood and evidence practice, collected 2026-07-30:** Stripe’s dispute best-practices docs state fraudulent disputes account for over half of disputes; publish a Radar win-likelihood ranking where even the strongest (5-dot) band is only about a **60%** expected win rate when relevant evidence is submitted; and advise chronological, reason-specific, concise packets with hard file limits (combined ≤4.5MB / network page caps). [Source](https://docs.stripe.com/disputes/best-practices)
- **Visa Compelling Evidence 3.0 (CE 3.0), collected 2026-07-30:** Stripe documents CE 3.0 for eligible Visa fraud (reason 10.4) cases: two prior undisputed same-method transactions in a defined lookback window, with matching IP/device (and related) elements; Stripe can flag eligibility, pre-populate prior transactions, and submit via the enhanced evidence API, while still recommending a full standard evidence object as fallback. [CE 3.0 API](https://docs.stripe.com/disputes/api/visa-ce3) · [Checkout.com explainer](https://www.checkout.com/blog/visa-compelling-evidence-3-0)
- **Competitive intensity, collected 2026-07-30:** Chargeflow markets end-to-end automation with **25% of recovered chargebacks**, $29 per successful alert deflection, and Prevent scans after a free tier; Stripe Apps listing positions Chargeflow as filling gaps outside Stripe-native Smart Disputes. [Chargeflow pricing](https://www.chargeflow.io/pricing) · [Stripe Apps](https://marketplace.stripe.com/apps/chargeflow)
- **Workflow fragmentation (vendor-authored, corroborating), collected 2026-07-30:** US Tech Automations describes evidence living across Shopify, Stripe, carriers, email, and helpdesk under tight deadlines; Rebutto and similar vendors claim large win-rate gaps between unformatted and formatted submissions. Treat performance percentages as marketing unless independently verified. [US Tech Automations](https://ustechautomations.com/resources/blog/ecommerce-chargeback-dispute-shopify-stripe-recipe-2026) · [Rebutto stats page](https://www.rebutto.com/blog/chargeback-win-rate-statistics)
- **Technical feasibility / build-fit signals, collected 2026-07-30:** open-source projects show solo-developer-scale approaches—(1) [MerchantGuard/agentguard-cb](https://github.com/MerchantGuard/agentguard-cb) (MIT, 0★ as of fetch, CE 3.0 schema assembly with default `submit:false`), (2) [ZhenyuanPAN822/chargeback-evidence-desk](https://github.com/ZhenyuanPAN822/chargeback-evidence-desk) (MIT, 1★, local-first triage/checklists, no processor submission), (3) [alexander-zuev/riposte](https://github.com/alexander-zuev/riposte) (AGPL-3.0, 0★, Stripe dispute agent with optional human-in-the-loop). These are implementation precedents, not demand proof. Founder public repos previously noted in this day’s first entry remain data/agent-oriented; repository metadata alone does not establish payments-risk domain expertise.

## Inference

- Card-network rules (especially Visa CE 3.0) and Stripe’s own evidence schema create a durable compliance workflow, not a short-lived AI fad. The durable product is **evidence assembly + deadline triage + human-controlled submit**, not a chatbot wrapper.
- A credible wedge vs success-fee automation is: merchants with **low monthly dispute count and/or high AOV** who want flat pricing, packet review before submit, and reason-code checklists mapped to Stripe fields—without granting a third party auto-submit authority.
- One strong developer can ship an MVP as a multi-tenant web app: Stripe Connect/OAuth + webhooks, reason-code checklist, file vault with size/page validators, PDF/packet builder, optional Shopify/carrier CSV import, and `disputes.update` with `submit:false` then explicit human submit. AI should only draft narratives and map uploads to fields from structured inputs.
- Incumbent economics (0 monthly fee + 25% of wins) are hard to beat for low-AOV / high-volume shops. Without interview proof that the flat-fee + control segment will pay, Build is premature.

## Assumptions

- A meaningful segment of US Stripe/Shopify merchants rejects success fees or auto-submit on disputes above a few hundred dollars.
- Merchants will connect Stripe (and optionally Shopify/helpdesk) rather than only paste CSVs.
- $39–$79/month or ~$19 per dispute is credible vs eating losses or paying 25% recovery fees.
- Operating a dispute-evidence product does not require card-network TPA registration if the merchant remains the submitting party (must be confirmed with counsel; AgentGuard’s public legal posture is not advice).
- Indonesia is only a secondary market if sellers clear US card transactions; domestic Indo payment rails are out of scope for v1.

## Consulting assessment

### Trend durability

Dispute representment is a recurring cost of accepting cards. CE 3.0 is a network-rule evolution that raises the value of structured historical and device/IP evidence. Durability rests on payment-network process, not a search spike (Trends values unavailable here).

### Customer and JTBD

**Buyer:** founder/ops lead at a US DTC brand or B2B SaaS charging cards on Stripe (roughly mid–high AOV or subscription). **User:** whoever currently assembles dispute packets under deadline. **JTBD:** before the issuer deadline, assemble a reason-code-correct evidence packet from order, delivery, and support artifacts; review it; submit without losing the case to formatting or missing fields.

### Pain evidence

Stripe’s own docs show that even “best” predicted cases often lose (~40%+), that fraud is the majority category, and that reviewers punish irrelevant dumps. Vendor blogs consistently describe multi-system scavenger hunts. That supports process pain; it does **not** yet prove subscription WTP versus Chargeflow’s pay-on-win model. Interviews required.

### Alternatives

- Stripe Dashboard + Smart Disputes / Radar win scores / native CE 3.0 helpers.
- Success-fee automation: Chargeflow, Justt, and peers.
- Accounting/ops platforms that export order/tracking context (e.g., Webgility-style ops tools) without full representment.
- Manual Google Drive + spreadsheet checklists; local-first OSS desks.
- Doing nothing / accepting the dispute (still pays dispute fee; counts toward ratio).

Gap to test: **flat-fee, human-gated packet builder** for merchants who find success fees or black-box auto-submit unacceptable—not “can AI write a rebuttal.”

### Wedge

v1: Stripe-only workspace for **≤15 open disputes/month**, reason-code checklists, CE 3.0 eligibility surfacing when Stripe provides it, packet size linting to 4.5MB/page caps, and **review-then-submit**. Explicitly not prevention alerts, not Ethoca/Verifi, not “we guarantee wins.”

### MVP

- Stripe OAuth/webhook dispute inbox with deadline countdown and win-likelihood display when available.
- Per-reason evidence checklist mapped to Stripe evidence fields.
- Upload/combine PDFs/images with compression guidance; customer-communication concatenator.
- Optional CSV/manual attach for tracking numbers and helpdesk threads.
- Generate summary narrative + staged `submit:false` update; one-click submit after human confirm.
- Stripe Billing for flat monthly plan or per-dispute credits.

### AI role

AI drafts the short neutral narrative and suggests which uploaded files map to which Stripe fields / CE elements. It must not invent tracking, AVS, or prior-transaction matches. Durable value is schema mapping, deadlines, and auditability—not a generic “dispute GPT.”

### Pricing

**Hypothesis, not verified:** $49/month including 8 dispute packets, then $15/packet; or $99/month unlimited packets with fair-use cap. Concierge test: $79 for one live packet built with the merchant’s data in 48 hours.

### Distribution

Stripe App Marketplace listing, Indie Hackers / DTC Slack communities, and content SEO on “Stripe dispute evidence checklist” / CE 3.0 packet templates. Partner with Shopify fulfillment apps for delivery proof only after Stripe wedge works. Do not lead with Indonesia; optional later for Indo brands selling USD on Stripe.

### Risks

- **Market:** success-fee incumbents and Stripe-native tooling may be “good enough.”
- **Product:** without Shopify/carrier/helpdesk connectors, packet quality stays weak for INR (item not received) cases.
- **Technical:** webhook reliability, PII/security of dispute artifacts, file-size constraints.
- **Legal/network:** misrepresenting evidence, or operating in a way that triggers third-party agent obligations—require counsel.
- **Go-to-market:** dispute volume is lumpy; churn when a shop has a quiet quarter.

### Kill criteria

Stop after 15 qualified merchant interviews unless ≥6 describe ≥2 hours lost per dispute or a missed deadline in the last 90 days, and ≥3 prepay a $79 concierge packet or commit to $49/mo. Stop if targets consistently prefer Chargeflow-style success fees, or if Stripe-native CE 3.0 + Dashboard already closes their gap. Stop if counsel flags the submission model as requiring network registration the solo product cannot support.
