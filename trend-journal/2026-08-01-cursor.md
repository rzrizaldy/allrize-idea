# Title II Digital Asset Inventory & Exception Log — 2026-08-01

**Contributor:** Cursor
**File:** `trend-journal/2026-08-01-cursor.md`
**Market:** United States first; Indonesia relevance: not established
**Motion:** B2B — buyer is the ADA coordinator / IT director / clerk at a US state or local government entity (or their municipal IT contractor); residents with disabilities are beneficiaries, not payers
**Verdict:** Watch

## Summary

Explore a Stripe-billed web app that helps **small US public entities** (towns under 50k, special districts, small school districts) run the pre-deadline work the DOJ’s Title II web/mobile rule actually requires: **inventory digital assets** (sites, apps, PDFs, vendor portals), **classify the five narrow exceptions**, track **vendor VPAT / WCAG contract clauses**, and produce a council-ready progress log—without selling another generic WCAG “AI scanner.” The regulatory floor is durable (ADA Title II + WCAG 2.1 AA + April 2026 IFR deadlines of **2027-04-26** / **2028-04-26**), and the buyer universe is large (~90k local governments). It is **not** promoted to Build today: CivicPlus/AudioEye/Siteimprove/Acquia Web Governance already sell into municipal accessibility; consultancies give away Excel inventory trackers that occupy the exact wedge; government procurement and Stripe self-serve collide; and no primary interviews yet show willingness to pay for inventory SaaS vs free spreadsheets or bundled CMS accessibility.

## Inference

- Demand durability comes from **enforceable WCAG 2.1 AA under Title II**, not from a search spike. The April 2026 IFR slipped deadlines by a year, which *extends* the planning window for small entities rather than canceling the obligation—and ADA effective-communication duties still apply before the WCAG start dates.
- The durable product for under-resourced clerks is **asset inventory + exception classification + vendor evidence + progress reporting**, not an LLM that “auto-fixes” HTML. Overlay widgets and AI scanners are crowded, contested, and easy to reject under the scout’s anti-wrapper bar.
- A credible solo wedge vs CivicPlus would need self-serve onboarding for entities that will never buy a full municipal CMS suite: e.g. **≤5 domains, CSV/sitemap import, exception checklist mapped to DOJ categories, VPAT vault, PDF “active service vs archived” labeling, and a one-page PDF status report**—priced for p-card purchase. That differentiation is hypothesized, not verified.
- Government go-to-market is the binding constraint: fiscal-year budgets, procurement, and preference for invoice/PO over Stripe subscriptions make “simple Stripe monetizable web app” harder here than for private SMBs—even if the workflow is solo-buildable.
- Indonesia is not a secondary market for v1; Title II is US-specific. Any Indo accessibility angle would be a different statute (and buyer) set.

## Assumptions

- A meaningful share of sub-50k municipalities and special districts still track digital assets in email/spreadsheets and have not already bundled AudioEye/CivicPlus/Siteimprove.
- Those buyers will pay **$49–$149/month** (or a $499 one-time FY package) for structured inventory/exception logging instead of using free Excel trackers or waiting for a CMS vendor.
- An ADA coordinator or town clerk can purchase with a p-card / Stripe Checkout without a multi-month RFP for this budget size.
- Positioning as an **ops workspace** (not “guaranteed ADA compliance” or legal advice) is acceptable to counsel and to risk-averse public buyers.
- Optional light axe-core scans are a nice-to-have; the paid core remains inventory/exceptions/vendors. If buyers only want scans, the wedge collapses into Siteimprove/Monsido substitutes.

## Consulting assessment

### Trend durability

Statutory and regulatory, not fad-driven. Title II digital accessibility has always existed in principle; the 2024 rule made WCAG 2.1 AA the concrete standard, and the 2026 IFR only moved dates. Recurring work continues after deadlines (new PDFs, vendor swaps, content editors). Search Trends unavailable here; do not treat search as the durability proof.

### Customer and JTBD

**Motion:** B2B. **Buyer:** ADA coordinator, IT director, town clerk, or outsourced municipal webmaster who controls a small software budget. **User:** the same person plus department content owners who upload PDFs and maintain microsites. **JTBD:** before the entity’s 2027/2028 date (and under ongoing ADA duties), know every digital property and vendor portal that delivers public services, mark which items fall under a DOJ exception, assign remediation owners, and produce auditable progress evidence for leadership or counsel.

### Pain evidence

DOJ’s own small-entity guide foregrounds vendor responsibility and the difficulty of old PDFs. CivicPlus and TestPros content (vendor-authored) repeatedly cite limited staff, PDF/forms backlog, and third-party portals as failure modes; TestPros’ free Excel tracker is itself evidence that the inventory job is painful enough to productize. Missing: primary interviews and budget line-item proof from named small cities.

### Alternatives

- Full municipal platforms: CivicPlus + AudioEye + DocAccess + Acquia Web Governance.
- Enterprise scanners/governance: Siteimprove, Level Access, Deque axe Monitor, and peers.
- Free/cheap substitutes: TestPros Excel tracker; manual SharePoint lists; one-off consultant audits.
- DIY: axe-core / Pa11y in CI for the main site only (misses PDF/vendor inventory).
- Overlay/widget vendors (often criticized; not a substitute for WCAG conformance evidence).

Gap to test: **self-serve exception-aware inventory + VPAT vault** for entities that will not buy a CivicPlus stack—not “AI accessibility scanner #N.”

### Wedge

v1: public-entity workspace for **≤5 hostnames**, sitemap/CSV asset import, DOJ exception classifier (archived content, preexisting conventional docs, third-party unaffiliated posts, password-protected, individualized documents—mapped carefully to rule text), vendor record with VPAT/ACR upload and contract-clause checklist, remediation Kanban, and exportable status PDF. Explicitly **not** automated remediation, not overlay injection, not a claim of legal compliance.

### MVP

- Org profile: entity type, 2020 Census / SAIPE population band → deadline badge (2027 vs 2028).
- Asset registry: URL/app/PDF/vendor rows with owner, last reviewed, exception flag, evidence links.
- Exception wizard with plain-language questions tied to DOJ categories; force human confirmation.
- Vendor desk: VPAT upload, WCAG 2.1 AA clause template (non-advice), renewal date reminders.
- Optional: nightly axe-core sample scan of homepage + contact form only (signal, not certification).
- Stripe Checkout: monthly subscription or FY annual invoice-friendly annual plan.

### AI role

AI drafts plain-language exception rationales and summarizes uploaded VPATs into a checklist of gaps. It must not invent WCAG pass/fail results or auto-declare an entity “compliant.” Durable value is structured inventory, deadlines, and audit trail.

### Pricing

**Hypothesis, not verified:** $79/month (or $790/year) for one entity / 5 domains / unlimited PDF rows with fair-use storage; $29 one-week “inventory concierge” to seed the registry from a sitemap crawl. Kill if buyers refuse anything above free Excel.

### Distribution

SEO on “ADA Title II inventory checklist” / “special district WCAG deadline”; partnerships with state municipal leagues and ICMA/NAGW communities; outbound to clerks in under-50k Census places; optional white-label for small web agencies that already maintain town sites. Do not lead with Indonesia.

### Risks

- **Market:** CivicPlus bundles and free Excel trackers may be “good enough.”
- **Product:** without credible PDF/document workflow, the registry becomes a toy URL list.
- **Technical:** crawling government sites (auth walls, CAPTCHAs) is brittle; keep human CSV import first-class.
- **Legal:** marketing “ADA compliance” invites liability—ops-tool positioning required; counsel review before launch.
- **Go-to-market:** long sales cycles, fiscal calendars, and Stripe vs PO friction.
- **Deadline slip risk:** further federal delays could push urgency out again (already happened once in 2026).

### Kill criteria

Stop after 20 outreach conversations with ADA coordinators / town clerks / municipal web vendors unless ≥6 confirm they currently maintain a painful multi-sheet inventory or have no inventory, and ≥3 prepay a $29 concierge seed or commit to ≥$79/mo. Stop if targets already have CivicPlus/AudioEye/Siteimprove covering inventory reporting, or consistently prefer free Excel. Stop if procurement blocks card-not-present Stripe for this budget class in the first 10 buying attempts.
