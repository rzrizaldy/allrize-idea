## Summary

**Ride24 Packet** is a California-first desk that turns a reportable amusement-ride incident into a timed workflow: immediate Cal/OSHA ART phone notification checklist, scene-preservation log, photo/witness capture, and a Division-form written accident report within 24 hours—plus an exportable packet for the insurer and counsel. Today’s Google Trends spike (“six flags roller coaster brain surgery,” 20K+ / +1,000%) is event attention around X2 neurological-injury coverage and a pending wrongful-death trial, not proof of software demand. The legal clock is real and durable under California Labor Code §7914 and 8 CCR §344.15, but US FEC incumbents (Sumba incident reporting; MaintainIQ at $79/location/mo) already sell adjacent inspection + incident defensibility. Without prepaid interviews showing portable operators or small permanent parks still lose the 24-hour window on Drive/paper, this stays **Watch**—not Build, and not Reject.

## Inference

- The durable job is not “AI ride safety score.” It is **“when a guest loses consciousness or needs more than first aid, hit the ART phone clock, preserve the scene as instructed, assemble the Division’s written report in 24 hours, and keep an insurer-ready evidence trail.”**
- Six Flags-scale parks already have counsel and enterprise EHS; the only solo-SaaS-shaped buyer is a **small permanent park, municipal fair ride operator, or portable/carnival owner** still running paper + Drive when EMS arrives.
- Sumba’s incident module is the primary kill risk for FEC buyers. A surviving wedge is narrower: **CA ART-specific notification + 24h form assembly + preservation checklist for operators who will not buy a full FEC ops suite**—especially portable operators whose itinerary/permit paperwork is already mandated.
- MidwayCert/CarnyCert-style GitHub READMEs prove founder *interest* in carnival compliance narratives, not market pull or a shipping competitor.
- Indonesia theme-park / carnival regulation (e.g. BPOM-adjacent tourism attractions) is not evidenced here; treat any Indo expansion as speculative after a US CA wedge works.

## Assumptions

- A meaningful set of CA independent/portable operators has missed or nearly missed a §7914 phone or 24-hour written report in the last 24 months.
- Operators will pay for a Stripe-billed packet tool rather than counsel templates + binder checklists when incidents are rare (churn risk if billed monthly with zero events).
- Division-designated accident forms can be mapped into a guided web form without claiming to be an official electronic filing channel.
- Buyers accept that the product records decisions and evidence; it does not certify ride safety or replace a Qualified Safety Inspector.

## Consulting assessment

### Trend durability

Neurological-injury coverage of extreme coasters is episodic. **Amusement-ride accident notification and record retention are durable** under CA Labor Code / Title 8 and ASTM F770 practice. Retention for a SaaS must come from recurring daily/pre-open checks *or* from rare but high-stakes incident packets—ideally both. A product that only wakes up on viral news will churn.

### Customer and JTBD

- **Motion:** B2B.  
- **Who pays:** owner/GM of an independent CA permanent park, FEC with rides, or portable amusement company.  
- **Who uses:** ride lead, safety coordinator, or night manager on shift when EMS is called.  
- **JTBD:** Immediately after a reportable incident, complete ART phone notification, freeze/preserve as directed, capture ride ID / cycle / restraint / witness / medical-handoff evidence, and file the written Division report inside 24 hours with a shareable packet for insurance and counsel.

### Pain evidence

Observable urgency: closed high-profile ride, state investigation coverage, wrongful-death trial scheduling, and statutory “immediate” + “24 hours” language. Pain for *small* operators is inferred from the portable owner guide’s paper checklist culture; this scout found **no interview quotes or spend data** proving they will pay for a dedicated ART packet desk.

### Alternatives

| Alternative | Gap vs Ride24 |
| --- | --- |
| Paper + Cal/OSHA PDF forms + binder | Easy to miss clocks; hard to export a clean insurer packet under stress |
| Sumba (US FEC) | Already covers inspections + incidents + training; demo GTM may be “enough” for FECs |

### Wedge

v1 CA-only: **reportable incident → ART call script + timestamped preservation log + guided written-report fields + PDF packet**. Explicitly not a full CMMS, not predictive g-force AI, not a Six Flags replacement for Sumba-class daily ops.

### MVP

Stripe-monetizable web app a strong solo developer can ship:

1. Operator profile: rides register, ART office phone numbers, insurance contacts.  
2. Incident wizard mapped to §7914 triggers (LOC, >first-aid, major mechanical, fall).  
3. Call checklist with timestamps; preservation yes/no + Division instruction fields.  
4. Mobile photo/video upload, witness statement capture, staff PIN sign-off.  
5. Generate Division-style written report PDF + insurer packet zip within a 24h countdown.  
6. Optional: daily F770-style pre-open checklist only as a retention hook—not the v1 sale.

### AI role

AI drafts a neutral chronological narrative from structured fields and flags missing §7914 elements before export. It must **not** invent medical diagnoses, g-force claims, or “safe to reopen” advice. Value is deadline + checklist + audit trail—not a generic safety chatbot.

### Pricing

**Hypothesis (unverified):** $99 one-time “incident readiness setup” + $49/mo for log retention and drills; or $149 per live incident concierge packet in validation. Compare against MaintainIQ’s $79/mo facility checklist and Sumba’s opaque demo pricing. Prefer per-incident credits if monthly churn after quiet seasons is high.

### Distribution

- SEO/content on “California amusement ride accident report 24 hours” / ART notification checklist.  
- IAAPA / WWA / state fair association forums and carnival owner Facebook groups.  
- Partnerships with amusement-ride insurance brokers who already nag clients for documentation.  
- Do not lead with Indonesia.

### Risks

- **Market:** Sumba/MaintainIQ “good enough”; buyer count of CA portable operators is small.  
- **Product:** Official e-filing may never exist; PDF mimicry can drift from Division forms.  
- **Legal:** Product liability if users treat the app as safety certification; counsel review required.  
- **GTM:** Incident-driven acquisition is lumpy; news spikes do not convert operators.  
- **Technical:** Offline fairground capture and PII/medical-adjacent data handling.

### Kill criteria

Stop after 15 qualified CA operator interviews unless ≥5 describe a missed/near-miss ART phone or 24h written report (or insurer demand for a packet) in the last 24 months, and ≥3 prepay a $149 concierge packet or $49/mo. Stop if targets already use Sumba (or equivalent) for incidents, or if they insist counsel/paper is sufficient. Stop if counsel flags the product as practicing unauthorized safety certification or improperly intermediating Division filings.

## Verdict

**Watch.** Regulatory clocks and today’s attention spike make the problem legible, but US FEC SaaS already sells incident defensibility, explore Trends is unavailable here, and there is no prepaid willingness-to-pay evidence for a CA-only ART packet. Do **not** promote to `idea-grill/`. Revisit only after portable/small-park interviews beat Sumba + paper.
