## Summary

ColdClaim would be an incident-mode web app for a restaurant or small food retailer facing a prolonged power outage. It would timestamp the outage, guide staff through temperature checks and disposal decisions, capture photos and supplier invoices, calculate the documented inventory loss, and export a claim-ready evidence packet. The current Michigan outage demonstrates urgency, and insurance guidance confirms that documentation matters. But the app would be used rarely, coverage turns on policy-specific exclusions, and strong adjacent products already handle temperature histories, inventory, damage records, and claim-packet export. The remaining integration is too episodic and too easy to reproduce to justify a standalone SaaS.

## Inference

- A restaurant experiencing a long outage has a short window to preserve evidence before unsafe food must be discarded, so a guided incident flow is more useful than a generic inventory screen at that moment.
- The strongest value is a one-time recovery artifact rather than continuous monitoring; that weakens subscription retention and makes event-timed acquisition expensive.
- Restaurants already using Preloss, Jolt, MarketMan, or similar systems can assemble most of the proposed evidence without a new vendor.
- Coverage uncertainty cannot safely be resolved by software alone: whether off-premises utility failure, waiting periods, deductibles, spoilage, and business interruption apply depends on the policy and cause.

## Assumptions

- Independent restaurants lose enough perishable inventory in a covered outage to pay $49–$99 for a packet after considering their deductible.
- Owners can discover and trust a new tool during an active outage rather than calling their broker or insurer.
- Utilities or a licensed data provider can supply a location-specific, defensible outage timeline at solo-SaaS economics.
- Insurers accept an independently generated packet without requiring their own forms or adjuster workflow.

## Consulting assessment

### Trend durability

The DTE search spike is temporary and storm-driven. Grid outages and refrigeration failures recur, but any individual restaurant needs a claim workflow infrequently. The durable adjacent markets are prevention, temperature monitoring, inventory control, and broad property documentation—areas already served by established products.

### Customer and JTBD

**Motion:** B2B. An independent restaurant, grocer, caterer, or specialty-food shop pays. The owner or on-duty manager captures evidence; a broker or public adjuster may guide the submission; the carrier receives the packet; a health inspector may need the disposal record. JTBD: “Document what became unsafe, why, when, and what it cost before we dispose of it.”

### Pain evidence

FDA's four-hour threshold creates urgency. Insurance sources confirm that reimbursement depends on coverage and documentation, while operator discussion shows uncertainty about whether general contents coverage applies. The pain is credible, but no source demonstrates willingness to pay for standalone claim-packet software.

### Alternatives

- Preloss: pre-loss business inventory, damage documentation, and claim-packet export.
- Jolt and other IoT temperature systems: continuous temperature and power-loss evidence.
- MarketMan, MarginEdge, and POS/accounting systems: inventory valuation and supplier invoices.
- Insurer portals, broker assistance, spreadsheets, phone photos, and PDF/ZIP folders.
- PowerOutage.us and utility maps: outage timelines, subject to licensing and address-level precision.

### Wedge

The narrow wedge would be a mobile-first “outage mode” that starts immediately, applies FDA discard rules, captures evidence before disposal, imports recent invoices, and produces a carrier-neutral packet. It is clear and buildable, but Preloss already owns much of the claims artifact while operational platforms own better source data.

### MVP

A solo developer could ship a PWA with outage start/end timestamps, refrigerator/freezer temperature entries, photo capture, CSV or manual inventory lines, invoice upload, disposal attestations, a coverage checklist with explicit non-advice language, and PDF/ZIP export. Stripe could charge once per incident. The MVP should not interpret policies or promise claim approval.

### AI role

AI is unnecessary for the core workflow. Optional OCR could extract line items and prices from supplier invoices, with mandatory human confirmation. It should never decide whether food is safe, whether a loss is covered, or what the insurer owes.

### Pricing

Hypothesis only: $59 for one incident packet or $149/year for preparedness plus one incident. A broker-sponsored plan could cost $499/year for client access, but there is no evidence brokers want this. One-off pricing fits usage better than a monthly subscription and also exposes weak lifetime value.

### Distribution

Event-timed search ads for utility-outage queries would be costly and ethically awkward. A more repeatable channel would be independent insurance brokers serving restaurants, but they may prefer carrier tools and free checklists. Another path is integrations with temperature-monitoring vendors, which could add the feature themselves.

### Risks

- **Market:** rare use, low repeat purchase, deductibles that exceed smaller losses, and uncertain paid demand.
- **Product:** staff may lack pre-loss invoices, temperature logs, or a safe moment to document each item.
- **Technical:** fragmented utility feeds, commercial data licensing, address-level attribution, offline operation, and trustworthy timestamps.
- **Legal:** accidental insurance advice, unsafe food recommendations, privacy, evidence authenticity, and carrier-specific requirements.
- **Go-to-market:** acquisition occurs during emergencies; trusted brokers and incumbents have stronger distribution.

### Kill criteria

Reject permanently if fewer than three of 20 independent restaurants with spoilage or utility-interruption coverage prepay $59, if two insurance brokers say carrier-specific submissions make a neutral packet unusable, or if licensed address-level outage evidence costs more than 15% of expected revenue. Reconsider only if a broker or monitoring vendor brings at least 25 locations and pays for a pilot.
