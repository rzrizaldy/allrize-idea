# LiquidationClock: customs liquidation and protest-deadline monitoring for small US importers (2026-08-29)

**Contributor:** Claude (Cowork scheduled task)
**File:** `trend-journal/2026-08-29-claude.md`
**Market:** United States first. The entire mechanism is US statutory customs procedure (19 U.S.C. 1514, 19 CFR 159) plus US federal trade litigation. Indonesia relevance: not established, and the mechanism does not transfer.
**Motion:** B2B. The importer of record pays, out of a finance or trade-compliance budget. A controller, a one-person compliance manager, or the owner uses it. Customs brokers, trade attorneys, and CPA firms are the plausible acquisition intermediaries. None of those channels are validated.
**Verdict:** **Watch**

---

## Summary

Three separate US tariff regimes were struck down, expired, or entered litigation between February and August 2026. Each collapse turned billions of dollars of already-paid duty into a refund that importers must affirmatively claim, on a per-entry deadline that is jurisdictional and cannot be extended. That created a large, immediate, money-attached job: know when each of your customs entries liquidates, and file into the right channel before the clock runs out.

I researched building a solo-SaaS around that job. The underlying driver is more durable than I expected going in, because the refund cycle has now repeated three times in seven months rather than happening once. But the specific product shape is already occupied. In roughly six months a crowd of specialist vendors, freight forwarders, law firms, and accounting firms has taken the ground, including free tools that do the sizing step and a free government channel that does the filing step. Every one of them, including the best-resourced, is blocked by the same wall: there is no third-party API into CBP's ACE system, so the importer must export their own entry data by hand before any software can help.

The residual opportunity is narrower than the one I set out to test. It is not refund recovery. It is always-on liquidation-date alerting priced as cheap infrastructure rather than as a recovery service. That residual is real but unproven, and one factual question that decides whether it is buildable at all could not be answered in this run. Watch, not Build, and not Reject.

## 1. Trend durability

**Verified evidence.** On 20 February 2026 the Supreme Court ruled 6-3 in *Learning Resources, Inc. v. Trump* that IEEPA does not authorize the President to impose tariffs, invalidating the IEEPA tariff regime. CBP halted collection for entries on or after 24 February 2026. Source: [Sheppard Mullin, "From CAPE to Cope?"](https://www.sheppard.com/insights/blogs/from-cape-to-cope-cbp-reverses-position-on-universal-ieepa-duty-refunds) and [Gateway Lines, CAPE portal guide](https://gatewaylines.com/press-releases/ieepa-tariff-refunds-complete-2026-guide-cape-portal).

**Verified evidence.** The administration replaced the lost revenue with a Section 122 temporary surcharge of 10 percent effective 24 February 2026. Section 122 caps duties at 15 percent for no more than 150 days, so it expired by operation of law at 12:01 a.m. on 24 July 2026 without congressional extension. Sources: [Gateway Lines](https://gatewaylines.com/press-releases/ieepa-tariff-refunds-complete-2026-guide-cape-portal), [Honigman alert](https://www.honigman.com/alert-3462), [Holland & Knight, "And the (Tariff) Beat Goes On"](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2026/07/and-the-tariff-beat-goes-on).

**Verified evidence.** A new Section 301 tariff layer, grounded in USTR forced-labor investigations, took effect at 12:01 a.m. on 24 July 2026, the same minute Section 122 lapsed, applying additional duties of 10 or 12.5 percent to most imports from roughly 60 economies. Unlike Section 122 it carries no statutory sunset. Sources: [Honigman](https://www.honigman.com/alert-3462), [Holland & Knight](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2026/07/and-the-tariff-beat-goes-on), [Sheppard Mullin, "Section 301'ing the World"](https://www.sheppard.com/insights/blogs/section-301ing-the-world-or-99-4-of-it).

**Verified evidence (reported).** The Court of International Trade separately struck down the Section 122 tariffs, per [PwC Canada's tax insight](https://www.pwc.com/ca/en/services/tax/publications/tax-insights/us-court-strike-down-section-122-tariffs-2026.html). On 3 August 2026, 25 states sued at the CIT to block the new Section 301 forced-labor tariff and to recover duties already paid under it. I have this from search-result summaries of law firm coverage and did not open the underlying complaint or docket.

**Inference (from the four items above).** This is not a spike. In seven months the United States produced three distinct retroactive-refund events: IEEPA struck down, Section 122 lapsed and separately struck down, and the replacement Section 301 layer sued within eleven days of taking effect. The pattern that matters for a product is not "there was a big refund in 2026." It is that duty paid today has a materially non-trivial probability of becoming refundable later, and that recovering it depends on per-entry deadline discipline. That is a structural change in what a US importer's finance function has to do.

**Inference.** The counter-argument is real and I want to state it rather than bury it. The refund events are driven by an unusually aggressive tariff program meeting an unusually active court docket. A change of administration, a legislative settlement, or a Federal Circuit ruling that stabilizes the field could end the cycle. Durability here is a bet on continued legal churn, not on a demographic or technological shift. I would put that at durable through at least 2027 and genuinely uncertain after that.

**Assumption.** I did not verify current search interest for terms like "tariff refund" or "customs protest deadline" because Google Trends was unreachable. My proxy is that specialist vendors and national law firms were publishing dense long-tail content on these exact phrases in July and August 2026, which is behavior consistent with rising, commercially valuable search demand. That is a proxy, not a measurement.

## 2. Customer and job to be done

**Inference (from the eligibility mechanics described in the verified sources).** The customer is a US importer of record with enough entries that deadline tracking cannot live in one person's head, and not enough scale to employ a trade-compliance team. Practically that is a company filing somewhere between roughly 50 and a few thousand entry lines a year: a mid-size e-commerce brand, a specialty distributor, a small manufacturer buying components abroad. Below that range a spreadsheet genuinely suffices. Above it, the company already has enterprise trade software.

The job they are hiring the tool for, stated the way the buyer would state it: *tell me, without me having to think about it, which of my entries just started a clock, how many days are left, and which door is still open.*

**Verified evidence.** The job decomposes into a small number of mechanical steps that are individually simple and collectively unforgiving:

- Liquidation is CBP's final computation of duty on an entry. Until it liquidates, duty is not final.
- Under [19 U.S.C. 1514(c)(3)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/19/1514), a protest must be filed within 180 days after the date of liquidation.
- Under [19 CFR 159.9](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/19/159.9), the notice of liquidation is dated with the date it is posted electronically on cbp.gov, and that electronic posting is deemed the legal evidence of liquidation. The courtesy notice a broker forwards is expressly informal and does not set the date.
- Under [19 CFR 159.11](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/19/159.11), an entry not liquidated within one year of entry is deemed liquidated by operation of law, at the numbers the importer asserted. [19 CFR 159.12](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/19/159.12) allows extensions in increments of not more than one year, to a four-year outer cap.

Source for the assembly of these into a single workflow: [GingerControl, "How to Check Entry Liquidation Status in ACE"](https://gingercontrol.com/blog/how-to-check-entry-liquidation-status), 20 July 2026.

**Inference.** Two of those four rules are traps rather than tasks. An importer who calendars from the broker's courtesy email is calendaring from the wrong event. An importer who reads "unliquidated" as "no clock" misses deemed liquidation. Both traps produce silent, total, unrecoverable loss rather than a visible error. That is the profile of a problem software is good at and humans are bad at.

## 3. Pain evidence

**Verified evidence.** CBP deployed the CAPE refund tool inside ACE on 20 April 2026 (CSMS #68340863). CAPE accepts unliquidated entries and entries within approximately 80 days of liquidation, and rejects entries more than 80 days past liquidation. The 80-day CAPE window and the 180-day protest window run independently, so a single entry can fall out of CAPE while still being protestable. Source: [GingerControl](https://gingercontrol.com/blog/how-to-check-entry-liquidation-status), citing [CBP CSMS #68340863](https://content.govdelivery.com/accounts/USDHSCBP/bulletins/412cc7f).

**Verified evidence.** On 29 May 2026 CBP filed a motion asserting, for the first time since the Supreme Court ruling, that it lacks legal authority to refund finally liquidated entries without an importer-specific court judgment, stating that "Once an entry is finally liquidated, CBP has no authority to reliquidate or refund money without a court order." Source: [Sheppard Mullin](https://www.sheppard.com/insights/blogs/from-cape-to-cope-cbp-reverses-position-on-universal-ieepa-duty-refunds), quoting the government's filing in *V.O.S. Selections, Inc. v. United States*, Ct. No. 25-00066, ECF No. 88.

**Verified evidence.** The same source records the CIT lead judge issuing an order to show cause on 27 May 2026 and directing the CBP Commissioner to appear in person, and the CIT denying CBP's motion to substitute witnesses. The government subsequently appealed to the Federal Circuit. Follow-on coverage: [Holland & Knight, government appeals](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2026/06/ieepa-tariff-refund-update-government-appeals), [Morgan Lewis](https://www.morganlewis.com/pubs/2026/06/tariff-refund-battle-continues-government-appeals-order).

**Verified evidence (reported).** On 17 July 2026 the CIT ordered CBP to begin processing Phase 3 refunds for finally liquidated entries, but only for importers that had filed their own civil suits. Importers that did not sue remain dependent on future litigation or the appeal outcome. Source: [Thompson Hine SmarTrade](https://www.thompsonhinesmartrade.com/2026/07/cit-orders-cbp-to-process-ieepa-tariff-refunds-for-phase-3-finally-liquidated-entries/), also [Amundsen Davis](https://www.amundsendavislaw.com/alert-ieepa-tariff-refunds-cape-phase-iii), [Green Worldwide](https://www.greenworldwide.com/cape-phase-3-for-ieepa-tariff-refunds-on-track-for-end-of-july-as-federal-circuit-appeal-continues/).

**Reported, not verified.** Figures I encountered in search-result summaries of pages I did not open, and which I am recording as claims rather than facts: approximately 330,000 importers paid an estimated 166 billion dollars in IEEPA duties across more than 53 million entries; CBP reported refunding approximately 85 billion dollars as of 27 May 2026; the government estimated more than 30 billion dollars of exposure in the contested finally-liquidated category; and roughly 39 billion dollars sits in entries no framework can reach because the importer missed the 180-day protest window or a protective court filing. The last figure appeared in vendor marketing content and should be treated with the most suspicion of the four.

**Inference.** The strongest pain signal in this research is not a quoted complaint. It is the shape of the market's own content. A vendor writing a how-to guide chose to open it with the scenario of a compliance person being told by their CFO to find out "whether each entry has liquidated and when," logging into ACE, and "staring at a wall of ES-00x reports with no obvious liquidation status answer." Vendors write that opening because it converts, which means it describes something buyers recognize.

**Assumption / gap.** I did not find first-person importer complaints in forums such as Reddit, and my searches surfaced vendor and law firm content almost exclusively. I do not know whether that is because importers complain in closed channels such as ICPA and AAEI member forums, or because search results for these commercial terms are simply saturated by vendors. Either way, this entry lacks the direct buyer-voice evidence the repo standard asks for, and that absence is part of why the verdict is Watch.

## 4. Alternatives

**Verified evidence: the free government path.** CAPE is CBP's own tool inside the ACE portal and costs nothing. Any importer of record can apply for an [ACE Secure Data Portal account](https://www.cbp.gov/trade/automated/how-to-use-ace/portal-applying), run [ACE Reports](https://www.cbp.gov/trade/automated/ace-reports), and file. Per GingerControl's own guide, pulling the ES-003 report with the Liquidation Status and Liquidation Date columns added is free and "something you should do yourself" for a small book of entries.

**Verified evidence: the legal notice source is free and public.** The [Official Notice of Extension, Suspension and Liquidation bulletin](https://trade.cbp.dhs.gov/ace/liquidation/LBNotice/) is updated daily, is searchable by filer code or by date, and retains postings for 15 months. I could not load this page directly, so the description of its search fields is GingerControl's, not mine.

**Verified evidence: a direct competitor already owns the exact wedge.** GingerControl (Flowyth Co, Austin TX) sells an IEEPA Refund Recovery service that "buckets entries by liquidation status, tracks the deadline math per entry," plus a protest deadline calculator, a policy-change briefing digest, and industry landing pages. Its content is co-signed by a licensed customs broker with 42 years of compliance experience, and it lists ICPA, AAEI, and HCBFFA as partners. It publishes a comparison page naming [Pax, Trava, and Caspian](https://gingercontrol.com/compare/pax-vs-trava-vs-caspian-vs-gingercontrol) and another naming Gaia Dynamics as competitors.

**Verified evidence: free sizing tools from freight forwarders.** Gateway Lines offers a no-signup [IEEPA refund calculator](https://gatewaylines.com/press-releases/ieepa-tariff-refunds-complete-2026-guide-cape-portal) that scans entry-summary data, covers the full HTS, and distinguishes refundable IEEPA codes from non-refundable Section 301, 232, and 122 codes. A forwarder can give this away because the refund is a hook for the freight relationship.

**Verified evidence: additional specialist vendors visible in one search pass.** [Tariff Spot](https://www.tariffspot.com/blog/180-day-protest-deadline-liquidation-finality), [TariffRefundIQ](https://www.tariffrefundiq.com/refund-tracker), [TariffsTool](https://www.tariffstool.com/tariff-refund-tracker), [TariffRefund.com](https://tariffrefund.com/), [Tariff Refund HQ](https://www.tariffrefundhq.com/), [Gaia Dynamics](https://www.gaiadynamics.ai/blog/why-many-importers-miss-tariff-rebate-checks-and-what-options-are-available), and [Peacock Tariff Consulting](https://www.peacocktariffconsulting.com/how-to-file-cbp-protest-guide/).

**Verified evidence: professional services.** Trade attorneys and accounting firms are selling into the same buyer, including [Ayar Law](https://ayarlaw.com/tariff-refund-attorney), [Phillips Law Group](https://phillipslaw.com/tariff-refund/), and [CLA](https://www.claconnect.com/en/resources/articles/26/tariff-refunds), alongside alerts from Sheppard Mullin, Holland & Knight, Morgan Lewis, Skadden, Foley, Thompson Hine, Benesch, Amundsen Davis, and Forvis Mazars.

**Reported pricing, not independently verified.** Per [FindCustomsBroker](https://findcustomsbroker.com/blog/ieepa-cape-duty-refund-costs-what-importers-need-to-know-in-2026), flat-fee refund filing is often around 200 dollars for the first entry plus 125 dollars per additional entry for recoveries under 5,000 dollars; CIT litigation contingency proposals run 15 to 25 percent; and international trade firms bill 500 to 1,500 dollars per hour. The same source notes that for importers with mostly unliquidated or recently liquidated entries, the cost is close to nothing beyond data preparation because the existing broker plus CAPE is the right path.

**Verified evidence: open source.** GitHub carries a [trade-compliance topic](https://github.com/topics/trade-compliance) and an [hs-code topic](https://github.com/topics/hs-code), and at least one active academic project, [Analytics-Everywhere-Lab/hts](https://github.com/Analytics-Everywhere-Lab/hts), a multi-agent pipeline predicting Canadian HTS codes from plain-text descriptions. I found no open-source project that ingests ACE entry data and computes liquidation and protest deadlines. The open-source gravity in this space sits on classification, not on deadline management.

**Inference.** The competitive picture is worse than "crowded." It is crowded at every price point simultaneously: free from the government, free from forwarders as a lead magnet, low flat fee from filing services, subscription from specialist software, and hourly or contingency from lawyers. A new entrant has to be better than free at the bottom and cheaper than a lawyer at the top, in a category where the buyer's default instinct is to ask their existing customs broker.

## 5. Wedge

**Inference.** The one thing no vendor in this research claims to do is watch the clock for you continuously. GingerControl states its own limits plainly: the service "works from the ES-003 export you provide," and "does not connect to or pull from ACE for you," is not a licensed customs broker, and does not file underlying entries. Every competitor I saw operates on the same model, a point-in-time engagement around a data file the customer produces by hand.

That leaves a narrow shape: a low-priced, always-on monitor rather than a recovery engagement. The importer connects nothing and hires nobody. They drop in an ES-003 export, and the product thereafter watches the public liquidation bulletin daily, matches new postings to their entries, and emails them when a clock starts and again as it runs down, with the CAPE and protest lanes computed separately.

**Inference.** The positioning that follows is "smoke detector," not "recovery firm." The recovery firms are paid when money is already at risk. A monitor is paid to make sure the money never reaches that state. Those are different budgets and different sales conversations, and the second one survives after the current refund wave clears because liquidation and protest clocks are permanent features of US customs law, not features of the IEEPA episode.

**Open question that decides the wedge.** The CBP bulletin is described as searchable by filer code or by date. The filer code belongs to the broker, not to the importer. If the bulletin does not expose the importer of record, an automated monitor cannot isolate one customer's entries from the daily feed without the customer supplying their entry numbers first, which weakens but does not destroy the design. I could not load the bulletin to check, so I am recording this as unresolved rather than guessing. **This is the single fact that should be verified before any further work on this idea.**

## 6. MVP

**Inference.** Smallest shippable scope, assuming the bulletin question resolves favorably:

1. Upload an ES-003 CSV export. Parse entry number, entry date, liquidation status, liquidation date, HTS lines, and duty paid.
2. Bucket every line into the four lanes the law creates: unliquidated, within the CAPE window, past CAPE but inside 180 days, and past 180 days.
3. Compute a deemed-liquidation warning for unliquidated lines approaching one year from entry date.
4. Poll the public liquidation bulletin daily, match new postings against stored entries, and set the legal liquidation date from the posting rather than from any courtesy notice.
5. Email digest: what started a clock this week, what expires in the next 30 days, what expired.
6. Export a deadline-bucketed worksheet the customer can hand to their broker or counsel.

Deliberately excluded from v1: filing anything, giving a recommendation on which channel to use, classifying goods, and estimating refund amounts. Each of those crosses into either regulated activity or a crowded feature set.

**Inference.** Build effort for one strong developer: the parsing, bucketing, and date arithmetic are a few weeks. The bulletin scraper plus matching logic, kept correct as CBP changes the page, is the ongoing cost and the real work. Calling it four to six weeks to a paid pilot is plausible. Calling it a weekend is not.

**Assumption.** That an ES-003 export has a stable enough column structure to parse reliably across accounts. GingerControl's guide notes that the liquidation columns are off by default and must be added from a column picker, which implies user-configurable exports and therefore variable files. Unverified.

## 7. AI role

**Inference.** AI adds little to the core of this product, and I want to say that plainly rather than manufacture a use for it. The valuable operations are date arithmetic against statutory windows and exact matching of entry numbers. Those must be deterministic and auditable. A model that is approximately right about a jurisdictional deadline is worse than no product, because it produces confident wrong answers about money that cannot be recovered after day 181.

**Inference.** Two defensible narrow uses. First, parsing heterogeneous uploads: normalizing an ES-003 export whose columns the user chose, or reading a scanned CBP Form 7501, is genuine document-extraction work where a model beats brittle rules, provided every extracted value is shown to the user for confirmation. Second, summarizing policy movement into plain language for the weekly digest, which is a content feature rather than a decision feature. Neither is a moat. Competitors already market themselves as AI trade-compliance platforms, so AI is table stakes in the category's positioning and is not a differentiator.

## 8. Pricing

**Inference, anchored to the alternatives in section 4.** The anchors are: free (CAPE and self-service ACE), roughly 200 dollars plus 125 dollars per additional entry for flat-fee filing, 15 to 25 percent contingency for litigation, and 500 to 1,500 dollars per hour for counsel.

A monitor should price well below the filing services, because it does less and because its value is insurance rather than recovery:

- Solo: 39 dollars per month, up to 250 entry lines tracked.
- Growing: 99 dollars per month, up to 2,000 entry lines, weekly digest, multi-user.
- Portfolio: 249 dollars per month, unlimited lines, multiple importer-of-record numbers, broker-shared access.

Stripe subscriptions, no contingency, no percentage of recovery. Taking a percentage would reposition the product as a recovery service and put it in direct competition with law firms, which is a fight a solo developer loses.

**Inference.** The pricing logic to lead with is that a single missed protest window on a mid-size entry exceeds a year of subscription by an order of magnitude. That framing is honest and checkable by the buyer against their own duty numbers, which matters in a category full of inflated refund promises.

**Assumption.** That importers in the 50 to 2,000 entry range will pay a recurring subscription for monitoring at all, rather than treating deadline tracking as something their broker should already be doing for free. This is the weakest link in the whole model and is unvalidated.

## 9. Distribution

**Inference.** The realistic first-100 path, in order of my confidence:

1. **Customs brokers as a channel, not a competitor.** Small and mid-size brokers are the natural distributor: they already hold the relationship, they already send courtesy notices, and a monitor makes their courtesy notice less dangerous. A white-label or referral arrangement is plausible. This is also the highest-risk channel, because brokers may see it as commoditizing something they consider their own service.
2. **Trade compliance associations.** GingerControl's partner list points at where the buyers gather: [ICPA](https://www.icpainc.org/), [AAEI](https://aaei.org/), and regional broker and forwarder associations. Association membership is a paid, self-identified list of exactly the right people.
3. **Trade attorneys and CPA firms.** They currently do this tracking by hand for clients whose entries do not justify an engagement. A tool they can hand a client is a referral, not a threat.
4. **Long-tail content.** Honest assessment: this channel is closed. The specific queries are already saturated by well-executed content from vendors and national law firms as of July and August 2026. Entering on SEO now means competing against a page reviewed by a 42-year licensed customs broker with association partnerships behind it.

**Assumption.** None of these channels is validated. I have no evidence any broker would distribute a third-party monitor, and a plausible reading is that they would not.

## 10. Risks

**Verified evidence, risk 1: no third-party data access.** There is no path for a third party to pull an importer's entry data from ACE. Every product in this category depends on the customer manually exporting a report. That friction sits between the customer and every activation, and it recurs whenever the data must be refreshed. This is a structural ceiling on the whole category, not a competitive disadvantage unique to a new entrant.

**Inference, risk 2: regulated activity.** Transacting customs business for others is restricted to licensed customs brokers under 19 CFR part 111. A product that merely reports dates and statuses should sit outside that line, but a product that recommends "protest this one" is closer to it, and closer still to legal advice. Note that the leading competitor states explicitly that it is not a licensed customs broker and does not file entries, which suggests the operators closest to this market treat the boundary as real. I have not read part 111 directly in this run, so treat the specific boundary placement as unverified reasoning rather than a legal conclusion.

**Inference, risk 3: the driver could stop.** The refund cycle depends on continued litigation against continued aggressive tariff action. A Federal Circuit ruling, a legislative settlement, or a policy change could end it. The permanent floor is ordinary liquidation and protest discipline, which is a much smaller business than the current wave suggests.

**Inference, risk 4: the incumbent path is free and adjacent.** The buyer's first instinct is to ask their broker, and the broker already sends a notice. Convincing an importer that the notice they already receive is legally the wrong date is a real education burden on every single sale.

**Inference, risk 5: liability asymmetry.** If the product misses a posting or mis-parses a date and a customer loses a six-figure protest window, the claimed damage far exceeds any plausible subscription revenue. A 39-dollar-per-month product carrying that tail is badly shaped. Disclaimers help and do not fully solve it.

**Inference, risk 6: late entry.** The wave began in February 2026. Competitors have had six months of content, partnerships, and customer relationships. Arriving in September 2026 means arriving after the peak of the IEEPA cycle.

## 11. Kill criteria

Falsifiable, with thresholds, in the order they should be tested:

1. **Bulletin identifiability.** If the CBP electronic liquidation bulletin does not permit isolating a specific importer's entries by any field an importer reliably knows, and matching therefore requires the customer to supply every entry number in advance, kill the always-on monitoring premise. Test cost: one hour of reading the bulletin. **Do this first.**
2. **Broker channel.** Contact 15 small and mid-size customs brokers. If fewer than 3 express interest in referring or white-labeling a monitor, the primary distribution channel is dead.
3. **Willingness to pay.** Offer 20 importers a manual, human-performed version of the monitor for 99 dollars per month, delivered by hand. If fewer than 5 pay for a second month, the subscription premise is dead. This is the decisive test and it requires no code.
4. **Displacement.** If in interviews more than half of importers say their broker already alerts them on posted liquidation dates and they trust it, the product is solving a problem the channel already covers.
5. **Data friction.** If more than half of pilot users fail to produce a usable ES-003 export within one week of signup without hand-holding, activation cost exceeds the price point and the model does not work at 39 to 249 dollars per month.
6. **Driver decay.** If the Federal Circuit or Congress resolves the refund litigation such that no new retroactive refund event occurs for four consecutive quarters, revisit whether the permanent floor supports a business.

## 12. Verdict: Watch

**Reasoning.**

Not **Build**, for three reasons. The wedge is occupied by at least seven specialist vendors, a set of freight forwarders giving the sizing step away free, and a national law firm bench, all of whom arrived six months earlier. The one differentiating idea I could construct, continuous monitoring rather than point-in-time recovery, rests on an unverified fact about a government web page I could not load. And the willingness-to-pay assumption at the heart of the model, that importers will subscribe to monitoring rather than expect it from their broker, has no supporting evidence in this research.

Not **Reject**, for two reasons. First, the driver turned out to be more durable than the framing "one-time refund wave" implies. Three retroactive-refund events in seven months, with the newest tariff layer sued eleven days after taking effect, is a pattern rather than an episode. Second, and more importantly, the deadline itself is permanent statutory plumbing. The 180-day protest clock, the electronic-posting rule, and deemed liquidation at one year exist independently of any tariff program, and the current wave has simply made them visible to a buyer population that previously ignored them. A product built on permanent plumbing and introduced by a temporary wave is a better shape than a product built on the wave.

**Watch** means: do not write code. Answer kill criterion 1 with an hour of reading, then run kill criterion 3 by hand with real importers. If both clear, this returns as a Build candidate with the wedge narrowed to monitoring and the price anchored below the filing services. If either fails, it is a Reject and should be recorded as one.

**What I would want before promoting this.** Direct buyer-voice evidence, which this entry lacks. Every pain signal here is refracted through vendors and law firms who profit from the pain existing. That is a real weakness in the evidence base and it is why this entry does not go to the idea grill.
