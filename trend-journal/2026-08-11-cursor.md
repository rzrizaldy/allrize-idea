# FccIdGate — 2026-08-11

**Contributor:** Cursor

**Verdict:** Watch

## Summary

FccIdGate is a Stripe-billed web app that helps mid-market US online marketplaces comply with the FCC’s newly adopted § 2.803(c) duty to display and verify FCC IDs at the online point of sale for certified radiofrequency devices. Sellers enter an FCC ID per SKU; the product checks validity against the FCC OET Equipment Authorization System, stores a seller accuracy attestation, produces a storefront display payload, and keeps an audit log of checks. Category-1 marketplaces that take title or physical custody also get a human review queue for “valid and accurate” matching—the harder job the FCC itself says EAS cannot automate at scale. The July 22, 2026 Third Report and Order is a durable process mandate, not a fad, and no dedicated SMB SaaS for this exact wedge was found. The correct verdict is still **Watch**: the Federal Register has published the accompanying Further Notice as a proposed rule, but a final-rule FR publication that starts the 180/270-day clocks was not found; Amazon already collects FCC IDs; free format checkers and the public EAS API lower willingness to pay; and prepaid demand from mid-market multi-vendor operators is unverified.

### FCC Third Report and Order (primary)

- On **July 22, 2026**, the FCC adopted, and on **July 23, 2026** released, **FCC 26-50** (ET Docket No. 21-232): *Third Report and Order and Third Further Notice of Proposed Rulemaking — Protecting Against National Security Threats to the Communications Supply Chain Through the Equipment Authorization Program*. Full text: [FCC-26-50A1.pdf](https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/FCC-26-50A1.pdf), collected **2026-08-11**.
- The Order clarifies that **online marketplaces** that list, distribute, or offer regulated equipment—including third-party listings when combined with consignment, warehousing, inventory, order processing, labeling, packaging, billing, or similar fulfillment—are engaged in “marketing” under **47 CFR § 2.803(a)**. It adopts new **§ 2.803(c)** requiring display of the **FCC ID at the online point of sale** for certified devices, with limited exceptions. Same PDF, §§ III–marketing / § 2.803(c) discussion, collected **2026-08-11**.
- **Two-tier liability (paraphrase of Order + Cooley summary):**  
  - **§ 2.803(c)(1) / Category 1:** marketplaces that sell the device themselves, take title, or have physical access (warehousing, fulfillment, consignment, shipping) must display an FCC ID that is **valid and accurate** for the listed product. Compliance effective **180 days after Federal Register publication of the Order**.  
  - **§ 2.803(c)(2) / Category 2:** marketplaces without title/physical access must display a **valid** FCC ID, take reasonable steps to verify validity in EAS, and obtain seller certification of accuracy. Compliance effective **270 days after FR publication**.  
  Primary: [FCC-26-50A1.pdf](https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/FCC-26-50A1.pdf) (effective-date paragraphs for § 2.803(c)(1)/(c)(2)). Counsel summary: [Cooley alert, 2026-07-29](https://www.cooley.com/news/insight/2026/2026-07-29-fcc-expands-restrictions-on-covered-list-equipment-and-supply-chains). Collected **2026-08-11**.
- **Exemptions verified in the Order:** existing listings grandfathered until amended/updated/republished (non-substantive price/layout/translation changes do not count); **used devices** exempt; **small sellers** below the INFORM Consumers Act “high-volume third-party seller” threshold (more than **200 sales / $5,000** gross revenue in a continuous 12-month period across 24 months—per INFORM Act definition the Order incorporates) exempt for those third-party listings; SDoC information need not be displayed under the adopted display rule. Same PDF + [Dickinson Wright / Mondaq client alert](https://www.mondaq.com/unitedstates/telecoms-mobile-cable-communications/1825410/the-fcc-looks-inside-the-box-new-rules-for-components-and-online-marketplaces), collected **2026-08-11**.
- The Order acknowledges **EAS tooling limits**: commenters (including CCIA) argued a marketplace can confirm an FCC ID **exists** but cannot reliably confirm at catalog scale that the ID **corresponds to the product listed**; EAS lacks bulk-access, reverse-lookup, and change-notification capabilities. The Commission notes the public **getFCCIDList** API exists for validity checks, directs OET to improve marketplace API access, and states it is “unlikely to pursue enforcement” against marketplaces that undertake **reasonable due diligence**. Same PDF ¶¶ on EAS / API (including example `https://apps.fcc.gov/OETLabServices/getFCCIDList?fccId=QIS`), collected **2026-08-11**.
- The Order estimates industry-wide costs on the order of **≤ ~$300M one-time** and **< ~$40M recurring annual** for the adopted package (component + marketplace measures). This is an FCC Regulatory Flexibility / cost estimate, not SaaS willingness-to-pay. Same PDF, costs section, collected **2026-08-11**.

## Inference

- The demand driver is a **durable federal marketing rule** tied to national-security equipment authorization, not today’s meat-recall or flight-delay search spikes. Spikes were only discovery noise.
- The defendable wedge is **not** “another FCC ID lookup.” It is an **audit-ready listing gate** for mid-market multi-vendor operators who lack Amazon-scale compliance engineering: validity check → seller attestation → storefront display field → retention of who certified what when → Category-1 human review when title/custody applies.
- Big platforms (Amazon, eBay, Walmart, large Mirakl tenants) will build or buy enterprise connectors. Solo-SaaS economics only work if enough **smaller multi-vendor electronics marketplaces** (vertical B2B catalogs, regional marketplaces, Shopify Marketplace Kit builds, specialty RF gear platforms) need a $49–$199/mo tool before counsel invoices accumulate.
- Category-2 validity+attestation is technically easy and therefore easy to commoditize (or ship free inside Mirakl/Shopify). Category-1 **accuracy** matching is the hard job and the Order itself says EAS cannot do it scalably—so any Build claim must either (a) sell human-in-the-loop review workflows priced like light compliance ops software, or (b) wait for better FCC reverse-lookup tooling that may erase the wedge.
- Indonesia is only a secondary angle for manufacturers exporting Wi-Fi/Bluetooth modules into US marketplaces; v1 stays US marketplace operators.

## Assumptions

- A countable set of US mid-market multi-vendor marketplaces sell enough certified RF SKUs that § 2.803(c) will apply when the FR Rule clock starts.
- Those operators will pay a subscription rather than a one-week contractor script against `getFCCIDList`.
- Sellers will complete attestation flows without killing conversion.
- Stripe self-serve billing works for these buyers (no heavy PO-only procurement).
- The eventual FR Rule publication will preserve the 180/270-day structure described in the adopted Order.

### Wedge

**First wedge:** Category-2 mid-market marketplaces — CSV/API listing import → EAS validity poll → mandatory seller certification → embeddable “FCC ID: XXX” display snippet → immutable check log. Explicitly **exclude** SDoC display, Covered List component BOM, and full supply-chain OS from MVP.

**Stretch wedge (only if interviews demand it):** Category-1 accuracy review queue (photo of device label vs grant description + human approve/deny) priced higher.

### MVP

Solo-developer Stripe web app:

1. Workspace per marketplace; roles for admin and reviewer.  
2. Listing table: SKU, title, seller, FCC ID, custody flag (Cat 1/2), status.  
3. On save: call OET `getFCCIDList` (with caching + backoff); store grant snapshot JSON + timestamp.  
4. Seller magic-link attestation (“I certify this FCC ID accurately identifies the listed device”) with IP/time.  
5. Read-only display token / Shopify metafield / HTML snippet for PDP.  
6. Export CSV audit pack (listing, FCC ID, API response hash, attestation, reviewer).  
7. Stripe subscription.  
No auto-filing with FCC. No legal determination that a device “is compliant.” No Covered List BOM engine in v1.

### AI role

Bounded: suggest whether a listing title/category is likely an RF-certified device (needs FCC ID field), draft seller reminder emails, and flag gross mismatches between listing title tokens and grant product description for **human** Category-1 review. Reject a chatbot that “approves compliance.”

Hypothesis only: **$79/mo** up to 500 active RF listings; **$199/mo** to 5,000; **$0.02**/extra validity refresh; optional **$299** one-time catalog backfill. Must beat a weekend script + Google Sheet. Unverified.

### Distribution

1. Outreach to operators of specialty electronics / drone / IoT / pro-AV multi-vendor marketplaces.  
2. Content SEO on “FCC ID online point of sale” once the FR Rule publishes and deadlines become calendar facts.  
3. Partnerships with Shopify Marketplace Kit agencies and small Mirakl implementers.  
4. Not paid social; CAC must stay low vs $79–$199 ARPU.

### Risks

- **Market:** Too few mid-market buyers; Amazon/Mirakl ship free-enough features.  
- **Regulatory:** FR Rule delayed, narrowed, or litigated; Enforcement Bureau stays soft.  
- **Product:** EAS rate limits / Access Denied without allowlisting; validity≠accuracy.  
- **Legal:** Product mistaken for legal advice; false “compliant” signals.  
- **GTM:** RF marketplace operators are sparse and relationship-sold.

### Kill criteria

Kill or park if any of the following:

1. Within 30 days of FR Rule publication, two of three interviewed target marketplaces say they will use a spreadsheet or native platform field and will not prepay.  
2. Mirakl, Shopify, or a major PIM ships § 2.803(c) validity+attestation as a default checkbox before five paying FccIdGate customers exist.  
3. OET ships bulk reverse-lookup that makes Category-1 accuracy a free commodity and Category-2 ARPU collapses below ~$40/mo with no expansion path.  
4. Ninety days after FR Rule publication: fewer than three paid workspaces.
