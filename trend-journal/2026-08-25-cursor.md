# CapFee Desk — H-1B cap-subject all-in cost & hire/no-hire decision desk for US small employers — 2026-08-25

**Contributor:** Cursor (Grok)
**File:** `trend-journal/2026-08-25-cursor.md`
**Market:** United States first; Indonesia relevance: credible secondary only via Indo/Indian talent agencies and employers who sponsor or place workers into US H-1B roles (cost modeling + lottery timing), not a domestic Indonesian product
**Motion:** B2B — US small-employer owner / people-ops lead pays and uses; optional later B2B2C via immigration counsel or Indo/India recruiting partners who white-label scenario PDFs for client employers
**Verdict:** Watch

## Summary

CapFee Desk would be a Stripe-billed web app that helps US small employers decide whether to sponsor a specific candidate under today’s H-1B fee stack versus the DHS-proposed **$103,265** additional fee on **all cap-subject** petitions (NPRM published / scheduled for the Federal Register on **2026-08-25**). The product would classify cap-subject vs cap-exempt, build all-in government + counsel + premium-processing scenarios, map OPT/STEM-OPT end dates against the March registration calendar, and export a board-ready hire/no-hire memo—not a generic AI immigration chatbot. Google Trends shows a same-day attention cluster around mass visa revocation / visa news (**50K+**), and USCIS/DHS primary sources confirm the fee proposal plus a Regulatory Flexibility finding that **~11,051 small entities** (76% of FY2025 small cap filers) would face significant economic impact if finalized. This stays **Watch**: the rule is only proposed (30-day comment window), Alma already sells flat-rate H-1B counsel + platform at **$3,500**/petition, Boundless/Envoy/Rippling and free blog calculators occupy budgeting, and open-source GitHub tools mostly serve job-seekers against DOL LCA data rather than employer fee decisions. Revisit after prepaid interviews or a final rule with an effective date.

## Inference

- The **durable job** (if any) is not “explain the news.” It is **employer capital allocation**: for a named candidate, decide sponsor / delay / hire domestically / use EOR / pursue cap-exempt path when the proposed fee would turn a ~$5k–$10k sponsorship into a **>$100k** cash event.
- DHS’s own RFA language (11k+ small entities) is strong **pain-population evidence**, but it is still a **proposed** burden estimate—not proof those entities will buy a SaaS desk instead of calling counsel once.
- Alma’s published **$3,500** flat petition fee already bundles the compliance workflow startups need; a solo Stripe calculator that stops at a PDF memo must beat free blog math + counsel’s intake form. Differentiation only holds if employers want a **pre-counsel, multi-scenario board pack** they refresh as the NPRM moves—or if counsel white-labels it.
- Indonesia is a plausible **distribution/secondary** angle (agencies counseling US-bound talent and their US employer clients) but not a primary market: the fee attaches to USCIS petitions under US law.
- Same-day B-1/B-2 revocation headlines inflate “visa” search volume without creating an employer SaaS wedge for CapFee Desk; do not conflate the two policies.

## Assumptions

- A meaningful share of US employers with **≤25 employees** who file (or consider) cap-subject H-1Bs will pay a small subscription or one-shot fee for scenario modeling before engaging counsel.
- Cap-subject vs cap-exempt classification can be productized as a **checklist with human confirmation**, not automated legal advice.
- The NPRM will either finalize with a known effective date (creating a rush to model budgets) or die in comments/litigation (killing urgency)—either outcome is observable within months.
- No prepaid LOIs or employer interviews were collected in this scout (none claimed).

## Consulting assessment

### Trend durability

**Policy durable, attention spiky.** Cap-subject H-1B fee fights and lottery rule changes have been recurring 2025–2026 employer issues (proclamation fee vacated/appealed; wage-weighted lottery; now an NPRM to permanently attach ~$103k to cap filings). Search spikes around “visa revocation” are news-cycle. Durability for a product exists only if employers must repeatedly model **candidate-level** cost under changing fee rules across annual registration seasons—not if they treat this as a one-time headline.

### Customer and JTBD

- **Motion:** B2B (optional later B2B2C via counsel / Indo-India recruiting partners).
- **Buyer who pays:** founder, COO, or people-ops lead at a US small employer (typically ≤25 FTE for small-employer fee tiers) considering 1–5 international specialty hires.
- **User who uses:** same buyer; sometimes an external bookkeeper preparing a board pack.
- **Intermediary (optional):** immigration attorney, startup accelerator, or overseas recruiting agency.
- **JTBD:** “Before I open an H-1B registration or make an offer, show me all-in cost under current fees vs the proposed $103,265 add-on, whether this person is cap-subject, and what happens if I wait, switch to cap-exempt, or do not sponsor.”

### Pain evidence

DHS RFA text asserts significant economic impact on **11,051** small FY2025 cap filers if the fee is finalized—hard primary-source evidence of financial stakes. Employer-facing firms (Envoy, Alma, Boundless) are already publishing urgent explainers, which indicates confusion and budget pressure. Missing: verified interviews that small employers will pay for a **standalone** desk rather than folding the question into Alma/Boundless/counsel engagements they already need for the petition itself.

### Alternatives

1. Immigration counsel / Alma ($3,500 flat) / Boundless / Fragomen-class firms — full filing + advice.
2. Free / content calculators and blog cost tables (Rippling, Boundless, Alma guides, Beancount-style posts).
3. HRIS immigration modules (Rippling and peers) and EOR alternatives when sponsorship math breaks.
4. Spreadsheet + USCIS fee schedule maintained by the founder.
5. OSS job-seeker LCA browsers (VisaTrack, opportunity-scorer)—wrong buyer.

### Wedge

**Narrow:** pre-counsel, candidate-level **cap-subject classification + dual-scenario fee board pack** for US employers with ≤25 employees who have **not** yet retained petition counsel—and who need a refreshable memo while the NPRM is open. Explicitly **not** petition preparation, LCA filing, or lottery registration automation (those are Alma/USCIS territory).

### MVP

Smallest Stripe-monetizable web app (~3–5 focused weeks for one strong developer):

1. Employer profile (headcount band, nonprofit/research flags for cap-exempt heuristics).
2. Candidate card: location (US vs abroad), current status (F-1 OPT / STEM OPT / other), degree type, target start date, offered wage band.
3. Deterministic fee stack tables: current USCIS fees (small vs large employer) ± premium processing ± **proposed $103,265** toggle with “not yet in force” banner and NPRM effective-date field.
4. Cap-subject vs likely cap-exempt checklist with mandatory human confirmation and disclaimer (not legal advice).
5. Export PDF/CSV board memo: scenarios A/B/C, cash timing, kill/hire recommendation fields filled by the user.
6. Stripe Checkout: **$49 one-shot** per employer workspace (or **$19/mo** while modeling through comment/final-rule window).

### AI role

Optional and subordinate: draft plain-language board memo prose and suggest which scenario labels match the user’s checklist answers. Fee math and cap/exempt flags must remain **deterministic tables + human confirmation**. Reject a generic “ask an immigration AI” wrapper.

### Pricing

Hypothesis: **$49** one-shot board pack or **$19/mo** through the regulatory window; optional **$99** assisted scenario review (still not legal advice). This only clears WTP if interviews show employers will pay **before** the $3,500 Alma engagement—not instead of it. If buyers say “my lawyer already emailed a spreadsheet,” kill.

### Distribution

1. SEO/content around “H-1B $103,265 fee small employer calculator” during the comment window and any final-rule announcement.
2. Outbound to YC / Techstars / indie SaaS founders with OPT employees approaching cap season.
3. Partner embeds for Indo/India recruiting agencies preparing US employer clients (secondary market).
4. Do **not** rely on Trends “visa revocation” ads—those audiences are mostly consumers/asylum news readers.

### Risks

- **Market:** NPRM never finalizes → urgency collapses; or finalizes with exemptions that remove the small-employer wedge.
- **Product:** unauthorized-practice-of-law perception if copy overclaims advice.
- **Competitive:** Alma/Boundless absorb the calculator as free lead-gen; free blog posts suffice for one decision per year.
- **Retention:** annual lottery cadence may not support monthly SaaS without a broader immigration calendar product.
- **Go-to-market:** CAC against noisy immigration news is high; buyers may only convert when a final effective date is announced.
- **Legal/compliance:** must cite primary sources, version fee tables, and keep disclaimers prominent.

### Kill criteria

Stop unless, within **21 days** of a **final rule with effective date** (or sooner if interviews allow):

1. At least **8** US employers with ≤25 FTE confirm they need a written multi-scenario pack **before** retaining petition counsel.
2. At least **2** pay a non-refundable **$49** for a concierge memo using their real candidate facts.
3. Interviewees do **not** uniformly say Alma/Boundless/counsel already covers the decision at acceptable cost.
4. Cap-subject classification for their common cases can be checklisted without custom legal research per employer.
5. Indonesia/partner channel (if pursued) produces at least one paying US employer introduction—otherwise drop the secondary-market claim.

### Verdict

**Watch.** Primary-source fee proposal and small-entity impact estimates are strong, and a solo developer can ship the desk—but the rule is not final, counsel platforms already monetize the adjacent job at known prices, and today’s Trends spike mixes unrelated visa headlines. Do **not** create an `idea-grill` entry. Revisit on final-rule publication or after prepaid concierge memos clear the kill criteria.
