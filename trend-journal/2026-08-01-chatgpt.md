## Summary

**PracticeGrid** is a narrow visual planner for indoor youth-sports programs that run several teams and coaches through shared gym zones during the same practice. Its core screen preserves the spreadsheet mental model already described by one coach: coaches or teams on rows, time on columns, and gym areas as colored blocks. Each block opens a nested lesson plan, while independent counters rotate warm-ups, strength plans, and station curricula based on the number of times a team has visited an area rather than a simple weekly recurrence.

The opportunity is more focused than another TeamSnap: no rosters, parent messaging, registration, payments, athlete profiles, or video library. The proposed wedge is **resource allocation inside a practice plus recurrence-by-visit**, not merely scheduling the practice or writing a drill list. It is technically simple and compatible with Stripe subscriptions. However, today's pain evidence is concentrated in one unusually detailed first-person report, while Practice Plan App, TeamSnap, and Gymnastics Tools already occupy adjacent planning, station, floor-layout, and multi-team workflows. The product remains Watch until direct interviews show that existing tools cannot reproduce the coach × time × zone matrix and that clubs will pay for it separately.

## Inference

- The nested recurrence problem is meaningfully different from a normal calendar rule. “Use plan 4 on the fourth visit to area 2” depends on event history, not a fixed weekday, which explains why a coach may fall back to handwritten tracking.
- A coach × time × zone matrix could deliver value without storing children's personal data, reducing privacy, COPPA, onboarding, and parent-support scope.
- The closest plausible wedge is a lightweight **intra-practice operations layer** that exports a mobile/print brief and coexists with TeamSnap rather than replacing it.
- Practice Plan App's public pricing establishes a low-cost category anchor. A new product cannot charge enterprise prices without proving that collision prevention and automated rotation materially save program-director time.
- Because one detailed complaint can represent an edge case, discovery risk is materially higher than build risk.

## Assumptions

- At least one narrow vertical, likely gymnastics, cheer, volleyball, swimming, or multi-court academies, regularly coordinates multiple simultaneous groups and coaches.
- Existing tools cannot configure the exact coach × time × physical-zone view and independent curriculum counters without awkward workarounds.
- A club will tolerate a separate tool alongside its roster/communication platform if setup takes less than 30 minutes and the weekly plan exports cleanly.
- A program director will pay $29–$59 per month, or a one-time setup fee, instead of continuing with Excel/Google Sheets.

## Consulting assessment

### Trend durability

Youth sports participation is a durable US activity base and reportedly increased in the latest federal data. Practice planning is recurring each week or season. Google Trends did not provide usable quantitative evidence in this run, so the opportunity is not framed as a rising-search trend. Its durability rests on a repeated operational job, not a short-lived news spike.

### Customer and JTBD

**Motion:** B2B.  
**Buyer:** owner, head coach, recreation director, or program director at a multi-team youth-sports club.  
**Users:** head coaches and assistant coaches preparing and following practice rotations.  
**Excluded from MVP:** athletes, children, parents, payment collection, attendance, medical data, and messaging.

**JTBD:** “Before a shared-facility practice, assign every team and coach to a safe non-conflicting zone for each time block, attach the correct lesson plan for that team's next visit, and give every coach one clear mobile or printable run sheet.”

### Pain evidence

The strongest evidence is the coach's unusually precise workaround: a two-dimensional spreadsheet for space plus handwritten tracking for curricula with different recurrence cycles. That is excellent problem detail but weak market coverage because it comes from one person. Broader sources establish that timed station plans and multi-team program planning exist, but do not independently quantify hours lost, collision frequency, safety incidents, or willingness to pay.

### Alternatives

- Google Sheets or Excel with colors, merged cells, copied tabs, and manual counters.
- Paper calendars and printed practice-plan templates.
- Practice Plan App for periods, templates, shared schedules, multiple teams, and mobile views.
- TeamSnap for the overall schedule, roster, messages, and practice content.
- Gymnastics Tools for floor layouts and station placement.
- Full club-management suites such as Zen Planner for classes, attendance, billing, and member operations.

The manual alternative is free and flexible. The adjacent SaaS alternatives are inexpensive. PracticeGrid must be dramatically faster for this exact matrix, not merely prettier.

### Wedge

Start with **gymnastics programs running three or more concurrent groups in one facility**. The first promise is:

> Turn your coach × time × apparatus spreadsheet into a conflict-checked practice plan with the correct next lesson attached to every block.

The defensibility, if any, comes from reusable facility layouts, visit-based curriculum state, and accumulated templates. AI is not the wedge.

### MVP

1. Define teams, coaches, facility zones/apparatus, practice duration, and transition buffer.
2. Drag colored team blocks across a time grid; flag double-booked coaches or zones immediately.
3. Attach reusable warm-up, flexibility, strength, and apparatus lesson plans.
4. Maintain an independent “next plan” counter per team × curriculum family or team × zone.
5. Clone last practice or apply a saved rotation template.
6. Generate one mobile link plus printable PDF/PNG views for the full floor and each coach.
7. Stripe subscription and a sample workspace; no athlete records or parent accounts.

A strong developer could prototype the core grid and export flow in roughly two to four focused weeks. This is an inference, not a committed estimate.

### AI role

Do not lead with AI. The core is deterministic scheduling and state. A narrow optional use could parse an uploaded spreadsheet into proposed teams, zones, and blocks, with human confirmation. A language model should not resolve collisions or choose safety-sensitive supervision assignments.

### Pricing

**Hypothesis:** $29/month for up to five teams and one facility; $59/month for up to twenty teams and three facilities. Offer a $99 one-time concierge import from the club's current spreadsheet. These prices deliberately align with Practice Plan App's verified $29 and $59 program tiers. They are not validated willingness-to-pay evidence.

### Distribution

Begin with one vertical and a visual artifact:

1. Build a clickable mockup reproducing the exact coach × time × zone workflow.
2. Contact US gymnastics program directors through public club directories and local associations.
3. Post a before/after spreadsheet conversion video in coach communities where promotion is allowed.
4. Offer ten free spreadsheet conversions in exchange for a recorded workflow interview.
5. Ask for a $99 paid setup before building team accounts or integrations.

This is founder-led outbound, so cash CAC can be low initially, but labor CAC may be high. No paid acquisition should begin until conversion and retention are observed.

### Risks

- **Demand concentration:** one detailed pain report may be idiosyncratic.
- **Incumbent adjacency:** Practice Plan App or Gymnastics Tools may already handle the workflow or add it quickly.
- **Low price ceiling:** inexpensive tools and spreadsheets anchor expectations below $60/month.
- **Setup friction:** facility maps and curriculum rules may require too much configuration.
- **Retention:** some clubs may plan once per season and then churn or pause.
- **Technical:** complex drag/drop on phones, print fidelity, time-zone/calendar edge cases, concurrent edits, and accessibility.
- **Safety/liability:** collision warnings must not be presented as safety certification; humans remain responsible for supervision and apparatus decisions.
- **Go-to-market:** youth-sports communities may resist vendor outreach, and Rizaldy's domain credibility is unproven.
- **Expansion:** Indonesian academies may use WhatsApp and spreadsheets with lower willingness to pay; localization is not just translation.

### Kill criteria

Run a 14-day validation sprint before coding the SaaS:

- Interview 15 US program directors or head coaches in one sport.
- **Kill** if fewer than 6 currently use a spreadsheet or paper grid to coordinate simultaneous teams, coaches, and zones.
- **Kill** if 5 or more of the first 10 qualified respondents can reproduce the desired workflow comfortably in an existing paid tool.
- **Kill** if fewer than 3 of 15 will provide an anonymized real schedule for conversion.
- **Kill** if fewer than 2 pay $99 for a concierge conversion or sign a concrete $39/month pilot commitment.
- After MVP, **kill or reposition** if fewer than 5 of 10 pilots still use it weekly after six weeks.

The current evidence does not justify Build because it rests too heavily on one coach, adjacent products already sell multi-team planning at low prices, and no paid commitment or independently verified gap exists.
