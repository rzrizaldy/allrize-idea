# ALEPacket — Wildfire evacuation ALE / prohibited-use expense claim packet — 2026-08-24

**Contributor:** Cursor
**File:** `trend-journal/2026-08-24-cursor.md`
**Market:** United States first (Nevada attention wedge via Hawk / Reno wildfires + AB376); Indonesia relevance: not yet established
**Motion:** B2C primary (displaced household pays and uses); optional later B2B2C via independent insurance agents / public adjusters who gift or resell packets
**Verdict:** Watch

## Summary

Scout a Stripe-first web app that helps US households displaced by wildfire assemble an **Additional Living Expense (ALE) / “prohibited use” expense claim packet**—dated receipts, evacuation-order timeline, coverage-type notes from the declarations page—not a general home-inventory catalog. Google Trends shows a large **hawk fire** attention spike (200K+), Nevada DOI and United Policyholders both urge saving evacuation receipts and distinguishing short “prohibited use” benefits from longer ALE when the home is damaged, and Nevada’s AB376 created a durable statutory path for wildfire exclusions (though **no carrier has invoked them yet** as of mid-August DOI confirmation). This stays **Watch**: free DOI/NAIC/UP guidance plus phone video and a spreadsheet already occupy most of the job; Sortly / HomeProof / Nestarr cover inventory; B2C CAC during trauma is hard; AB376 “coverage gap auditor” demand is premature until exclusions appear on renewals.

## Inference

- The **attention spike** (Hawk / Reno fire searches) is event-driven; the **durable underlying jobs** are (1) receipt-backed ALE / prohibited-use substantiation during displacement and (2) eventual AB376-driven coverage literacy at renewal—not “another AI home inventory.”
- A credible product wedge vs free NAIC inventory + UP PDFs is narrowly **expense-timeline + coverage-bucket labeling** (hotel, food delta, pet boarding, mileage, deposits) tied to evacuation-order dates and declarations notes—not belongings cataloging.
- AB376 creates a multi-year statutory sandbox through ~2030, but DOI confirmation that **no carrier has excluded yet** means a “did my renewal drop wildfire?” auditor is **early**. Prefer watching carrier filings / renewal notices over building that wedge now.
- B2C willingness to pay during evacuation is uncertain: survivors already get free checklists; many will use Notes + camera roll; lawyers/public adjusters take a cut of large claims. A **$19–$39 one-shot Stripe packet** is plausible only if it clearly beats a Drive folder on adjuster readability within hours of displacement.
- Indonesia is not a credible secondary market for US homeowners-policy ALE workflows.

## Assumptions

- A meaningful share of evacuated households will pay a small one-shot fee rather than use free UP/DOI templates + a spreadsheet.
- “Prohibited use” vs ALE labeling can be productized as **education + structured fields**, not legal advice, without becoming an unauthorized practice-of-law product.
- Independent agents might distribute white-label packets (B2B2C) if households churn too hard for direct B2C ads.
- Wildfire seasons in the West continue to generate recurring displacement cohorts large enough for SEO/content acquisition outside a single fire’s news cycle.

## Consulting assessment

### Trend durability

Wildfire displacement and Loss-of-Use / ALE disputes are recurring West Coast insurance workflows (UP has published guidance for years). Nevada AB376 is a durable **regulatory option** for carriers through a four-year experimentation window, but the **exclusion product change has not yet hit most policies**. Hawk Fire search volume is a short-lived attention spike that can seed distribution content; it should not be mistaken for durable SaaS demand by itself.

### Customer and JTBD

**Motion:** B2C. **Buyer/user:** adult household member under evacuation order or recently returned, filing or preparing a Loss-of-Use / ALE claim. **JTBD:** while displaced (or immediately after), keep every reimbursable extra expense with dates, categories, and policy-bucket notes so the insurer’s ALE review does not stall on missing receipts or confused “prohibited use” vs ALE timelines. Optional later partner: independent agent who wants a client-facing packet link.

### Pain evidence

DOI tells residents to save evacuation receipts; Indy reporting names ALE duration and valuation disputes as the complaint pattern; UP emphasizes claim diaries and receipt retention. That is observable process pain. It does **not** yet prove paid conversion against free apps and PDFs.

### Alternatives

- Free: phone video + camera roll; spreadsheet; DOI/NAIC inventory app; UP checklists and sample ALE letters; DOI complaint escalation.
- Paid household inventory: HomeProof (~$20/yr), Sortly (higher SMB pricing), other catalog apps.
- Pro channel: public adjusters, bad-faith counsel, restoration firms on Encircle.
- Doing nothing / accepting adjuster framing (under-collection risk).

**Gap to test:** a **displacement expense packet** (not inventory) that auto-builds a chronological PDF with category totals and a prohibited-use vs ALE timeline section.

### Wedge

v1 geography: **Nevada + California wildfire-evacuated households** who need ALE expense packs in the first 72 hours. Explicitly **not** rebuilding cost estimating, not smoke-remediation scope, not AB376 legal advice, not a Sortly clone.

### MVP

Solo-dev Stripe web app:

1. Create claim file: address, carrier, claim #, evacuation order start/end (manual dates + optional photo of order).
2. Receipt capture (mobile web): photo → OCR amount/date/merchant; category tags (lodging, food delta, pet boarding, mileage, deposits, other).
3. Declarations helper: upload dec page; checklist prompts for Loss-of-Use / ALE / prohibited-use language (**human confirms**; no coverage determination).
4. Export insurer-ready PDF: timeline, totals by category, receipt thumbnails, diary of adjuster contacts.
5. Stripe Checkout: **$29 one-shot** per claim file (or $9/mo while displaced, cancel anytime).

### AI role

OCR receipts and draft category suggestions + a neutral cover letter summarizing totals. Must **not** invent policy limits, promise coverage, or generate fake receipts. Durable value is structured expense evidence under time pressure—not a chatbot “claims lawyer.”

### Pricing

**Hypothesis, unverified:** $29 one-shot packet; $49 concierge (human review of PDF within 24h) as validation offer. Agent seat later: $39/mo for 10 client packet links. Kill if prepaid interviews refuse anything above $0 when shown UP’s free PDF beside the mock.

### Distribution

- SEO/content timed to named fires (“Hawk Fire ALE receipts checklist”) with strong free tier / template lead magnet.
- Partnerships with United Policyholders-adjacent community orgs only if they accept non-exploitative positioning (likely hard—UP is free-first).
- Independent insurance agents in Washoe / Clark counties for post-evacuation client kits.
- Avoid paid social that looks like disaster profiteering; trust risk is existential.

### Risks

- **Market:** Event-driven acquisition; off-season retention near zero for pure B2C.
- **Product:** Looks like inventory; users bounce to NAIC free app.
- **Legal/trust:** Coverage interpretation edges into UPL; disaster-pricing optics.
- **GTM:** Competing with free nonprofit guidance that survivors are told to use first.
- **AB376 narrative risk:** Building an “exclusion checker” before exclusions exist wastes runway.

### Kill criteria

- 15 prepaid ($29) or 20 problem interviews with recently evacuated households fail to prefer the packet over Drive + UP checklist.
- ≥2 credible consumer apps ship the same ALE-timeline packet at ≤$20 with distribution already in wildfire Facebook groups.
- DOI/UP publish an official free ALE expense workbook that matches the MVP output.
- Carrier AB376 exclusions remain unused through the next full renewal cycle **and** interview demand was only for exclusion checking (not expense packets)—then drop the NV-specific thesis.
