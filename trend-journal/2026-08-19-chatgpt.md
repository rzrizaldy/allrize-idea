## Executive summary

**StormMargin** is a pre-season contract stress tester for small snow-removal and landscaping contractors. It imports a proposed per-push, per-inch, or seasonal contract; combines contractor-supplied labor, equipment, salt, fuel, overhead, and service-trigger assumptions with historical NOAA snowfall observations; then shows how that bid would have performed across prior winters. The output is a P10/P50/P90 margin range, cash-exposure warning, contract-model comparison, and an exportable bid-assumption sheet.

This is intentionally **not** dispatch software, proof-of-service software, or a long-range snowfall forecast. The wedge is the decision made before signing a contract: “Can this price survive a bad winter?”

- **Who pays:** owner or estimator of a small snow-removal/landscaping contractor.
- **Who uses it:** owner, estimator, or operations lead.
- **Intermediary:** optional acquisition partners such as snow-industry consultants, equipment dealers, insurance brokers, or accountants; none is required to deliver the product.
- **Suggested monetization:** $79 per pre-season portfolio stress test; optional $29/month August–November workspace; $199/season consultant tier.
- **Why Watch:** the pain and data feasibility are credible, but paid demand for a standalone pre-bid simulator is not yet verified and adjacent estimating products are plentiful.

### Workflow and pain

- Current snow-pricing guidance tells contractors to research historical snowfall and estimate a base price from per-visit cost multiplied by expected events. [FieldCamp, 2026](https://fieldcamp.ai/snow-removal-software/how-much-to-charge-for-snow-removal/)
- A February 2026 pricing guide recommends using five to ten years of historical weather, calculating cost per event, and targeting a 15–25% net margin. [TinyLawn](https://gettinylawn.com/blog/snow-removal-contracts-flat-rate-vs-per-push-pricing/)
- Industry guidance separately recommends roughly ten years of historical weather and testing contract increments against extreme snowfall and ice seasons. [Turf Magazine](https://turfmagazine.com/using-historical-weather-data/)
- Contractor discussions describe seasonal pricing as highly dependent on local weather and advise inexperienced operators to use per-push pricing until they understand the risk. This is anecdotal evidence, not a market-size estimate. [Contractor discussion](https://www.reddit.com/r/sweatystartup/comments/178xm9l/snow_removal_contract_help/)

### Data and technical feasibility

- NOAA’s Daily Summaries dataset integrates daily observations including snowfall and snow depth. [NOAA NCEI Daily Summaries](https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/access/search/datasets/daily-summaries/)
- NOAA’s GHCN-Daily is an integrated, quality-reviewed database of daily land-station observations. [NOAA GHCN-Daily](https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/products/land-based-station/global-historical-climatology-network-daily)
- The open-source [get-weather-data](https://github.com/gojiplus/get-weather-data) project already demonstrates ZIP/coordinate lookup, automatic NOAA station selection, coverage checks, missing-variable fallback, and GHCN Daily/nClimGrid access. This lowers ingestion risk but does not solve contract economics.
- The open-source [Schneespur](https://github.com/noschmarrn/Schneespur) project covers GPS tracking, automatic weather records, photos, PDFs, customer portals, and offline field use for winter-service operators. That confirms post-service evidence is already an established product area; StormMargin should stay pre-bid and analytical.
- The builder’s public [jkt-drivetime](https://github.com/rzrizaldy/jkt-drivetime) project demonstrates geospatial APIs, MapLibre/Turf, routing, scenario multipliers, caching, and tests. [deck_of_prosperity](https://github.com/rzrizaldy/deck_of_prosperity) demonstrates React/TypeScript/Vite, automated tests, and deployed web delivery. The MVP is technically well matched.

### Competition and substitutes

- Snow-first operator software already covers scheduling, dispatch, invoicing, and reporting; Yeti starts at $76/month. [Yeti](https://www.yetisnow.com/snow/)
- Broad estimating/field-service suites are available from $29.99/month and include estimates, invoicing, and scheduling. [QuoteIQ pricing](https://intercom.help/quoteiq/en/articles/11389931-quoteiq-pricing-plans)
- Free generic snow pricing estimators and contract templates exist. [CrewNest estimator](https://www.crewnest.app/snow-removal-pricing)
- Existing alternatives are spreadsheets, an accountant’s model, broad estimating suites, and intuition. None of the sources reviewed clearly markets a historical-season portfolio stress test as the core product, but absence from this review is not proof of an empty category.

## Inference

- The trending query is useful timing: contractors and customers are beginning to think about winter. It does not establish willingness to pay for StormMargin.
- The narrow wedge is stronger than “another snow CRM”: upload one real bid, run historical seasons, expose downside, and export assumptions.
- A one-time or seasonal price fits the buyer’s annual decision cadence better than a year-round subscription.
- Portfolio-level exposure could be more valuable than a single-job calculator because correlated storms can make multiple fixed-price contracts fail at once.
- Optional AI may extract pricing tiers, caps, triggers, and exclusions from a contract PDF, but all weather aggregation and margin simulation should be deterministic and auditable.
- Indonesia is not a sensible near-term expansion market because the core job depends on snow. A future rain/flood service-contract product would be a different thesis and is not part of this opportunity.

## Assumptions to validate

- Small operators keep enough structured cost data to produce a useful model without lengthy onboarding.
- Ten to twenty years of nearby station observations can be matched to service locations with acceptable missing-data handling.
- Daily snowfall can be grouped into billable events without materially misrepresenting local trigger, ice, drifting, and salting conditions.
- Contractors will pay for risk analysis before they already own a full estimating suite.
- Consultants and equipment/accounting partners can become lower-cost acquisition channels.
- The product can avoid implied forecasting or financial guarantees through clear methodology, source disclosure, and user-controlled assumptions.

## MVP boundary

A two-week validation build should contain only:

1. CSV/manual input for location, contract model, trigger tiers, costs, capacity, and proposed price.
2. Nearest-station selection with coverage disclosure and user confirmation.
3. Historical-season simulation with editable event grouping.
4. P10/P50/P90 gross-margin and cash-exposure view.
5. Per-push versus seasonal comparison.
6. PDF/CSV export of assumptions and outcomes.
7. Stripe Checkout for a one-season report.

Do not build dispatch, route optimization, GPS tracking, invoicing, customer portals, or precise winter forecasting.

## 48-hour validation experiment

1. Make a one-page interactive mockup with one anonymized sample property and three historical winters.
2. Contact 12 US snow/landscape contractors who sell seasonal contracts.
3. Ask each to bring one current spreadsheet or bid and run it manually through the model.
4. Offer the finished portfolio report for a **non-refundable $79 deposit**.
5. Record whether the analysis changes a price, contract model, cap, or go/no-go decision.

### Build gate

Promote to **Build** only if, within 12 qualified conversations:

- at least five contractors currently use a spreadsheet or manual historical-weather process;
- at least three pay the $79 deposit;
- at least two reports change or reject a real bid; and
- no dominant incumbent is already bundled into most prospects’ workflow.

### Kill criteria

Reject the thesis if fewer than three deposits are obtained, users mainly demand dispatch/CRM features, station/event reconstruction requires extensive manual meteorological work, or no tested bid decision changes.
