**Verdict:** **Reject**

## Summary

TrapManifest would be a mobile-first web app for completing grease-interceptor pump-out records in the field, capturing customer and driver signatures, linking a disposal ticket, producing a jurisdiction-specific FOG manifest, and scheduling the next service. The underlying compliance workflow is real: local programs in Tifton, Georgia and Abilene, Texas require a manifest for each pump-out, while other jurisdictions require records to be retained and submitted. The opportunity should nevertheless be rejected in this form. PumpDocket already packages field closeout, FOG manifests, recurring scheduling, compliance profiles, invoicing, Stripe payments, and mobile use for pumping companies; ServiceCore explicitly serves grease-trap operators with scheduling, routing, billing, a driver app, proof-of-service photos, and compliance reporting; GreaseIQ markets digital manifests and compliance automation. The proposed wedge is therefore an incumbent feature bundle, not a new product position.

### Regulatory workflow

- Tifton–Tift County's FOG program requires a manifest for every grease-interceptor location serviced. It specifies generator, transporter, and receiving-facility information and allows the manifest to be mailed, faxed, or submitted electronically within 14 days: [Tifton FOG Management Program](https://www.tiftonga.gov/DocumentCenter/View/233/FOG-Ordinance-PDF).
- Abilene, Texas requires every grease-trap or interceptor pump-out to be accompanied by a manifest for recordkeeping: [Abilene FOG Guidelines and Procedures](https://abilenetx.gov/DocumentCenter/View/4080/08-18-2017-Exhibit-B-City-of-Abilene-FOG-Guidelines-and-Procedures-PDF).
- Lincoln County, North Carolina requires food-service users to keep permits, pump/clean manifests, and repair records on site for three years; its FOG unit sets cleaning frequency and says an interceptor may not be scheduled less frequently than quarterly: [Lincoln County FOG program](https://www.lincolncountync.gov/DocumentCenter/View/23964/2024-09-16_Lincoln-County-FOG-program_FULL-DOC_ADOPTED).
- Requirements are not uniform. PumpDocket's state guide identifies Delaware grease-trap hauling as requiring registration and a written maintenance contract but not a manifest. This is a vendor summary rather than the governing regulation, but it is a useful counterexample to the Drive document's nationwide framing: [PumpDocket Delaware guide](https://www.pumpdocket.com/compliance-guide/delaware).
- EPA describes FOG programs as based on EPA CMOM programs and state regulations; the operational forms and frequencies are implemented through state and local programs, not one universal federal four-part grease-manifest template: [EPA FOG Management & Control presentation](https://www.epa.gov/system/files/documents/2023-10/fog-slides.pdf).

### Alternatives and competitive overlap

- [PumpDocket pricing](https://www.pumpdocket.com/pricing) lists a **$230/month Team** plan for 4–10 trucks with recurring scheduling and reminders, regulatory-profile trip tickets, FOG manifests and grease-trap compliance, QuickBooks sync, unlimited customers, and mobile field closeout. Its Starter plan is **$99/month** for 1–3 trucks with dispatch, invoicing, online payments through Stripe, customer portal, e-signatures, and grease-trap asset tracking.
- PumpDocket explicitly says it serves septic tanks, grease traps, and portable restrooms. Its comparison page states that PumpDocket and Tank Track both cover dispatch, recurring scheduling, invoicing, compliance, and e-signatures and that PumpDocket is phone-optimized with offline support: [PumpDocket comparison](https://www.pumpdocket.com/resources/tanktrack-alternative).
- [ServiceCore grease-trap software](https://servicecore.com/grease-trap-pumping-software/) is purpose-built for grease pumping and markets scheduling, dispatch, route optimization, compliance reporting, invoicing, and QuickBooks compatibility. Its mobile workflow includes time-stamped proof-of-service images: [ServiceCore workflow](https://servicecore.com/how-servicecore-software-works/).
- [GreaseIQ](https://greaseiq.com/) markets digital manifests, route optimization, and compliance automation specifically for grease-trap businesses.
- [Linko's Pumpout Manifest Portal](https://www.linkopom.com/) already supports electronic pump-out manifests for municipal FOG programs. [SwiftComply](https://www.swiftcomply.com/fog-program-software/) and [LinkoFOG](https://www.linkotechnology.com/fats-oil-grease-software.html) sell the receiving municipal side of the workflow.
- Bellevue, Washington moved grease-device service records from mail and fax to SwiftComply, demonstrating a material platform-dependency risk: a municipality can prescribe its own portal rather than accept a third-party PDF: [Bellevue enrollment notice](https://bellevuewa.gov/sites/default/files/media/pdf_document/2021/SwiftComply%20Customer%20Enrollment%20Mailer.pdf).

### Relevant GitHub evidence and founder build fit

- [USEPA/haztrak](https://github.com/USEPA/haztrak) is an MIT-licensed Django/React/PostgreSQL example showing how waste-management software can connect to EPA's hazardous-waste e-Manifest API. Grease-interceptor waste is a different regulatory domain, so Haztrak cannot be treated as a ready-made compliance backend. It does show that serious electronic manifesting involves identities, receiving facilities, chain-of-custody state, and external-system interfaces rather than PDF generation alone.
- The founder's [RizeBot Hermes](https://github.com/rzrizaldy/rizebot-hermes) demonstrates deterministic scheduled checks, policy-driven state transitions, retention controls, bounded reports, audit history, and human-review gates. Those are useful primitives for recurring service reminders and defensible compliance records.
- The founder's [twin.md](https://github.com/rzrizaldy/twin_md) demonstrates TypeScript workspace development, local state, action approvals, structured capture, build tooling, and packaged delivery. Founder build fit is high for the workflow and document-generation layer; field-device adoption, municipal template maintenance, and sales access remain unproven.

## Inference

- The compliance need is durable because restaurants repeatedly clean interceptors and must preserve or submit records in many jurisdictions.
- A digital form can reduce illegibility and missing fields, but the proposed product must maintain jurisdiction-specific rules and integrations, which is operationally heavier than a simple PDF SaaS.
- The exact target segment—small pumping companies—already has at least two industry-specific full-stack products plus specialist manifest and municipal platforms.
- PumpDocket's public pricing creates a possible low-price opening below $99–$230/month, but price alone is weak differentiation when a new vendor lacks dispatch, billing, QuickBooks, support, regulatory content, and customer migration.
- Municipal portals can disintermediate a standalone manifest generator or force per-city integrations.
- OCR of disposal tickets is a feature candidate, not a standalone moat. The Drive document provided no direct user evidence that ticket transcription is the top unresolved problem.
- Indonesia may have an analogous commercial-kitchen maintenance workflow, but this run verified no nationwide manifest rule, searchable buyer directory, or Stripe-compatible acquisition channel there.

## Assumptions

- One- to three-truck grease pumpers still use enough paper to consider another vendor.
- They would adopt a separate manifest tool rather than choose an all-in-one pumping platform.
- Local authorities accept electronic signatures and third-party generated PDFs in the intended jurisdictions.
- A solo founder can keep hundreds of local form templates and submission rules accurate without legal or regulatory staff.
- Drivers have reliable enough devices and connectivity, or an offline PWA can cover the field environment.
- Disposal-ticket photos are consistently readable enough for OCR and human confirmation.
- Pumping-company owners can buy a $49–$99 monthly subscription by card without integration or onboarding services.
- A partner channel through disposal facilities or industry associations is available on commercial terms.

## Consulting assessment

### 1. Trend durability

The recurring maintenance and recordkeeping behavior is real. Google Trends did not return usable data, so the proposal has no verified growth signal and the `+40% YoY` claim must be discarded.

### 2. Customer and JTBD

- **Motion:** B2B.
- **Who pays:** independent grease-trap pumping company, septic/liquid-waste hauler, or small fleet owner.
- **Who uses:** vacuum-truck driver, dispatcher, office administrator, and owner.
- **Who receives or validates output:** restaurant manager, disposal facility, municipal FOG inspector, and bookkeeper.
- **Potential intermediaries:** wastewater and onsite-wastewater associations, disposal facilities, vacuum-truck equipment dealers, and municipal onboarding programs.
- **JTBD:** complete a valid record once at the job, preserve chain-of-custody evidence, submit it in the required format, and schedule the next service without re-keying data.

### 3. Pain evidence

Official programs verify that manifests contain multiple parties' data, require signatures, and may have submission and retention deadlines. That creates administrative burden. This run found no first-person interview, support thread, or paid pilot showing that PumpDocket, ServiceCore, existing portals, or paper are failing in a repeated way that justifies a new purchase.

### 4. Alternatives

- Paper or fillable jurisdiction forms plus a calendar and accounting package.
- PumpDocket for small-to-medium pumping fleets.
- ServiceCore for grease, septic, and other liquid-waste operations.
- GreaseIQ and GreaseTrapDispatch/DispatchNode for grease-specific digital manifests and automation.
- Tank Track and other liquid-waste field-service suites.
- Linko POM, LinkoFOG, SwiftComply, and other municipality-selected portals.

The proposed MVP is already covered by individual incumbents and by combinations of these tools.

### 5. Wedge

Proposed wedge: an inexpensive, driver-first offline manifest with disposal-ticket OCR and auto-generated local forms for 1–5 trucks.

The positioning is intelligible, but not yet defensible. PumpDocket already targets 1–3 trucks, works on phones, closes jobs in the field, takes payments, tracks grease traps, and adds FOG manifests and reminders at the next tier. OCR is not enough unless interviews prove it removes a uniquely expensive bottleneck incumbents cannot address.

### 6. MVP

Do not build a SaaS yet. A validation-only concierge prototype could:

1. support one city and one approved manifest;
2. prefill generator and hauler records;
3. capture required fields and signatures;
4. attach a disposal-ticket photo without OCR;
5. generate a reviewable PDF and submission checklist; and
6. schedule the next service reminder.

The test should be sold to one qualified pumper before any multi-jurisdiction template engine or AI component is developed.

### 7. AI role

AI is optional. OCR could suggest ticket number, receiving facility, date, and quantity, but a driver or office user must confirm each field. Rules, required fields, retention, and submission routing should be deterministic and versioned. AI should never assert that a manifest is legally compliant.

### 8. Pricing

Unverified validation offers:

- $99 one-time setup for one jurisdiction and one form.
- $49/month for one truck and up to 100 records.
- $89/month for three trucks, recurrence reminders, and audit export.

Stripe is a technically simple payment path. These prices intentionally undercut PumpDocket, but the buyer may rationally pay more for a complete dispatch, invoicing, compliance, and accounting system.

### 9. Distribution

The least speculative test is direct outreach to 20 independent pumpers licensed or listed by one municipality, offering a paid one-form conversion. A disposal facility could be an intermediary because it sees every load, but no willingness to distribute software was verified. SEO calculators and broad association sponsorships should wait until the workflow has paid validation.

### 10. Risks

- **Competition:** PumpDocket, ServiceCore, GreaseIQ, Linko, and SwiftComply overlap heavily.
- **Regulatory fragmentation:** local fields, signatures, frequencies, destinations, and submission methods vary.
- **Platform dependency:** a city can mandate its own portal.
- **Liability:** “compliant” output may be rejected or become outdated.
- **Integration:** dispatch, QuickBooks, payments, and municipal exports can turn a small app into a broad field-service platform.
- **Adoption:** drivers may reject a second app or redundant data entry.
- **Data quality:** OCR errors can corrupt chain-of-custody evidence.
- **GTM:** small industrial operators are fragmented and may require phone sales, demos, migration, and support.
- **Market size:** the Drive estimate of 35,000 businesses was not verified.
- **Expansion:** Indonesia's rules and buyer channel were not verified.

### 11. Kill criteria

Reject permanently unless a one-jurisdiction validation produces all of the following:

- at least 10 interviews with owners of 1–5 truck pumping businesses;
- at least 5 show the same current paper or re-keying failure;
- at least 3 prepay $99 setup plus a monthly subscription;
- none of those buyers already gets an acceptable workflow from PumpDocket, ServiceCore, or a required municipal portal;
- the chosen city confirms acceptance of the generated electronic record and signatures;
- one disposal facility or municipal program provides stable receiving-field requirements;
- fewer than 10% of pilot records require office correction after driver entry; and
- support plus form maintenance can be delivered profitably below $100/month per customer.

### 12. Verdict

**Reject.** The problem is real and technically buildable, but the proposed solution duplicates current pumping software and municipal manifest platforms. The Google Trends growth claim is unverified, the national regulatory framing is too broad, and no direct paid-demand evidence supports the OCR wedge. Do not create `idea-grill/trapmanifest.md`. Reconsider only if a single-jurisdiction paid pilot exposes one repeated workflow failure that PumpDocket, ServiceCore, and the local portal do not solve.
