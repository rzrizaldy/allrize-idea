**Verdict:** Reject

## Summary

ModelSignoff would turn a model-version change into a client-readable approval packet: replay a fixed test set against the current and candidate models, show behavior/cost/latency differences, collect exception decisions, and record client approval before deployment. The launch of Claude Fable 5.1 makes model migration salient, and regression risk is real. However, the core testing work is already well covered by mature open-source and hosted evaluation products. The remaining client-signoff layer looks like a feature or service template, not a defensible standalone solo SaaS. Do not build it unless agencies first pay for a manual version of the exact artifact.

## Inference

- Model releases create recurring migration work, but the Google Trends spike is event-driven and says nothing about agency budgets.
- A client-readable acceptance record could reduce ambiguity between an agency and its client about known regressions, approved tradeoffs, and deployment responsibility.
- The narrow sign-off feature is technically feasible for one strong developer, but incumbents already own the evaluation data and release workflow. They can add approval state more cheaply than a new vendor can acquire and integrate the same data.
- The likely initial product is closer to a productized consulting deliverable built on promptfoo than an independent SaaS category.

## Assumptions

- Small US agencies change foundation models often enough to need a repeatable client approval process.
- Clients will review and value a structured sign-off packet rather than approving in email, a ticket, or a shared document.
- Agencies would pay at least $99 per migration or $149 per month despite free evaluation tooling.
- Test prompts and outputs can be handled with acceptable privacy and contractual controls by a small vendor.

## Consulting assessment

### Trend durability

The Fable 5.1 search spike is short-lived launch attention. The durable underlying trend is frequent model turnover and the resulting need to validate behavior, cost, latency, and safety before migration. Existing platform documentation and active evaluation projects support that underlying workflow, but not demand for a separate sign-off product.

### Customer and JTBD

**Motion:** B2B. A small AI implementation agency or specialized consultancy pays. Engineers run comparisons; the delivery lead chooses exceptions and sends the packet; a client product owner, compliance lead, or operations owner reviews and signs. The job is: “Show what changed, document accepted exceptions, and prove who approved the model upgrade before it reaches production.”

### Pain evidence

Model updates can alter refusals, tool calls, tone, cost, and latency. Teams already discuss behavioral regressions that normal deterministic tests miss. The unverified part is whether agency-client approval—not evaluation itself—is painful and frequent enough to fund a standalone tool.

### Alternatives

- promptfoo: open-source cross-model evals, assertions, CI/CD, reports, and a shareable web viewer.
- DeepEval / Confident AI: open-source evaluation framework plus hosted collaboration.
- Braintrust: datasets, scorers, comparisons, observability, and collaboration with a free tier.
- LangSmith and ordinary GitHub pull requests: evaluation traces and existing review gates.
- Manual workaround: export results to a slide, spreadsheet, PDF, ticket, or email and record approval there.

### Wedge

The proposed wedge is not “another eval platform.” It is a white-labeled, immutable client acceptance packet tied to the model IDs, prompt/test-set hashes, exceptions, approver, and timestamp. That is narrow and understandable, but insufficiently validated and easy for incumbents or general approval tools to reproduce.

### MVP

A solo developer could build a web app that imports promptfoo JSON, stores only redacted metrics and selected examples, creates a current-versus-candidate comparison, lets reviewers approve or reject individual regressions, captures an electronic acknowledgment, and exports a signed PDF/JSON receipt. Stripe could charge per packet without requiring deep provider integrations. No generative AI is required.

### AI role

AI should not make the release decision. An optional model-specific judge could suggest clusters of changed behavior, but deterministic assertions and named human approval must remain authoritative. This keeps the product from becoming a generic AI wrapper.

### Pricing

Hypothesis only: $99 per migration packet or $149/month for five active client workspaces, with model API costs passed through. The lower-risk validation offer is a $199 manual “model upgrade acceptance pack” assembled from the agency's existing eval output.

### Distribution

Start with AI implementation agencies found through Anthropic, OpenAI, and automation-consultant directories and communities. Offer a migration checklist plus a manual packet for the current release. A repeatable path would require at least three agencies to pay and then request a second packet for another client or model release.

### Risks

- **Market:** approval may be rare, informal, or included in existing agency delivery work.
- **Product:** importing results without owning test execution produces a thin feature; owning execution recreates crowded eval platforms.
- **Technical:** provider/model churn, non-determinism, large test sets, and reproducibility complicate trustworthy comparisons.
- **Legal/security:** packets may contain client data, proprietary prompts, regulated content, or implied warranties about model safety.
- **Go-to-market:** engineers can use promptfoo for free; agency buyers may prefer their existing ticketing and document stack.

### Kill criteria

Reject permanently if fewer than three of 15 targeted US AI agencies pay $199 for a manual packet, if fewer than two paying agencies need a second sign-off within 90 days, or if interviews show that clients accept upgrades through existing PR/ticket workflows without dispute. Reconsider only after paid evidence demonstrates repeat use and that the approval artifact—not eval setup consulting—is the purchased value.
