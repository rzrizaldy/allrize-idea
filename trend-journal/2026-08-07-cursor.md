# FIFAVault — NY/CA/IL hiring-party freelance contract & payment evidence vault — 2026-08-07

**Contributor:** Cursor  
**File:** `trend-journal/2026-08-07-cursor.md`  
**Market:** United States first (New York launch; California and Illinois as near-term expansion); Indonesia relevance: not yet established  
**Motion:** B2B. The hiring party (agency, production company, brand marketing team, or SMB that regularly retains freelancers) pays; producers, producers’ assistants, AP clerks, and ops leads use it. Freelancers receive contracts and payment status but are not the initial payer. DCWP / NY AG / state labor agencies are enforcement intermediaries, not customers.  
**Verdict:** Watch

## Summary

Explore **FIFAVault**: a Stripe-monetizable web app that helps US hiring parties (starting with New York creative/production firms) generate statute-required freelance contracts, deliver and retain them for the mandated period, track the $800 / 120-day aggregation threshold, and surface payment-due deadlines so late/non-payment does not become a DCWP or AG case. The workflow is real and durable: NYC’s Freelance Isn’t Free Act has years of enforcement (4,832 complaints; >$3.5M recovered), statewide NYS FIFA (Aug 2024) adds six-year retention and AG enforcement, and February 2026’s Splashlight settlement ($528,817 / 350 freelancers) shows pattern-of-practice risk for production companies. California SB 988 and Illinois FWPA extend the same written-contract + timely-payment pattern nationally.

This remains **Watch**, not Build. Free official model contracts (NY DOL, Illinois DOL) and Freelancers Union’s Contract Creator already cover generation; Legal Chain and other AI/template tools sell drafting; Bill.com / QuickBooks / agency production tools already own payment execution. No primary interviews yet show hiring parties will pay $49–$99/mo for a retention+deadline vault instead of Drive + spreadsheet + free PDF. Prefer Watch until prepaid demand for the **hiring-party evidence vault** (not another contract PDF generator) is proven.

### New York statute and official tooling

- New York State’s Freelance Isn’t Free Act (General Business Law Article 44-A) took effect **August 28, 2024**. NY DOL publishes a model Freelance Worker Agreement and directs freelancers to the Attorney General for complaints: [NY DOL FIFA page](https://dol.ny.gov/freelance-isnt-free-act) · [model PDF](https://dol.ny.gov/system/files/documents/2024/08/freelance-worker-agreement.pdf).
- Core hiring-party duties (written contract with named elements; timely payment by contract date or within 30 days if silent; anti-retaliation; NYS six-year retention with delivery of a copy to the freelancer) are summarized in contemporaneous counsel alerts and the statute’s private-right / AG enforcement structure: [Sher Tremonte client alert, 2026-06-23](https://shertremonte.com/2026/06/23/client-alert-new-yorks-freelance-isnt-free-acts-what-businesses-need-to-know/) · [Justia §1414 violations](https://law.justia.com/codes/new-york/gbs/article-44-a/1414/).
- NYC Local Law 140 (effective May 15, 2017) remains in force in parallel inside the five boroughs: written contract, timely payment, anti-retaliation; DCWP complaint / navigation program: [DCWP FIFA page](https://www.nyc.gov/site/dca/about/freelance-isnt-free-act.page).

### Enforcement / pain evidence (primary)

- On **February 24, 2026**, NYC DCWP announced a **$528,817** settlement with Splashlight (NYC creative production) for systematic late/non-payment under the city FIFA, covering **350** freelancers. DCWP’s own release states that since 2017 the agency has received **4,832** complaints, closed **4,394** cases, and helped freelancers recover **over $3,526,572**: [DCWP press release](https://www.nyc.gov/site/dca/news/021-26/dcwp-wins-500-000-restitution-freelancers-holds-production-company-accountable-for).
- Counsel summarizing the same enforcement trajectory (nearly 5,000 NYC complaints; >$3.5M recovered; ~86% of 2019–2023 DCWP complaints alleging late/non-payment; no reported NYS AG FIFA actions yet as of mid-2026) aligns with the DCWP primary numbers: [Sher Tremonte](https://shertremonte.com/2026/06/23/client-alert-new-yorks-freelance-isnt-free-acts-what-businesses-need-to-know/). Treat the 86% figure as counsel-reported aggregation of agency data, not independently re-tabulated here.

### Multi-state durability (same job, different thresholds)

- California Freelance Worker Protection Act (SB 988), effective **January 1, 2025**: written contract for professional-services freelancers at **$250+** (single or 120-day aggregate); payment by contract date or ≤30 days; **≥4-year** retention: [bill text](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/billNavClient.xhtml?bill_id=202320240SB988) · [Buchalter summary](https://www.buchalter.com/insights/the-freelance-worker-protection-act/).
- Illinois Freelance Worker Protection Act, effective **July 1, 2024**: **$500+** threshold (single or 120-day aggregate); written contract; timely payment; IDOL publishes multilingual model contracts: [IDOL FWPA](https://labor.illinois.gov/laws-rules/legal/freelance-worker-protection-act.html) · [820 ILCS 193](https://www.ilga.gov/legislation/ilcs/ilcs3.asp?ActID=4441&ChapterID=68).

## Inference

- The durable shift is a **multi-state wave of freelance written-contract + timely-payment statutes**, not a search spike. NYC’s multi-year complaint volume and 2026 Splashlight settlement show that pattern-of-practice late payment is an enforceable, expensive failure mode for creative production firms.
- The defendable wedge is **not** “AI writes a freelance contract.” Free DOL PDFs and Freelancers Union already do drafting. The wedge is a **hiring-party operating system**: required-field gate before work starts, delivery receipt, six-year (NY) / four-year (CA) vault, 120-day aggregation counter, payment-due countdown with AP export, and a complaint-response packet (contracts + payment ledger) when DCWP/AG inquire.
- Creative production, media, marketing agencies, and event firms that onboard many one-person LLCs/sole freelancers per month are the sharpest beachhead; general SMBs with 1–2 freelancers/year will stick to free PDFs.
- Solo-developer MVP is feasible: multi-tenant web app, PDF/HTML contract from statute field schema, e-sign or email delivery with timestamp, Stripe Billing, calendar reminders, CSV AP export. AI should only map free-text scopes into itemized services and flag missing statutory fields—not invent payment terms or classify worker employment status.
- Indonesia is not a v1 market; no verified analogue statute or buyer channel was researched for this entry.

## Assumptions

- A meaningful segment of NY/CA/IL hiring parties with ≥10 freelance engagements/quarter will pay a subscription rather than use Drive + free model PDFs after seeing Splashlight-scale headlines.
- Production coordinators (not only counsel) will be allowed to run the tool and will actually refuse to start work without a completed checklist.
- $49–$99/mo or ~$5–$8 per executed contract is credible versus double-damages / settlement risk (unverified WTP).
- The product can avoid unauthorized practice of law by staying in checklist + retention + reminders, with human-approved templates and clear “not legal advice” framing (counsel review required before launch claims).
- Indirect hiring through staffing/production vendors does not make “who is the hiring party” so ambiguous that the vault’s buyer cannot be identified (case law remains unsettled per counsel alerts).

## Consulting assessment

### Trend durability

Statutory and municipal enforcement since 2017 (NYC), 2024 (NY state, Illinois), and 2025 (California) create a durable compliance workflow. Durability rests on law and agency complaint channels, not on Google Trends (unavailable here). Risk: if hiring parties treat FIFA as a rare litigation event rather than an ops process, subscription churn will be high outside creative hubs.

### Customer and JTBD

**Motion:** B2B.  
**Buyer:** owner, head of production, or ops lead at a US creative/production/marketing firm (or multi-state SMB) that regularly retains solo freelancers.  
**User:** producers, coordinators, AP clerks.  
**JTBD:** before a covered engagement starts, issue a statute-complete written contract, prove delivery, schedule payment by the contractual (or 30-day default) date, retain the record for the statutory window, and produce the packet if DCWP/AG/freelancer counsel asks.

### Pain evidence

DCWP’s own stats (4,832 complaints; >$3.5M recovered) and the Splashlight $528,817 / 350-freelancer settlement are strong process-pain and consequence evidence for **late payment**. Missing-contract statutory damages and NYS six-year retention presumption add documentation pain. What is still missing is **willingness-to-pay evidence from hiring parties** for software versus free PDFs and existing AP tools.

### Wedge

v1: **New York City creative production & marketing firms** with ≥15 freelance engagements/month. Features limited to: NYS+NYC required-element checklist (starting from DOL model fields), email/e-sign delivery receipt, six-year vault, 120-day $800 aggregation banner, payment-due board with CSV export to AP. Explicitly **not** a payroll product, **not** worker-classification advisor, **not** a freelancer marketplace, **not** auto-pay.

### MVP

- Org + seat auth; freelancer roster (name, mailing address, email).
- Contract builder enforcing statutory fields; PDF export; send link + timestamped accept/acknowledge.
- Engagement dashboard: status (draft / delivered / in progress / payment due / paid / retained).
- Reminder emails at T-7 / T-1 / overdue relative to contract payment date or completion+30.
- 120-day running total per freelancer pair; warn before threshold without a written contract.
- Export “complaint response pack” (PDF zip of contracts + payment log).
- Stripe Checkout/Billing: monthly subscription.

### AI role

Optional: draft itemized service lines from a producer’s Slack/email brief; lint contracts for missing statutory elements; summarize payment-status for a DCWP response. Must **not** decide employment vs contractor status, invent addresses, or auto-approve waived rights. Durable value is checklist + vault + deadlines.

### Pricing

**Hypothesis, unverified:** $79/mo Studio (up to 40 contracts), $149/mo Agency (unlimited fair-use + multi-seat), or $9 per executed contract with $29/mo base. Concierge validation: $199 to migrate last 90 days of freelance engagements into a compliant vault and run one payment-deadline audit.

### Distribution

- SEO / content: “Splashlight settlement freelance payment checklist,” “NY DOL model contract digital vault,” “DCWP 20-day response packet.”
- Partnerships: NY bookkeepers serving creative firms; Freelancers Union-adjacent education (hire-side tooling, carefully positioned); local production Facebook/Slack groups.
- Outbound to NYC production companies listed in trade directories after counsel-approved messaging.
- Expand packs to CA SB 988 and IL FWPA only after NY paid retention is proven.
- Do not lead with Indonesia.

### Risks

- **Market:** free official PDFs + existing AP tools may be “good enough” until a complaint arrives; buyers may be the parties least motivated to buy until forced.
- **Product:** without payment confirmation hooks (ACH/Stripe payout marks), deadline tracking stays manual and weak.
- **Legal:** UPL risk if templates are marketed as guaranteeing compliance; unsettled “hiring party” definitions for subcontracted creatives.
- **Go-to-market:** concentrated in NY/CA creative metros; slow SMB education outside those hubs.
- **Technical:** six-year retention, PII security, e-sign audit logs.

### Kill criteria

Stop after **20** qualified hiring-party interviews unless **≥7** report a missed written contract, late payment, or DCWP/AG inquiry in the last 24 months **and** **≥4** prepay the $199 concierge vault audit or commit to ≥$79/mo. Stop if targets consistently say the DOL PDF + Bill.com already closes the job. Stop if counsel concludes the product cannot ship without bar-regulated legal advice the solo founder cannot support. Stop if a well-funded incumbent launches a hiring-party FIFA vault at ≤$49/mo with distribution into creative agencies before validation completes.
