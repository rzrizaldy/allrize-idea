# EventLedger — 2026-08-28

**Contributor:** ChatGPT/Codex
**File:** `trend-journal/2026-08-28-chatgpt.md`
**Market:** United States first; Indonesia relevance: not yet established
**Motion:** B2C with a professional referral channel. U.S. prediction-market traders pay and use the product; CPAs and enrolled agents can receive the evidence packet and refer clients.
**Verdict:** Watch

## Summary

EventLedger would import Kalshi, Robinhood event-contract, Polymarket, and wallet activity; reconcile fills, fees, settlements, transfers, and platform statements; flag missing or duplicate records; and produce a classification-neutral evidence packet for a tax professional. It would not tell the customer whether activity is gambling income, capital gain, or a Section 1256 contract. The market and pain are growing quickly, and public APIs make a focused MVP feasible, but tax characterization remains unsettled and Awaken, Koinly, CoinLedger, and specialist reconciliation firms already cover meaningful portions of the job. The opportunity should be validated with paid manual reconciliations before software is built.

## Inference

- Prediction-market tax reporting starts as a data-reconciliation problem before it becomes a legal characterization problem. A neutral ledger can create value without pretending to settle unsettled tax law.
- The sharpest wedge is cross-platform event-contract reconciliation, especially when a user trades through Kalshi directly, Robinhood's event-contract interface, and an on-chain venue. Generic crypto-tax software is more likely to mishandle the economic link between outcome tokens, settlement, and platform statements.
- A one-time annual purchase fits the seasonal job better than a monthly subscription. Annual repurchase and CPA referrals could support retention, but consumers with small gains will be price-sensitive.
- API churn and changing platform statements may create more maintenance than the small initial price suggests.

## Assumptions

- A material segment of U.S. traders uses more than one prediction-market venue or cannot reconcile its platform statement without help.
- Traders or their preparers will pay $49–$79 for a neutral evidence packet even when the product does not select a tax position or file a return.
- Robinhood event-contract exports expose enough identifiers to reconcile the underlying venue and avoid duplicate counting.
- CPA referral economics can keep blended customer acquisition cost below $20 for a $59 annual product.
- Read-only API credentials and CSV uploads can meet customer security expectations without custody or trading permissions.

## Consulting assessment

### Trend durability

The Google Trends result may be news-driven, but Pew and Reuters document large and rapidly rising trading volume. Tax reconciliation repeats every filing year and becomes harder as trading frequency and venue count rise. The durable shift is credible; consumer willingness to pay for this exact tool is not yet verified.

### Customer and JTBD

**Motion:** B2C with a professional referral channel. **Buyer/user:** U.S. retail prediction-market trader with meaningful or multi-venue activity. **Intermediary:** CPA, enrolled agent, or crypto-tax reconciler who receives the packet and may refer the user. **JTBD:** turn fragmented fills, fees, settlements, deposits, withdrawals, and statements into a complete, traceable ledger that a preparer can classify and file.

### Pain evidence

The IRS classification gap is acknowledged by tax-software firms and specialist CPAs, while a tax economist with a six-figure gain publicly remained unsure about treatment. The pain is high for large traders, but collected evidence does not establish how many ordinary users have enough complexity to pay.

### Alternatives

- Platform statements and manual spreadsheets.
- General crypto-tax software: Awaken, Koinly, CoinLedger, CoinTracker, and TokenTax.
- Human reconciliation: Count On Sheep and prediction-market-focused accounting services.
- A user's existing CPA, who may still require clean transaction data before giving tax advice.

The gap is not tax calculation in general. It is an event-contract-specific, cross-venue evidence trail that remains neutral about legal characterization.

### Wedge

Start with Kalshi CSV/API plus one Robinhood event-contract statement. Reconcile contract ticker, side, quantity, fill price, fee, settlement, and cash movement; surface exceptions; attach source-row provenance; and export a CPA packet. Add Polymarket only after the custodial workflow is reliable.

### MVP

- CSV upload for Kalshi and Robinhood event-contract activity.
- Deterministic normalized ledger with duplicate and missing-settlement checks.
- Contract-level gain/loss summaries and source-row drill-down.
- Classification-neutral exports: reconciled CSV, PDF evidence index, and preparer notes field.
- Stripe one-time checkout per tax year.
- No return filing, tax recommendation, wallet write access, or automated amendment.

One strong developer could ship the CSV-first version. Direct APIs and on-chain reconciliation should follow validation, not precede it.

### AI role

AI is optional and subordinate. It may map inconsistent platform descriptions to a likely contract record for human confirmation or summarize unresolved exceptions. Deterministic accounting identities must perform reconciliation. AI must not choose tax treatment or invent missing cost basis.

### Pricing

Hypothesis: **$59 per tax year** for up to 1,000 event-contract transactions; **$129** with manual exception review. This sits above CoinLedger's $49 hobby tier only if event-contract-specific reconciliation is visibly better. A subscription is the wrong initial packaging because usage is seasonal.

### Distribution

First channel: partner with crypto-aware CPAs and enrolled agents using a free client-import checker, paying a fixed referral fee only after purchase. Second channel: high-intent SEO around specific platform exports and tax-document gaps. Do not rely on broad prediction-market content or paid social until conversion is measured.

For B2C economics, target blended CAC below **$20**, first-year gross margin above 75%, and annual repurchase above 35%. Kill paid acquisition if CAC exceeds one-third of first-year revenue. Seasonal churn should be measured as next-tax-year repurchase, not monthly cancellation.

### Risks

- **Market:** large trading volume may be concentrated among few traders; most users may have simple statements or losses too small to justify purchase.
- **Product:** incumbent tax platforms can add first-party Kalshi and Robinhood importers.
- **Technical:** API migrations, renamed contracts, wallet transfers, and partial fills complicate reconciliation.
- **Legal:** marketing or report labels could be mistaken for tax advice; the product needs careful scope, disclaimers, and preparer-controlled classification.
- **Security:** tax and financial records require encryption, deletion controls, and minimal credential scope.
- **Go-to-market:** tax SEO is crowded and highly seasonal; professional referrals may demand support or revenue share that destroys low-ticket economics.

### Kill criteria

- Interview 15 U.S. traders with at least 200 annual event-contract transactions and five tax professionals. Stop if fewer than five traders report unresolved reconciliation work or fewer than three professionals will test a sample packet.
- Sell five manual 2026 reconciliation packets at **$59** before building API connections. Stop if fewer than three purchasers provide usable multi-venue data.
- Stop if Awaken, Koinly, or CoinLedger can already ingest Kalshi plus Robinhood event-contract history and generate an equally traceable exception report at $99 or less.
- Stop if legal review concludes the classification-neutral packet would still constitute regulated tax-return preparation in the intended operating model.

### Verdict

**Watch.** Market growth, documented tax ambiguity, and public APIs justify a paid manual test. Strong incumbents, unsettled classification, security burden, and seasonal B2C acquisition prevent a Build verdict today. Do not create an `idea-grill` file.
