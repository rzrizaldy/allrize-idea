## Summary

Lithium cells and batteries offered for transport must be of a type that passed UN Manual of Tests and Criteria §38.3 design tests, and manufacturers and subsequent distributors must make a standardized **Test Summary** available on request to others in the supply chain. That duty is durable, mechanical, and independent of any single news cycle. The proposed product is narrow: a Stripe-billed web vault that maps each SKU/ASIN to its Test Summary PDF, checks required summary fields for completeness, and issues a time-limited share link (or ZIP pack) so a solo ops person can answer a carrier or customs request in minutes—not a shipping-label generator, not hazmat classification software, and not a claim that the SaaS makes a battery “legal to ship.”

The attention hook today is real but short-lived: American Airlines Flight 2398 (ATL→DFW, 2026-08-21) had a passenger laptop fire that landed in major outlets on 2026-08-22. That spike raises lithium-battery salience; it does **not** by itself prove prepaid demand for a vault. Supply-side alternatives already cover adjacent jobs at SMB or enterprise prices (CHEMTREC CRITERION quote-only libraries, Sustalium public certificate pages from ~€10/doc, ShipWave hazmat labels at $19/mo, DGI Lithium Battery Wizard at $125/user/year, Labelmaster DGIS). Free Drive/Dropbox still closes the job for tiny catalogs. **Watch**, not Build, until interviews show sellers will pay monthly for share-links + field completeness beyond those options—and until the wedge is proven not to collapse into Amazon TIC Direct Validation workflows that no longer accept seller-uploaded reports for many ASINs.

### Credible web / primary regulatory sources (collected 2026-08-22)

- **PHMSA lithium-battery transport page.** PHMSA states lithium cells/batteries are regulated hazmat under the HMR; shippers must conform when offering them for air, highway, rail, or water; and under **Lithium Battery Test Summaries**, cells/batteries offered for transportation must have passed UN Manual §38.3 design tests, with manufacturers required to make test summary documents available upon request (page text cites effective January 21, 2022 for manufacturer availability of test summaries). Primary URL: <https://www.phmsa.dot.gov/lithiumbatteries> (page content retrieved successfully earlier this run via fetch; some later direct downloads from this environment returned HTTP 403).
- **PHMSA HM-268D electronic emergency-response information (adjacent, same August 2026 package).** Final rule *Hazardous Materials: Reducing Undue Paperwork Burdens to Domestic Carriers*, published 2026-08-04, effective **2026-09-03**, lets domestic carriers/facility operators keep emergency response information electronically rather than as a hard-copy ERG. Federal Register: <https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/08/04/2026-15808/hazardous-materials-reducing-undue-paperwork-burdens-to-domestic-carriers>
- **PHMSA HM-268C Materials of Trade lithium quantities (adjacent).** Final rule *Hazardous Materials: Reducing Burdens on Domestic Companies Using Battery-Powered Equipment in Trades*, published 2026-08-04, effective **2026-09-03**, raises MOT lithium-battery quantity limits for trades (construction, landscaping, etc.). Federal Register: <https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/08/04/2026-15823/hazardous-materials-reducing-burdens-on-domestic-companies-using-battery-powered-equipment-in-trades>
- **American Airlines Flight 2398 laptop fire (2026-08-21; reported 2026-08-22).** BBC: passenger laptop fire on AA2398 ATL–DFW; one passenger treated at airport; pilot ATC audio described cabin fire and possible burns; airline confirmed smoke from a customer device during landing. <https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/ce979zpjj85o> CBS and others independently covered the same event and cited FAA lithium-battery thermal-runaway guidance and year-to-date incident tallies (treat FAA counts as secondary unless pulled from FAA’s own tables).
- **Amazon Seller Central lithium / portable-power TIC Direct Validation (seller-forum primary threads).** Amazon staff posts state that under Direct Validation, an approved TIC provider must complete review and upload results; Amazon no longer accepts documents submitted by sellers for manual review for affected ASINs. Portable power / lithium policy threads also restate UN 38.3 Test Summary requirements alongside SDS and related docs. ## Inference

- The **Test Summary availability duty** is a durable logistics rule, not a 2026 fad. Cabin-fire news and PHMSA’s September 2026 MOT/e-ERG deregulatory package raise lithium salience but point in different product directions (passenger device safety vs trades MOT vs carrier ERG). SumLink should stay on the **document-availability** job.
- The open wedge—if any—is **not** “AI writes a Test Summary” (dangerous and likely false) and **not** another DG label printer (ShipWave/Labelmaster/DGI). It is: **SKU↔summary mapping + required-field completeness checklist + audited share link / request log** for sellers who already hold manufacturer PDFs and get blocked at tender.
- Amazon TIC Direct Validation **shrinks** the Amazon-upload wedge: for many battery ASINs the lab uploads, not the seller. Multi-channel sellers (Shopify + wholesale + non-Amazon marketplaces + forwarders) still need on-demand production of the same PDF. That is the beachhead to test.
- CHEMTREC’s quote-only CRITERION and Sustalium’s €10 pages imply the job is monetizable in principle; they also imply SumLink must win on **price transparency + SKU ops UX for 5–200 SKU catalogs**, not on inventing Test Summaries.
- Indonesia is a **secondary export** angle only if factories already produce UN 38.3 documentation for US-bound goods and need English share links for US partners—distribution and WTP unverified.

## Consulting assessment

### Trend durability

**Durable core:** UN 38.3 testing + Test Summary availability along the lithium supply chain (PHMSA lithium batteries hub). Device density and e-commerce SKU counts keep the document job alive regardless of any single flight incident.

**Non-durable spike:** AA2398 laptop fire coverage (2026-08-21/22) and any Trending Now echo. Use for distribution copy only after interviews, not as demand proof.

**Adjacent deregulation (effective 2026-09-03):** electronic ERG and higher MOT lithium limits ease paper burdens for carriers and trades; they do not remove Test Summary duties for commercial lithium product shippers.

### Customer and JTBD

- **Motion:** B2B.
- **Buyer:** Owner/ops of a US Shopify/Amazon/wholesale brand shipping power banks, cordless tools, IoT devices, e-bike parts, or replacement packs (roughly 5–200 lithium SKUs).
- **User:** Whoever answers “send us the UN 38.3 Test Summary for this SKU” under a tender deadline.
- **JTBD:** “When FedEx/UPS/the forwarder/customs asks for the Test Summary in the next few hours, give me a complete, shareable pack tied to the exact SKU—without digging through email threads or sending the wrong revision.”

### Pain evidence

- Regulatory: PHMSA’s public lithium page ties transport to §38.3 tests and on-request Test Summaries.
- Operational: Amazon Seller Central threads show sellers blocked on dangerous-goods / portable-power queues when UN 38.3 / TIC steps are incomplete; Direct Validation adds lab latency outside the seller’s control.
- Incident salience: AA2398 demonstrates why carriers and platforms keep tightening lithium controls (passenger harm + FAA incident tracking cited in press).
- Gap: this run has **no** primary interviews, invoice losses, or prepaid waitlist. Pain is process-real; WTP is unproven.

### Wedge

**Only:** “SKU-linked UN 38.3 Test Summary vault with completeness checklist and time-limited share link + request log for US SMB lithium sellers (non-Amazon or multi-channel first).” Explicitly **out of scope** for v1: IATA DGD generation, SoC sensors, training certificates, “AI compliance,” or claiming Amazon TIC replacement.

### MVP

Solo-dev Stripe web app (~2–4 weeks):

1. Auth + org; upload Test Summary PDFs; map to SKU / ASIN / supplier model.
2. Checklist of required Test Summary elements (from PHMSA/UN guidance) with pass/fail on extracted or manually confirmed fields.
3. One-click **expiring share link** + optional ZIP; email/SMS copy for carrier.
4. Request log (who asked, which SKU, which revision, when shared).
5. Stripe Checkout: $39/mo up to N SKUs or $99/mo higher cap; no usage AI fees required.
6. Human attestation: “I confirm this PDF came from the manufacturer/lab for this design.”

### AI role

**Allowed:** OCR + structured extraction to pre-fill checklist fields; flag missing elements; suggest which uploaded file matches a SKU from filename/model strings.  
**Forbidden:** Generating fake Test Summaries, auto-signing, or “guaranteeing” air eligibility. Generic chatbot wrappers fail the scout bar.

### Pricing

Hypothesis (unvalidated): **$39/mo** (≤50 SKUs) / **$99/mo** (≤250 SKUs), annual discount. Anchors: ShipWave hazmat $19 (different job), DGI Wizard $125/user/year (classification), Sustalium from €10/doc, CHEMTREC opaque enterprise. Kill if buyers only want one-off $20 uploads with no retention.

### Distribution

1. SEO/content for “UN 38.3 test summary request carrier” + Shopify lithium shipping guides.  
2. Amazon Seller / FBA communities (careful: not spam; lead with free checklist PDF).  
3. Freight-forwarder and 3PL partner pages (“send your merchant this link”).  
4. Post-incident PR only after product exists—do not build on AA2398 alone.

Indonesia later: English share links for factories exporting to US importers; acquire via freight forwarders in Jakarta/Surabaya—untested.

### Risks

- **Market:** Drive is “good enough”; Sustalium/CHEMTREC already sell shareable libraries.  
- **Product:** Sellers lack authentic PDFs; vault cannot invent them.  
- **Amazon:** TIC Direct Validation removes self-serve upload for many ASINs.  
- **Legal:** Users may treat the app as legal advice or as proof of shippability; need hard disclaimers.  
- **Technical:** PDF OCR quality varies; false “complete” checks create liability optics.  
- **GTM:** Hazmat audiences buy training and labels first; document vault may be a weak secondary purchase.

### Kill criteria

Stop or archive if any of:

1. 10 problem interviews fail to find at least 3 sellers who paid money or at least 4 hours in the last year chasing Test Summaries.  
2. A prepaid landing page (Stripe waitlist) gets fewer than 5 deposits of ≥$39 in 30 days after targeted outreach.  
3. Sustalium, CHEMTREC, ShipWave, or Amazon ships a **SKU vault + share link** at ≤$39/mo that interviews say closes the job.  
4. Counsel concludes storing/sharing Test Summaries creates unacceptable hazmat-advice exposure for a solo operator without partnering under a registered provider.

### Verdict

**Watch.** Durable regulation + clear JTBD + solo-buildable MVP, but incumbents and free folders occupy the job, Amazon’s TIC path narrows one channel, Trends durability is unmeasured here, and WTP is unproven. Do **not** promote to `idea-grill/` until kill-criteria tests flip.
