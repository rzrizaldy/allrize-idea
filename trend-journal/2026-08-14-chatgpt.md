**Verdict:** Watch

## Summary

**SilicaRoster** is a narrow, bilingual compliance-evidence workspace for small California countertop-fabrication shops. It does not teach safety through an AI chatbot and does not claim that software makes a workplace compliant. It keeps the evidence behind a shop's silica program coherent: high-exposure-task roster, English/Spanish training acknowledgments, exposure-control-plan version, respirator and medical-surveillance due dates, certificate uploads, and an inspector-ready annual attestation packet. The timing is real: California SB 20 requires annual electronic attestation beginning July 1, 2026. The pain is unusually severe, but paid demand remains unverified and existing free/generic tools overlap enough that this stays Watch.

### Regulation and workflow

- The enacted [California SB 20 text](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/billNavClient.xhtml?bill_id=202520260SB20) says:
  - dry methods are prohibited for high-exposure trigger tasks and effective wet methods are required;
  - shop owners must ensure affected employees receive Section 5204 training;
  - **beginning July 1, 2026 and annually thereafter**, employers must electronically submit a written attestation that each affected employee was trained;
  - knowingly false attestations are unlawful and can lead to citations and civil penalties.
- [Cal/OSHA's silica resources, updated July 29, 2026](https://www.dir.ca.gov/dosh/respiratory-silica-FAQ.html), list the operative evidence areas: exposure assessment, regulated areas, engineering controls, written exposure-control program, respirators, housekeeping, medical surveillance/removal, hazard communication, reporting, and recordkeeping.
- Federal [29 CFR 1910.1053](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.1053) requires a written exposure-control plan, at-least-annual plan review, medical surveillance for qualifying workers, training, and accurate exposure/objective/medical records.
- [Cal/OSHA enforcement data](https://www.dir.ca.gov/DIRNews/2024/2024-111.html) reported that 26% of inspected shops received Orders Prohibiting Use and 53 of 56 closed inspections, approximately 95%, resulted in citations.
- A 2026 [NIOSH bulletin](https://www.cdc.gov/niosh/bulletin/2026/engineered-stone-cos.html) identified 653 California fabricators, 480 with fewer than 10 employees. Its broader national discovery list contained 19,316 companies, although that list is not a verified count of active fabricators.
- The official [OSHA silica overview](https://www.osha.gov/silica-crystalline) provides free English/Spanish guides, sample training material, and countertop hazard alerts.

### Alternatives and technical evidence

- [CPWR Silica-Safe Create-A-Plan](https://www.silica-safe.org/plan-overview/) already generates exposure-control plans for free and lets registered users save and reuse them.
- [Ecesis silica compliance calendar](https://www.ecesis.net/Compliance-Calendar-Software/respirable-crystalline-silica-compliance-calendar-software.aspx) already tracks exposure assessments, medical exams, training anniversaries, control reviews, and field documentation.
- Paid silica training can cost as little as [$35 per worker](https://www.hazmatschool.com/oshas-silica-in-construction-course-for-exposed-workers/), while Oregon OSHA offers a free Spanish silica course. Training itself is therefore not a defensible product.
- GitHub search found no credible open-source silica-specific operating system. It did find the generic [contextkits/osha-compliance-assistant](https://github.com/contextkits/osha-compliance-assistant), an API-backed AI compliance-answering tool priced from $79/month, and [Curatrak OSHA Compliance](https://github.com/Curatrak/curatrak-osha-compliance), a multi-agent regulation-retrieval prototype. These reinforce that generic AI advice is crowded and unsuitable as the wedge.
- Founder build fit is high: [LPDP CTRL+F](https://github.com/rzrizaldy/lpdp_univ) demonstrates official-PDF ETL, normalization, validation, and a deployed Flask web app; [RizeBot Hermes](https://github.com/rzrizaldy/rizebot-hermes) demonstrates deterministic schedules, bounded records, retention, audit reports, and human review gates.

## Inference

- The durable demand is not the news spike. It is recurring evidence administration created by federal OSHA plus California's annual SB 20 attestation.
- Small shops are the sharpest wedge because NIOSH found 480 California fabricators with fewer than 10 employees, a segment likely to lack a full EHS platform.
- An inspector-ready evidence bundle may be more valuable than another course or plan generator because the required artifacts span training, plan versions, exposure data, respirator controls, and medical-surveillance status.
- The product must remain a workflow and records system. If it gives legal or medical verdicts, risk rises while differentiation falls.
- California's potential ban on engineered stone above 1% silica may reduce part of the addressable workflow. Natural stone and other covered high-silica materials would remain, but that continuity is not yet sufficient to underwrite Build.

## Assumptions

- At least 30 California shops will pay $299 setup plus $39–$79/month instead of using folders, spreadsheets, a generic LMS, or Ecesis.
- Cal/OSHA's electronic attestation process does not already provide enough roster and evidence management for small shops.
- Industrial hygienists, occupational-health clinics, insurers, training vendors, or stone distributors will refer customers without expensive enterprise partnerships.
- Owners will keep worker rosters and due dates current despite language, trust, and immigration-status concerns.
- The app can avoid storing clinical results and retain only minimum necessary completion dates, provider opinions, and work restrictions.
- A low-silica-material transition will not eliminate enough covered work to make this a short-lived California-only product.

## Consulting assessment

### Trend durability

The headline spike is episodic and tied to the August 12 report. The underlying requirement is more durable: federal silica recordkeeping and annual plan review already exist, and California's attestation repeats annually from July 1, 2026. Durability is moderate, not proven growth.

### Customer and JTBD

- **Buyer/payor:** owner, operations manager, or safety lead at a 2–50-worker countertop fabrication shop.
- **Users:** supervisors and competent persons maintain records; workers acknowledge training in English or Spanish.
- **Intermediaries:** industrial hygienists, occupational-health clinics, silica trainers, workers-comp brokers, and stone-industry associations can refer or bundle the product.
- **JTBD:** “Before I attest or an inspector arrives, show me which covered workers, tasks, controls, training, respirator items, surveillance dates, and supporting documents are missing—without buying an enterprise EHS suite.”

### Pain evidence

- Annual attestation is legally required and false statements are unlawful.
- 95% of closed Cal/OSHA silica inspections cited violations; 26% of inspected shops received temporary stop-work orders.
- The affected population includes very small employers, while the evidence spans several systems and long-lived records.
- The human harm is severe: 592 California cases, 65 transplants, and 31 deaths reported through June 2026.

No direct owner interview, paid pilot, cancellation record, or spreadsheet screenshot was found. Compliance urgency is verified; willingness to pay is not.

### Alternatives

1. Paper binders, Google Drive, spreadsheets, calendar reminders, and a generic LMS.
2. Free CPWR exposure-plan generation and official English/Spanish OSHA material.
3. Ecesis for silica-specific recurring deadlines and field records.
4. Enterprise EHS suites such as VelocityEHS.
5. Generic AI compliance assistants, which answer questions but do not prove that a shop implemented controls.

The gap is a low-cost, countertop-specific evidence workflow, but its breadth overlaps Ecesis and its simplest form can be reproduced with Google Workspace.

### Wedge

Start with **the annual SB 20 attestation evidence pack for California shops with fewer than 20 employees**:

- employee and high-exposure-task roster;
- English/Spanish acknowledgment;
- third-party training-certificate upload;
- exposure-control-plan version and annual-review date;
- respirator/fit-test and surveillance completion-status dates;
- missing-evidence checklist;
- immutable export with source-file hashes and a human sign-off step.

Do not submit the attestation for the employer. Produce a review packet and link the user to the official submission surface.

### MVP

A solo-developer web app can ship in four to six weeks:

1. organization, locations, roles, and worker roster;
2. deterministic obligation and due-date rules;
3. certificate/document upload with version history;
4. bilingual acknowledgment link suitable for a shared phone;
5. dashboard of missing/expiring evidence;
6. PDF/ZIP inspection packet and audit log;
7. Stripe Checkout for setup and subscription;
8. minimum-data design: no diagnosis, X-ray, spirometry, immigration, or full medical report storage.

### AI role

AI is optional and subordinate. OCR can propose names, dates, course titles, and provider names from certificates or air-monitoring PDFs. Deterministic validation and a human confirmation gate must control every saved field. AI must not decide compliance, diagnose disease, interpret medical results, or generate the legal attestation.

### Pricing

Pricing hypothesis:

- **$299 one-time setup:** roster import, current-document map, bilingual acknowledgment launch, and first evidence pack.
- **$49/month per shop:** up to 15 covered workers, reminders, audit log, and annual packet.
- **$99/month:** up to 50 workers and multi-location roll-up.
- No per-worker course fee; integrate certificates from whichever provider the shop uses.

The setup-first offer tests willingness to pay before subscription code. Gross margin should be high, but the California serviceable market may be too small for material scale.

### Distribution

First repeatable path:

1. recruit five independent industrial hygienists or silica trainers serving Southern California;
2. give each a co-branded “inspection packet gap check” landing page;
3. use public shop directories only for targeted outreach, not automated spam;
4. sell the $299 setup before building full automation;
5. later test workers-comp brokers and occupational-health clinics as referral channels.

Indonesia expansion should be deferred. The legal trigger, terminology, reporting route, and enforcement structure are U.S.-specific.

### Risks

- **Market:** 653 identified California fabricators is a narrow initial segment; many are microbusinesses.
- **Regulatory:** an engineered-stone ban could shrink the specific workflow; state forms or portals can change.
- **Competition:** CPWR is free; Ecesis already tracks core deadlines; enterprise EHS tools can move downmarket.
- **Behavior:** a dashboard cannot make unsafe wet methods, ventilation, or respirator use effective.
- **Legal:** the product cannot promise compliance or submit a potentially false attestation.
- **Privacy/security:** medical-surveillance metadata is sensitive; collect the minimum and separate worker-facing access.
- **Trust/access:** many affected workers are immigrants; employer-controlled records can create fear or misuse.
- **GTM:** consultants may prefer their existing templates and billable manual process.
- **AI:** OCR errors could corrupt evidence unless every extraction is confirmed.

### Kill criteria

Stop before SaaS build if any of the following occurs:

- fewer than 10 of 30 qualified California shop owners accept a 20-minute evidence-gap interview;
- fewer than three shops pay a refundable **$299 setup deposit** within 30 days;
- more than half of interviewees say the official attestation portal, their training provider, or Ecesis already solves the evidence problem;
- an industrial hygienist says the proposed packet omits artifacts essential to real inspections;
- legal review concludes the workflow would create unacceptable attestation or medical-record liability;
- customer acquisition requires more than $600 per shop at a $49/month price;
- proposed California rulemaking eliminates most covered fabrication with no adjacent natural-stone demand.

### Verdict

**Watch.** The regulatory trigger, enforcement rate, harm, buyer, and buildability are unusually concrete. The unresolved questions are paid demand, a defensible gap versus Ecesis/free tools, and whether the potential material ban collapses the wedge. The next action is three paid setup deposits—not application development and not an `idea-grill` promotion.
