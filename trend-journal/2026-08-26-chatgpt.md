## Executive summary

**GNSS ShiftGate** is a project-aware go/no-go and evidence workflow for small land-surveying and construction-layout firms that depend on centimeter-grade GNSS. It converts official space-weather conditions into crew-specific risk windows, preflight checks, an approved work/pause decision, customer-delay notices, and a retained QA record.

The pain is real: severe ionospheric disturbance can prevent receivers from locking or move reported positions far outside acceptable tolerances. But the evidence does not yet justify Build. NOAA already provides free forecasts and alerts, Trimble provides free planning tools and receiver-side mitigation, and field impact varies by receiver, correction method, location, and time. The initial wedge must therefore be operational accountability—not another space-weather forecast.

## Verified evidence

1. **A current event explains the spike.** NOAA's Space Weather Prediction Center reported on 2026-08-26 that geomagnetic activity from CMEs departing the Sun on August 25 could extend through August 29, with a G1 storm watch for August 27. Its current R1 radio-blackout notice says low-frequency navigation signals may be degraded briefly. [NOAA Space Weather Prediction Center](https://www.spaceweather.gov/)

2. **High-precision GNSS is exposed.** NOAA says high-precision dual-frequency GPS is used in farming, construction, exploration, surveying, and snow removal. During severe disturbance, ordinary GPS errors can increase to tens of meters or more; even dual-frequency receivers may lose satellite lock when the ionosphere is highly disturbed. [NOAA: Space Weather and GPS Systems](https://www.spaceweather.gov/impacts/space-weather-and-gps-systems)

3. **Field crews report operational disruption.** In a surveying-community discussion, one manager said crews were instructed to add checks during a geomagnetic storm. Other practitioners reported receiver lock failures, vertical jumps of several meters, schedule changes, and shifting drone flights to morning. This is direct practitioner testimony, not a representative market survey. [r/Surveying discussion](https://www.reddit.com/r/Surveying/comments/1ovadog/gnss_in_geomagnetic_storms/)

4. **Industry guidance treats this as a planning problem.** An industry publication says high-precision GNSS users report long initialization, poor results, and occasionally inoperable periods, and recommends using space-weather resources to plan field operations. [xyHt](https://www.xyht.com/gnsslocation-tech/solar-cycle-25-and-gnss/)

5. **Free official monitoring already exists.** NOAA provides a GPS community dashboard, forecasts, alerts, watches, warnings, Total Electron Content data, and other products. [NOAA GPS Community Dashboard](https://www.spaceweather.gov/communities/global-positioning-system-gps-community-dashboard)

6. **Receiver vendors mitigate part of the risk.** Trimble's IonoGuard is a receiver-side technology intended to preserve GNSS positioning through ionospheric disturbance, while Trimble also provides a free GNSS planning tool. Leica/Hexagon recommends multi-constellation, multi-frequency receivers, current firmware, and RTK corrections, while acknowledging that high-precision sensors are not immune. [Trimble IonoGuard](https://oemgnss.trimble.com/blog/oemgnss/en-US/article/trimble-ionoguard-aurora-borealis-geomagnetic-storm), [Trimble GNSS planning](https://positioningservices.trimble.com/en/solar-cycle), [Leica/Hexagon guidance](https://cpec.leica-geosystems.com/blog/equipment/gnss-gps/impact-of-increased-ionospheric-activity-upon-high-precision-gnss/)

7. **The public data is technically accessible.** The open-source [swpclib](https://github.com/tcarwash/swpclib) project is an MIT-licensed Python library for NOAA's Space Weather API. Its sparse feature set demonstrates data-access feasibility, not a product moat or market adoption.

8. **Founder fit is strong.** [Deck of Prosperity](https://github.com/rzrizaldy/deck_of_prosperity) demonstrates React, TypeScript, Vite, Vitest, Playwright, and web deployment. [RizeBot Hermes](https://github.com/rzrizaldy/rizebot-hermes) demonstrates deterministic monitoring, state changes, bounded reports, and human review gates—the core primitives for this product.

## Inference

- The durable job is not “show me solar activity.” It is **“decide whether this crew should perform this GNSS-dependent task, prove what checks were made, and communicate the operational consequence.”**
- NOAA conditions alone cannot establish whether a specific receiver or job will fail. A useful product must combine the external alert with the firm's equipment, location, precision requirement, correction method, and observed check shots.
- Small firms may lack an auditable policy even when crew chiefs monitor space weather informally.
- Trimble and Leica reduce the need for a universal stop-work alert. The wedge is stronger for mixed-equipment firms and high-liability work where a documented decision matters.
- Indonesia could become relevant for surveying, construction, mining, and precision agriculture near the magnetic equator, where NOAA notes ionospheric scintillation can cause dual-frequency receivers to lose lock. It should not be an initial expansion until reliable regional data and buyers are validated.

## Assumptions

- A meaningful segment has experienced at least one costly GNSS-quality incident or lost crew day in the past 12 months.
- NOAA alerts and public ionospheric data are timely enough to trigger a preflight workflow.
- Firms can define deterministic policies by job precision, equipment, correction method, and storm level.
- Crew chiefs will complete a short mobile preflight and attach check-shot evidence.
- A firm will pay **$149 for policy setup** and **$49 per month** for scheduling, evidence, and notifications.
- The product can avoid making accuracy guarantees by recording decisions and observed field checks rather than certifying GNSS performance.

## 1. Trend durability

The exact Google Trends spike is event-driven. Solar activity follows an approximately 11-year cycle, so attention and incident frequency will vary materially over time. Dependence on GNSS is durable, but a product tied only to major solar storms risks low usage and churn. Retention must come from integrating the check into every GNSS job preflight, not from waiting for rare severe events.

## 2. Customer and job to be done

- **Who pays:** a small U.S. land-surveying, construction-layout, drone-mapping, or civil-contractor firm with multiple field crews.
- **Who uses:** operations manager, project surveyor, crew chief, and QA lead.
- **Who receives value:** the general contractor or project owner receives a defensible delay notice or QA record.
- **Intermediaries:** NOAA supplies official space-weather data; GNSS hardware and correction-network vendors supply receiver status and mitigation; project-management or field-software tools receive exported tasks and evidence.

Core job: **Before centimeter-critical work begins, apply the firm's risk policy, verify field conditions, approve or pause the job, and retain evidence of the decision.**

## 3. Pain evidence

Observable pain includes lost receiver lock, unstable vertical positions, restricted work windows, additional control checks, and rescheduled drone or construction work. The cited practitioner thread shows real workarounds, but today's research found no independent estimate of incident frequency, financial loss per firm, or paid demand for a standalone workflow.

## 4. Current alternatives

- NOAA's free alerts, dashboards, and GPS community products
- Trimble's free GNSS planning tool and IonoGuard-enabled hardware
- Receiver firmware, multi-frequency/multi-constellation equipment, local bases, PPK, and conventional survey methods
- Crew texts, spreadsheets, safety briefings, and manual check-shot logs
- Existing field and project-management software

The strongest substitute is an experienced crew chief using free NOAA/vendor data plus standard QA procedures.

## 5. Differentiated wedge

**Space-weather condition → job-specific policy → crew preflight → approved decision → evidence packet.**

The product should not predict raw positioning error. It should:

- map a current alert to scheduled GNSS-dependent jobs;
- apply the firm's own thresholds by task and equipment;
- require appropriate control/check shots;
- capture proceed/pause and reviewer approval;
- generate a plain-language client delay notice;
- retain the source alert, observations, photos/screenshots, and timestamps.

## 6. Solo MVP

A three-to-five-week web MVP could include:

1. Stripe Checkout and one company workspace.
2. CSV/calendar import for jobs, approximate locations, precision class, crew, and equipment.
3. Scheduled polling of NOAA alerts and Kp/TEC products.
4. Deterministic policy rules configured during setup.
5. Mobile crew preflight with check-shot values and evidence upload.
6. Human approve, proceed-with-caution, or pause decision.
7. Client-notice template and exportable QA evidence packet.
8. Audit log and weekly false-alert/usefulness review.

Do not build receiver control, correction services, scientific forecasting, or automatic stop-work commands.

## 7. AI role

AI is optional and subordinate. It may translate technical NOAA language into a crew briefing or draft a customer notice. Risk thresholds, job matching, approval state, and evidence retention must remain deterministic. This is an operations product, not an AI space-weather expert.

## 8. Pricing and Stripe motion

Pricing is an assumption to test:

- **$149 one-time setup:** encode equipment, task classes, thresholds, and preflight templates.
- **$49 per month:** up to three crews, monitoring, decisions, and evidence packets.
- **$99 per month:** additional crews and client-branded reporting.

Stripe Checkout supports both setup purchases and subscriptions. A paid setup should precede software so the founder can learn whether policies are standardized enough for SaaS.

## 9. Distribution

Start with founder-led outreach to 20 small surveying and construction-layout firms in northern U.S. states and firms performing drone, grading, or high-precision stakeout work. Use the current August 27–29 watch as a timely interview prompt without claiming that it will cause local failure.

Offer a manual **GNSS risk-policy setup** and one-week monitoring pilot. Potential repeatable channels are local surveying associations, GNSS equipment dealers, correction-network providers, and survey-software consultants. None is verified yet.

## 10. Risks

- NOAA forecasts may be too geographically broad for a crew-level decision.
- Receiver, base, network, time, latitude, and site conditions dominate actual performance.
- False stop-work recommendations could cost more than the risk avoided.
- Modern hardware may mitigate most events, especially for Trimble-heavy firms.
- Severe events may be too rare to sustain retention after the current solar cycle.
- Incumbent hardware or field-software vendors can add the workflow.
- The product could create liability if marketed as a guarantee of accuracy.
- Evidence uploads may contain sensitive project locations or client information.

## 11. 48-hour validation experiment

Do not code. Create a manual preflight template using the current NOAA watch and interview 15 firm owners, project surveyors, or crew chiefs. Ask for the most recent GNSS-quality failure, equipment used, lost hours or rework, current space-weather checks, who can stop a job, and what evidence clients require.

Sell a **$149 policy-setup pilot** to three firms. Deliver risk thresholds, a crew checklist, one week of alerts, and a sample decision packet manually.

## 12. Kill criteria and verdict

Advance to Build only if all are met:

- At least 8 of 15 firms report changing or losing GNSS field work because of ionospheric conditions in the past year.
- At least 3 firms pay the $149 setup deposit.
- At least 70% of firms lack a documented space-weather/receiver-quality policy.
- The manual workflow produces a useful decision in under three minutes per job.
- Firms accept that alerts are advisory and still value the evidence log.
- A mixed-equipment wedge remains after testing Trimble and existing field-software alternatives.

Kill the idea if fewer than three deposits are collected, if receiver-side mitigation makes the workflow redundant, or if external conditions cannot be mapped to job-level risk without unacceptable false pauses.

**Verdict: Watch.** The pain and build fit are credible, but frequency, job-level predictability, willingness to pay, and incumbent overlap remain unverified. Do not create an `idea-grill` entry.
