# SRP Clock — ENISA Art. 14 reporting runbook for US makers of products with digital elements (2026-09-02)

**Contributor:** Cursor  
**File:** `trend-journal/2026-09-02-cursor.md`  
**Market:** United States first; Indonesia relevance: not yet established (only if a US or Indo manufacturer places PDE on the EU market)  
**Motion:** B2B — a US manufacturer (or branded seller) of products with digital elements that are placed on the EU market pays; a security/PSIRT lead, founder, or assigned representative uses it; ENISA and the coordinating CSIRT receive the official filing via the free SRP  
**Verdict:** **Watch**

## Summary

SRP Clock would be a Stripe-monetizable web desk that helps a US hardware/firmware/software product maker prepare for the EU Cyber Resilience Act’s Article 14 reporting cascade that starts **11 September 2026**: awareness timestamp, 24-hour early warning, 72-hour notification, final report (14 days after a vulnerability fix, or one month after the 72-hour notice for a severe incident), mandatory ENISA SRP field checklists, user-notification drafts, and a tabletop rehearsal pack. The regulatory driver is real and US counsel already flags it for exporters. It is **not** a Build today: the official filing channel is free, ENISA advises registering only when there is something to file, an open-source CLI already drafts Art. 14 packets, and CRA Evidence already markets an “ENISA reporting workflow” inside a broader CRA platform (custom pricing). Treat as Watch until prepaid readiness packs beat spreadsheet + counsel memo + free SRP.

### Credible web / legal sources (collected 2026-09-02)

- European Commission page [Cyber Resilience Act — Reporting obligations](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/cra-reporting): from **11 September 2026**, manufacturers must report actively exploited vulnerabilities and severe incidents; early warning within **24 hours**, full notification within **72 hours**, final report within **14 days** after a corrective measure for vulnerabilities or within **one month** for severe incidents; filing is once through the ENISA Single Reporting Platform (SRP). Page notes functional/security testing of the SRP is under way and references Commission guidance §9.1 and CRA implementation FAQs §5.
- [ENISA SRP FAQ](https://www.enisa.europa.eu/topics/product-security/single-reporting-platform-srp/frequently-asked-questions): platform operational target **11 September 2026**; Assigned Representatives authenticate with **EU Login** (2FA; personal accounts, not shared mailboxes); manufacturers are advised to **register only when they need to submit a notification**; validation by the coordinating CSIRT runs in parallel and does not block filing (up to 20 notifications before validation becomes mandatory); early-warning mandatory field set is small; there is **no public submission API** (browser workflow).
- [ENISA SRP hub](https://www.enisa.europa.eu/topics/product-security/single-reporting-platform-srp) and registration guidance pages (updated early August 2026 per ENISA labels) describe Primary/Secondary AR roles and manufacturer association.
- US-facing counsel summaries confirm extraterritorial reach to US makers selling into the EU: [Gesmer / Mondaq client alert](https://www.mondaq.com/unitedstates/compliance/1820902/client-alert-the-eu-cyber-resilience-act) (reporting from 11 Sep 2026; full conformity 11 Dec 2027; fines up to €15M or 2.5% worldwide turnover for core Art. 13/14 breaches) and [Crowell & Moring countdown alert](https://www.crowell.com/en/insights/client-alerts/eu-cyber-resilience-act-countdown-11-september-2026-incidentvulnerability-reporting-deadline-is-less-than-100-days-away) (no API; on-call staff must populate forms; map EU-marketed PDE; tighten upstream vuln-notification contracts).
- [CRA Evidence](https://craevidence.com/) / [pricing](https://craevidence.com/pricing): positions a full CRA compliance platform including **“ENISA 24h/72h/14d reporting workflow”**, SBOM/VEX, technical files; **Professional/Enterprise = custom pricing**; 14-day free trial, no public Stripe self-serve price published on the pricing page snapshot.
- [CRACheck / SolidwareTools](https://solidwaretools.com/cracheck/cra-authorised-representative-non-eu-manufacturer.html): sells an **€149 one-time** browser-side 8-document technical dossier (Art. 31 / Annex VII territory) and explicitly states Art. 14 ENISA reporting **cannot** be delegated to an authorised representative and remains with the manufacturer.

## Inference

- The durable shift is statutory: Art. 14 reporting applies from 11 Sep 2026 even to many products already on the EU market, while full Annex I conformity waits until Dec 2027. US exporters need a **reachable on-call process** before they need a full CE/SBOM factory.
- The official SRP closes the **filing** job. The remaining pain is operational: decide “aware,” freeze the clock, fill stage-correct fields without leaking premature architecture detail, notify users, and keep an audit trail if market surveillance asks later.
- Event frequency is the SaaS killer: actively exploited product vulns and “severe incidents” are rare for many SMB makers. A monthly subscription is hard to defend versus a **prepaid readiness pack** (tabletop + templates + field map) unless the product expands into ongoing vuln monitoring—which immediately collides with CRA Evidence / SBOM platforms.
- CRA Evidence already claims the ENISA reporting workflow as a feature, but hides price. That leaves a **possible** Stripe wedge for a cheap Art. 14-only desk, not proof of white space.
- Zero-star `cra-scope-cli` shows a solo developer can ship the packet builder; it also shows the core automation is already publishable as free software.

## Assumptions

- A meaningful set of US indie/SMB PDE makers sell into the EU (DTC, Amazon EU, OEM channels) and do **not** already retain EU cybersecurity counsel or a PSIRT retainer.
- Those buyers will pay **$79–$299 once** (or ~$29–$79/mo for a short readiness window) for a runbook/tabletop rather than only reading free ENISA/Commission pages.
- “Awareness” determination and parallel NIS2/GDPR reporting can be productized as checklists without becoming unauthorized legal advice (must be counsel-cleared copy).
- Indonesia expansion is unvalidated; only relevant if an Indo manufacturer places PDE on the EU market and needs the same ENISA workflow in English/Bahasa.

## Consulting assessment

### Trend durability

**Durable regulatory phase-in, weak same-day search spike.** Trends today are entertainment-heavy; CRA did not appear in Trending Now / RSS. Durability rests on Regulation 2024/2847’s hard application dates and counsel alerts to US exporters, not on Google query volume (explore blocked here).

### Customer and JTBD

**Motion:** B2B.  
**Who pays:** founder, Head of Product Security, or compliance owner at a US company that manufactures or brands PDE placed on the EU market (IoT, firmware appliances, on-prem/edge software products—not pure SaaS, which generally sits under NIS2 instead).  
**Who uses:** the Primary/Secondary Assigned Representative candidates, on-call engineer, and counsel reviewing drafts.  
**Recipient:** coordinating CSIRT + ENISA via SRP; users of the product for CRA user-notification duties.  
**JTBD:** when the org first has reasonable certainty of active exploitation or a severe product-security incident, produce stage-correct SRP content and user notices inside the statutory clocks, with a defensible internal record of when the clock started.

### Pain evidence

Counsel alerts emphasize that reporting infrastructure must exist **before** the first incident, that there is no SRP API, and that a single event can trigger CRA + NIS2 + GDPR clocks in parallel. ENISA’s own FAQ advising “register only when you need to file” confirms the portal is not a day-to-day system of record. Organic buyer complaint threads were thin in this pass (mostly vendor and law-firm content)—treat practitioner pain as **inferred from process design**, not from first-person SMB quotes.

### Alternatives

- Free: ENISA SRP + EU Login + Commission guidance §9.1 + CRA implementation FAQs + ENISA AR registration/notification guides.
- Cheap one-off docs (wrong primary job): CRACheck €149 technical dossier.
- Full platform (sales-led): CRA Evidence and peers claiming SBOM + ENISA reporting workflow.
- OSS: `cra-scope-cli` draft builders; readiness scanners; curated awesome lists.
- Enterprise: existing PSIRT / IR platforms, counsel retainers, EU authorised-representative service firms (€3k–8k/yr packages cited on CRACheck marketing—vendor-authored, discount accordingly).
- Manual: Notion/Drive checklist + spreadsheet clock + counsel-reviewed email.

### Wedge

Narrow entry only: **Art. 14 readiness pack for US exporters**—SKU/product inventory for EU-marketed PDE, AR/EU Login prep checklist, awareness-clock + stage field map aligned to ENISA mandatory fields, user-notification draft, and a 90-minute tabletop that produces a filled dry-run packet. Explicitly **not** SBOM generation, CE marking, or Annex I conformity (Dec 2027 crowded lane).

### MVP

Stripe Checkout for a one-time **Readiness Pack** ($149) and optional 30-day **On-Call Desk** ($79): product list, role roster, clock UI, three stage forms with mandatory-field gates, PDF/Markdown export for SRP copy-paste (no unofficial API), user-notice templates, and immutable event log. One strong developer can ship in ~2–3 weeks. AI role limited to turning structured incident notes into draft narrative fields—not a chatbot that “decides” reportability.

### AI role

Workflow-specific drafting only (map notes → early-warning/72h narrative fields; suggest user-facing mitigation language). Reject a generic “CRA compliance AI agent” that pretends to replace Annex I engineering or auto-submits to ENISA.

### Pricing

Hypothesis: **$79–$149 prepaid readiness / tabletop**; optional **$29–$79/mo** for 60–90 days around the Sep 2026 start, then churn to annual light retainer only if the buyer wants continuous drills. If buyers will not prepay without an active incident, economics fail.

### Distribution

US IoT/hardware Slack & Discord communities; Hackaday/EEVblog-adjacent forums; Amazon EU seller groups; “ship to EU” app/firmware indie communities; partnerships with EU authorised-representative boutiques who cannot take Art. 14 off the manufacturer’s plate; SEO around “ENISA SRP US manufacturer” once explore interest is measurable.

### Risks

- **Market:** rare filing events → no retention; buyers wait for first incident and use free SRP.
- **Product:** ENISA field schema / portal UX can change before/after go-live; dry-run fidelity drifts.
- **Legal:** “awareness” and reportability are legal judgments; product copy can be mistaken for advice; parallel NIS2/GDPR missed.
- **Competitive:** CRA Evidence or counsel packages ship a cheap self-serve reporting module; OSS CLI remains “good enough.”
- **GTM:** category attention is EU-heavy; US indie makers may wrongly assume CRA is “Europe’s problem” until a distributor asks.

### Kill criteria

Stop if any of the following hold within 30 days of outreach:

1. Fewer than **10 prepaid** readiness packs at ≥$79 from US PDE exporters (not freeloader EU Login tips).
2. ENISA or Commission ships a complete official dry-run + template pack that makes a third-party desk redundant.
3. CRA Evidence (or peer) publishes transparent **≤$49/mo** self-serve Art. 14-only pricing with the same checklist job.
4. Five target interviews say their only plan is “call counsel / EU AR when something happens” and refuse any prepaid software.
5. Explore Trends (when unblocked) plus customer interviews show **no** US exporter segment that both sells PDE into the EU and lacks an IR retainer.
