## Summary

PackMap Lite is a pre-portal data compiler for small and lower-middle-market consumer brands facing US packaging extended producer responsibility (EPR) reporting. A brand would import its SKU catalog and state-level sales, define each product's packaging components and weights, resolve missing supplier data, and export a dated state-by-state reporting worksheet with an audit trail for review before submission to Circular Action Alliance or a state system. The regulatory workflow is real: Oregon is already collecting producer reports and fees, California's permanent SB 54 regulations became effective May 1, 2026, and Maine says its first full producer reporting will use 2026 packaging data in 2027. Current producer guidance requires packaging weights and state-specific supply data, while recent practitioner discussions describe the exact gap between packaging specifications, SKUs, and destinations. The product is technically feasible and Stripe-monetizable, but Recyda, PCX, rePurpose Global, APR's Recyda-powered assessment tool, consultants, and CAA's own portal already cover adjacent work. Direct willingness-to-pay and a defensible small-brand segment remain unverified, so the correct decision is Watch rather than Build.

## Inference

- Packaging EPR is a durable multi-year compliance shift rather than a news spike: Oregon already charges fees, California's permanent rules are effective, and Maine's 2027 reporting uses 2026 data.
- The best initial customer is not a tiny Etsy seller. California's sub-$1 million exemption and the cost of customer support point toward US brands roughly in the **$1 million–$25 million annual-sales range**, with 20–500 SKUs and no dedicated packaging-compliance system.
- The sharpest wedge is upstream of official portals: create a canonical packaging bill of materials, connect it to SKU and state sales, expose missing evidence, and generate a reviewable export. Automated filing or legal eligibility decisions would add disproportionate risk.
- A packaging consultant, supplier, 3PL, or e-commerce accountant may have better distribution economics than direct paid search because each intermediary can reuse the workflow across many brands.
- The category's incumbents validate the job but also compress differentiation. A self-serve product must win on transparent pricing, narrow US-state scope, easy Shopify/CSV ingestion, and a clean handoff to CAA rather than broad global compliance.
- Deterministic rules and provenance are the product. Generative AI can assist classification, but an AI-generated material category or weight cannot be treated as compliance evidence.
- Indonesia expansion is not yet relevant. Indonesia's packaging and waste policy would require a separate legal taxonomy, reporting workflow, buyer study, and payment thesis; it should not be bolted onto the US product.

## Assumptions

- At least several hundred US consumer brands above relevant exemptions still prepare packaging-EPR data in spreadsheets or through expensive consultants.
- A target brand can obtain component weights and material declarations from suppliers without weeks of bespoke services.
- Shopify, ERP, 3PL, or order-export data can allocate unit sales by state accurately enough for a preflight worksheet.
- Buyers will pay **$499–$1,200 per year** for a self-serve preflight tool even though CAA and state portals remain the official submission routes.
- The product can maintain current state mappings from public regulator and PRO guidance without relying on inaccessible private rules.
- Consultants and accountants will refer or resell a product that preserves their advisory role rather than replacing it.
- The support burden from unusual packaging, affiliated-brand structures, exemptions, and rule changes will not turn the SaaS into a consulting business.
- A disclaimer and review workflow can keep the product on the data-preparation side of legal and regulatory advice.

## Consulting assessment

### 1. Trend durability

The regulatory transition is durable and staggered. Oregon has moved into reporting and fee collection, California's permanent rules took effect in 2026, and Maine's first full reporting will use 2026 data. More state workflows are still being defined, producing an ongoing need to maintain packaging data.

Google Trends did not yield reliable numeric cards in this run, so there is no claim that consumer search interest is growing. Regulatory implementation is the stronger demand driver.

**Assessment:** durable obligation, fragmented timing, unproven search-led demand.

### 2. Customer and JTBD

- **Motion:** B2B.
- **Who pays:** a US packaged-goods brand, initially a DTC or omnichannel company with approximately $1 million–$25 million in annual sales and 20–500 SKUs.
- **Who uses:** operations manager, finance/controller, supply-chain manager, packaging lead, or fractional sustainability/compliance specialist.
- **Intermediary:** packaging consultant, packaging supplier, 3PL, e-commerce accountant, or fractional COO can distribute and assist.
- **JTBD:** “Before our next EPR report, connect every SKU to its packaging components and state sales, show what evidence is missing, and give us a defensible worksheet that our advisor can review and submit through the official portal.”

### 3. Pain evidence

The strongest verified pain is structural:

1. CAA requires sales, packaging weights, brands, and state supply.
2. Oregon requires the producer to identify covered products, determine responsibility, and choose a reporting methodology.
3. Current practitioner discussions describe packaging data disconnected from SKUs and shipment destinations.
4. Existing vendors explicitly market an end to spreadsheet, geo-allocation, and data-cleaning work.

The missing evidence is quantified pain among the intended smaller brands: hours spent, consultant spend, error rates, deadline misses, and willingness to pay for a self-serve tool.

### 4. Alternatives

1. CAA/state portal plus internal spreadsheets.
2. Packaging-supplier specification sheets and manual SKU mapping.
3. A consultant or law firm managing the filing.
4. Recyda, PCX, rePurpose Global, Packa, ForSURE, Source Intelligence, and other commercial platforms.
5. APR's Recyda-powered assessment tiers for single packages and portfolios up to 100 SKUs.
6. ERP, PLM, or sustainability software used by larger brands.

The official portals are free submission infrastructure; enterprise products and consultants provide more scope and trust. PackMap Lite would need to occupy the narrow middle.

### 5. Wedge

Start with **one CAA-compatible US packaging-data preflight for Shopify brands**:

1. Import product/SKU and state-sales CSVs.
2. Define reusable packaging assemblies and component weights.
3. Attach supplier evidence to each component.
4. Map components to one state's reporting categories with source and rule version.
5. Flag missing weights, inconsistent units, uncovered SKUs, and unexplained estimates.
6. Produce a state worksheet, exception list, methodology memo, and immutable export snapshot.
7. Hand off to a human reviewer; do not submit, calculate legal status, or claim regulator approval.

Begin with Oregon because its workflow is already operational, then add another CAA state only after paid use.

### 6. MVP

A strong solo developer could ship a narrow four-week product:

1. Passwordless workspace and Stripe Checkout.
2. Shopify product import or CSV upload.
3. Packaging-component and reusable-assembly editor.
4. Unit normalization for grams, ounces, pounds, and item counts.
5. State-sales CSV import with reconciliation to total units.
6. Versioned Oregon category mapping sourced to official guidance.
7. Deterministic completeness and consistency checks.
8. Exportable CSV/XLSX worksheet, PDF methodology memo, and missing-data request list.
9. Evidence attachments with short retention controls.
10. No generative weight estimates, portal credentials, submission bot, legal opinion, or global EPR engine.

The first paid version should support one brand, one reporting entity, one state, and up to 100 SKUs.

### 7. AI role

AI is optional and constrained:

- Suggest a likely material category from a supplier description.
- Extract candidate weights and materials from a specification PDF.
- Draft a supplier follow-up asking for missing evidence.
- Explain a validation error in plain language.

Every extracted field must retain the source excerpt and require human confirmation. Calculations, category rules, totals, and exports must be deterministic. If AI is removed, the core product still works; this is not a generic AI wrapper.

### 8. Pricing

Pricing hypothesis:

- **$499/year Starter:** one brand, one state, up to 100 SKUs, self-serve worksheet and audit packet.
- **$999/year Multi-state:** up to four states and 500 SKUs once additional rules are validated.
- **$1,500/year Partner:** ten client workspaces for a consultant, accountant, or packaging supplier.
- **$299 one-time setup service:** import and normalize the first packaging catalog, capped to a defined template.

Annual Stripe billing fits recurring reports and rule updates better than monthly billing. The setup fee tests urgency without requiring a full SaaS build.

### 9. Distribution

First repeatable path:

1. Recruit five packaging consultants, sustainable-packaging suppliers, fractional COOs, and e-commerce accountants who already serve $1 million–$25 million brands.
2. Offer a free “EPR data readiness” CSV checker that returns a missing-field count and state applicability questions without offering legal conclusions.
3. Publish source-linked Oregon and California data-field checklists and supplier-request templates.
4. Target Shopify brands with 20–500 SKUs through packaging and operations communities, not broad sustainability advertising.
5. Give partners a co-branded export and revenue share while preserving their human review role.
6. Use 2026–2027 reporting deadlines as event-based acquisition windows.

Direct SEO may produce low-intent micro-sellers below exemptions; partner qualification is likely more efficient.

### 10. Risks

- **Market:** the addressable self-serve segment may be small after exemptions and enterprise adoption.
- **Competition:** Recyda, APR, PCX, rePurpose Global, consultants, and future CAA tooling can absorb the wedge.
- **Product:** packaging taxonomies, estimates, affiliated producers, exemptions, and state rules vary.
- **Data quality:** supplier specifications may omit component weights or use inconsistent material names.
- **Legal/regulatory:** the product must avoid legal advice, guaranteed-compliance claims, and unauthorized portal submission.
- **Trust:** brands may not trust a new vendor with sales, SKU, supplier, and packaging data.
- **Technical:** versioning rules and reproducing a clean methodology trail are harder than a static calculator.
- **GTM:** the founder lacks a current packaging-buyer network; partner-led sales may be slow.
- **Services creep:** onboarding and edge cases can turn each account into a custom consulting project.
- **Pricing:** a $499 annual price may not support high-touch compliance support, while a higher price collides with established platforms.

### 11. Kill criteria

Do not build a production SaaS unless all are met by **2026-08-24**:

1. Interview fifteen US consumer brands above likely state exemptions, including at least ten with 20–500 SKUs.
2. At least eight currently use spreadsheets or consultant-created workbooks to connect packaging, SKU, and state sales.
3. At least five quantify the task at **20+ staff hours per year** or **$2,000+ annual outside spend**.
4. At least four pay a **$250 refundable deposit** for an Oregon data-readiness pilot.
5. At least three provide de-identified packaging catalogs and sales exports that fit one shared schema without bespoke code.
6. Two packaging consultants or e-commerce accountants agree to refer a pilot and review the export.
7. One qualified packaging-EPR professional verifies the initial rule mapping and disclaimer boundaries.

Kill if fewer than four deposits arrive, if most qualified brands already use Recyda/APR/consultants successfully, if supplier data is too incomplete to self-serve, or if every account requires unique legal interpretation.

### 12. Verdict

**Watch.** Regulatory durability, a concrete data-preparation workflow, current practitioner pain, and founder technical fit are all credible. The product can be a focused Stripe-backed web app, and the safest wedge sits before official filing. However, the closest incumbents already offer packaging-data management, fee analysis, and reporting; APR is explicitly moving down-market; small-brand applicability is narrowed by exemptions; and no direct paid-demand evidence exists. No `idea-grill` promotion is warranted until four deposits and a repeatable schema validate the segment.
