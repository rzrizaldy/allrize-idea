# HeatDay — OSHA Heat NEP site-day evidence desk for small US outdoor contractors — 2026-08-23

**Contributor:** Cursor
**File:** `trend-journal/2026-08-23-cursor.md`
**Market:** United States first; Indonesia relevance: not yet established (driver is US OSHA enforcement, not a tropical outdoor-labor software market verified here)
**Motion:** B2B. A small landscaping, roofing, residential construction, or tree-care owner/operator pays; a designated heat safety representative and field supervisors use it; crews acknowledge training and day-of controls; GCs and OSHA CSHOs may receive exported evidence packs but are not the payer.
**Verdict:** Watch

## Summary

HeatDay would be a Stripe-billed web app that turns OSHA’s April 2026 Heat National Emphasis Program Appendix I checklist into a recurring site-day evidence pack for small outdoor contractors: heat-index or on-site reading plus exertion note, water/shade/rest attestations, acclimatization ramps for new and returning workers, training acknowledgements, a named heat safety representative, and a one-click PDF/CSV export for an inspector or GC. The demand shift is durable for five years under CPL 03-00-024 even though a federal heat *standard* remains unfinished. The product should stay **Watch**, not **Build**: free OSHA-NIOSH Heat App and state templates cover monitoring and plan text; TailgateDocs already sells a $49 one-shot written plan; broader safety suites sell inspection forms; paper and spreadsheets still close the job for many crews. Prepaid interviews must prove willingness to pay for *recurring day-of evidence*—not another static plan—before any build.

### Regulatory and enforcement primary sources

- OSHA’s heat-exposure topic page highlights **National Emphasis Program – Outdoor and Indoor Heat-Related Hazards, Directive CPL 03-00-024 (April 10, 2026)** as a new enforcement instrument and lists outdoor industries including construction (road, roofing), landscaping, agriculture, and delivery: [osha.gov/heat-exposure](https://www.osha.gov/heat-exposure).
- The full directive PDF [CPL_03-00-024_0.pdf](https://www.osha.gov/sites/default/files/enforcement/directives/CPL_03-00-024_0.pdf) confirms significant changes including **Appendix I: Evaluation of a Heat Program** and **Appendix J: Citation Guidance**, a refreshed high-hazard industry list in Appendix A, and a five-year program life from issuance.
- Appendix I (verbatim structure in the directive) requires CSHOs to determine whether the employer’s heat program addresses eleven items, including effective communication of a written or verbal program; monitoring of ambient temperature(s) **and** work exertion; cool water access; hydration and scheduled rest breaks; shade; acclimatization for new and returning workers; administrative controls; training on six named topics; employee/supervisor understanding; and a **designated heat safety representative**.
- Appendix J directs that proposed citations for heat-related health hazards (indoor or outdoor) be issued under the **General Duty Clause** when violation elements are established, because there is no finalized federal heat standard in this directive package.
- OSHA’s same heat-exposure page states that Washington, Minnesota, California, Oregon, and Colorado (among others named in secondary summaries) have specific occupational heat laws, and points employers to the free **NIOSH/OSHA Heat App** for heat-index guidance (not WBGT).

### Credible secondary enforcement and market sources

- Quantum Compliance (Aug 21, 2026) summarizes CPL 03-00-024’s Appendix I as eleven inspector-documented questions, notes heat-priority-day inquiry practice around an expected **80°F heat index**, and maps what on-site evidence typically answers each item: [usequantum.com analysis](https://www.usequantum.com/osha-published-11-questions-about-your-heat-program-inspectors-are-required-to-document-the-answers/).
- Legal/industry alerts (e.g., [Mitchell Williams](https://www.mitchellwilliamslaw.com/outdoor/indoor-heat-related-hazards-occupational-safety-and-health-administration-announces-updated-national-emphasis-program), [NFIB](https://www.nfib.com/news/legal-blog/osha-heat-safety-rules-for-small-businesses-and-steps-to-protect-your-workers/), [Tree Care Industry Mag](https://tcimag.tcia.org/safety/osha-updates-the-heat-national-emphasis-program/)) consistently describe the NEP as active while the proposed federal heat rule remains unfinished after 2025 hearings/comments.

### Alternatives already selling or free

- **Free official tools:** OSHA-NIOSH Heat Safety Tool (iOS/Android) calculates heat index and shows precautions; DOL publishes the Android source at [USDepartmentofLabor/OSHA-Heat-Safety-Android-En](https://github.com/USDepartmentofLabor/OSHA-Heat-Safety-Android-En) (~2 stars; last push ~2024) and reusable heat-index code at [Calculate-Heat-Index-Java](https://github.com/USDepartmentofLabor/Calculate-Heat-Index-Java) (~4 stars).
- **One-shot written plan incumbent:** [TailgateDocs Heat Illness Prevention Plan](https://tailgatedocs.com/heat-illness-prevention-plan) sells a state-matched written plan for **$49 flat** (Spanish +$19), cites CA/OR/WA/NV/MD rules plus federal GDC/NEP for other states, and explicitly says the plan is a standing document—not something regenerated every summer. Claims ~1,200+ documents / 350+ contractors on the marketing page (unverified beyond the page copy).
- **Broader safety suites:** Quantum’s Q-Inspection pitch positions custom site forms, e-sign, and corrective-action workflows against Appendix I–shaped checks. Enterprise/weather vendors (e.g., Perry Weather—already cited in the Rejected school HeatLedger entry) sell monitoring + logs upmarket.
- **Manual workaround:** paper daily heat log + spreadsheet acclimatization roster + toolbox-talk sign-in sheets remain the default for many small crews.

### Relevant GitHub / build-fit evidence

- [Purv-Kabaria/ThermoShield](https://github.com/Purv-Kabaria/ThermoShield) (0 stars when collected; updated 2026-07-02) implements WBGT/work-rest scheduling, acclimatization tracking, and tamper-evident compliance logs with a FastAPI + React stack—evidence that a solo-scale technical prototype of the *logging* job is feasible, not that a market exists.
- [open-meteo/open-meteo](https://github.com/open-meteo/open-meteo) (~6.1k stars; actively maintained as of this collection) can supply location heat-index inputs for a supervisor log; it does **not** replace on-site WBGT where employers choose meters, and commercial terms must be respected.
- Founder build fit for a deterministic checklist, scheduled reminders, acknowledgement gates, and audit export is high relative to prior Hermes-style automation work in this repo’s history; differentiation and distribution—not CRUD—are the hard parts.

## Inference

- The durable driver is **five years of checklist-shaped OSHA heat enforcement** under CPL 03-00-024, reinforced by state heat standards in several jurisdictions—not today’s “extreme heat watch” search spike.
- The unpaid job is not “generate a PDF plan once.” TailgateDocs already monetizes that. The unpaid recurring job is **prove, for this site and this crew on this hot day, that Appendix I items 2–11 happened**, especially acclimatization, exertion-linked readings, and named ownership.
- A thin AI wrapper that “writes a heat plan” would be Reject-class product. A workflow that timestamps readings, crew roster state, water/shade/rest attestations, and training acknowledgements—and exports an inspector packet—could be a defendable wedge *if* owners already lose work or face GC prequal friction without it.
- Buyer motion favors card-out Stripe more than municipal procurement: small outdoor contractors buy tools for GC onboarding and inspection fear. That is better solo-SaaS shape than water-utility LCRI software (where TapWorks already sells ~$49/system/mo against free state Excel templates—a different wedge scouted and set aside today).
- Indonesia expansion is not justified: no verified Indonesian regulatory twin or WTP channel was collected.

## Assumptions

- A meaningful share of <50-employee landscaping/roofing/residential construction firms lack any digital day-of heat evidence beyond the free Heat App screenshot.
- Owners will pay ~$29–$79/mo (or ~$9–$19 per active crew-day) rather than rely on paper when a GC or OSHA visit is plausible.
- Supervisors will complete a 60–90 second mobile log under real field conditions, including Spanish-language crews.
- Heat-index (app/API) is acceptable evidence for many federal GDC/NEP contexts even though OSHA technical guidance prefers WBGT for hazard determination—employers who need WBGT meters may outgrow a software-only MVP.
- Seasonal outdoor businesses will retain through winter if the product also stores the standing plan, training matrix, and acclimatization history—not only summer day logs.

## Consulting assessment

### Trend durability

**Durable enforcement program, seasonal urgency.** CPL 03-00-024 runs five years from April 10, 2026. Heat-priority days and NWS advisories create recurring activation. The unfinished federal heat *standard* is a risk to messaging (“no rule yet”) but not to NEP/GDC enforcement. Trending “extreme heat watch” is ephemeral attention only.

### Customer and JTBD

- **Motion:** B2B.
- **Who pays:** owner of a small outdoor contractor (landscaping, roofing, residential/specialty construction, tree care) in a federal OSHA state or a state with its own heat rule.
- **Who uses:** designated heat safety representative + field supervisors; crews acknowledge.
- **JTBD:** when the heat index hits priority thresholds or an NWS advisory posts, run today’s controls and leave an Appendix I–shaped evidence pack that survives an inspector interview and a GC document request.

### Pain evidence

- Directive-mandated documentation of eleven program elements on heat inspections.
- Secondary accounts of supervisors unable to show paper for monitoring and new-hire acclimatization when officers expand ordinary inspections on heat-priority days (Quantum Aug 21, 2026 narrative).
- Marketing claims from TailgateDocs that GCs and inspectors ask for written plans—evidence of document demand, not of recurring SaaS WTP.

### Wedge

**Day-of Appendix I evidence for one outdoor crew**, starting with landscaping and roofing in hot US regions (TX, AZ, FL, CA, Southeast), bilingual EN/ES, with acclimatization and training acknowledgements as the sticky core—not plan generation.

### MVP

Stripe subscription web app (mobile-first PWA):

1. Company standing heat program fields + named heat safety representative.
2. Crew roster with hire/return dates and acclimatization day counter.
3. Site-day log: location, heat index (API or manual) + exertion note, water/shade/rest checkboxes, admin-control notes, supervisor e-sign.
4. Training topic checklist (six Appendix I topics) with worker acknowledgements.
5. Export: dated PDF packet + CSV reading history for the last 90 days.
6. Optional SMS/email alert when NWS heat advisory is active for saved ZIP codes.

No wearable hardware, no WBGT meter sales, no generic chatbot “safety coach.”

### AI role

Narrow and optional: (a) draft plain-language Spanish/English toolbox talk from the day’s log fields; (b) flag missing Appendix I attestations before export. Reject any product whose core is “AI writes your OSHA plan.”

### Pricing

Hypothesis only: **$39/mo** for one company / up to 25 workers, **$79/mo** for multi-crew; or **$15 per active heat-priority site-day** with a $29/mo minimum. Must beat the mental anchor of TailgateDocs’ $49 one-time plan by selling *recurring evidence*, not another PDF.

### Distribution

1. GC prequal and insurance-broker intros to outdoor trades.
2. SEO/content around “OSHA heat NEP Appendix I checklist” and state rule pages.
3. Partnerships with landscaping/roofing associations and bilingual safety trainers.
4. Not school athletics channels (HeatLedger Reject territory).

### Risks

- **Market:** Free Heat App + $49 plan + paper may be “good enough”; seasonal churn.
- **Product:** If buyers only want the standing plan, HeatDay collapses into TailgateDocs.
- **Technical:** Heat-index vs WBGT credibility; offline field use; multi-state rule matrix.
- **Legal:** Product must not claim to prevent citations or replace counsel; evidence packs can be discoverable.
- **GTM:** Owner-operators are hard to reach; app fatigue in the field.

### Kill criteria

Stop if any of the following hold after a short validation sprint:

1. Fewer than 8 of 15 prepaid interview targets say they would pay ≥$29/mo *after* seeing TailgateDocs and the free Heat App.
2. Two or more SMB-priced incumbents already sell the same day-of Appendix I packet with public Stripe/self-serve checkout.
3. OSHA withdraws or guts CPL 03-00-024 / Appendix I without a replacement standard that preserves the evidence job.
4. Pilot crews abandon daily logging within two heat weeks even with SMS nudges.

## Verdict

**Watch.** Durable US enforcement creates a real, checklist-shaped workflow for outdoor SMBs, and a solo developer can ship a Stripe MVP. Do not promote to `idea-grill` until interviews prove recurring WTP beyond free apps and one-shot plan generators.
